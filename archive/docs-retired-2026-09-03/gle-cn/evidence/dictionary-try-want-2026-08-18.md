# gle-cn — dictionary and grammar evidence: TRY and WANT

**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Scope:** evidence only. No Supabase writes, no TTS, no content applied.
**Governing line:** orthography = An Caighdeán Oifigiúil; grammar and lexis = Connemara (Cois Fharraige).
**Question put:** a previous ruling settled *try* as **ag iarracht** ("Tá mé ag iarracht foghlaim"). Is that grammatical? Settle from sources.

Every claim below is marked **OBSERVED** (URL + verbatim quote) or **INFERRED**. Gaps are declared as gaps.

## Headline finding

**OBSERVED.** `ag iarracht` is **not attested in any of the four dictionaries searched**, in either direction, as a phrase, a headword, a sub-entry, or an example. `iarracht` is a **feminine 3rd-declension NOUN** with no verbal-noun function; `ag` + a plain noun does not form a progressive in Irish. Every dictionary rendering of "trying to do sth." uses either the verbal noun **`ag iarraidh`** (from the verb `iarr`), or a light-verb frame around the *noun* `iarracht` — **`iarracht a dhéanamh`**, **`iarracht a thabhairt ar`** — never `ag iarracht`.

`Tá mé ag iarracht foghlaim` is therefore unsupported by any source consulted, and is doubly wrong: wrong lexical item, and the complement is bare (`foghlaim`) where every attested frame takes a fronted object + `a` + verbal noun.

The form Kai suspected is right — **`ag iarraidh`** — is attested for *both* TRY and WANT, including by de Bhaldraithe, a Cois Fharraige native speaker.

### 1g. Verdict on Q1

- Part of speech: **noun, feminine, 3rd declension** (OBSERVED, §1a/1b/1c).
- Is `ag iarracht` attested anywhere as "trying"? **No — zero hits across FGB, An Foclóir Beag, de Bhaldraithe EID and focloir.ie NEID** (OBSERVED, §1d).
- **INFERRED (high confidence):** `Tá mé ag iarracht foghlaim` is ungrammatical. `ag` + a non-verbal-noun noun yields a locative/possessive reading, not a progressive; and the complement pattern is wrong even if the head were right (attested frames front the object: `iarracht a dhéanamh rud a fhoghlaim`, not `ag iarracht foghlaim`). The prior ruling should be reversed.
- **INFERRED:** the likely origin of the error is a conflation of `ag iarraidh` (verbal noun of `iarr`) with `iarracht` (noun) — they are the same root and near-homophones in Connacht, where `iarraidh` is /ˈiəɾˠə/ (see §3c).

### 2b. Does `ag triail` read as "testing/trialling"?

**OBSERVED.** `triail¹` is explicitly *vn. of triail²* and the FGB glosses of that verbal noun are "Trial, test" — driving test, speed trial, gun tests, trial by jury. The *only* FGB example where `triail` the verb takes a verbal complement ("try to do") is **"Thriail sé éirí, he tried to get up."**

**INFERRED (high confidence):** `ag triail` in isolation reads as *testing / trialling / putting on trial*, not as generic *trying to*. The default idiom for "give it a go" is the light-verb frame **`triail a bhaint as`** ("triail a bhaint as rud, to give sth. a trial"), which the NEID lists as the primary VP for TRY senses 2, 4, 5, 8. So `triail` is right for *try this / test this / have a go*, and wrong as a general progressive "I'm trying to learn".

Also note the legal collision: `triail` is the ordinary word for a court trial (`cuireadh triail choirpigh air`), which is a real ambiguity to weigh before making it a core course item.

### 2c. What focloir.ie's TRY entry says about `triail`

**OBSERVED** — quoted in full at §1e. `triail` is the *dominant* rendering: it heads noun sense 2, and is the verb given for senses 4 (transitive), 5, 6, 8 and 10, always either as bare `triail` or as `triail a bhaint as`. Key sentences:

## 3. `ag iarraidh` — does it mean TRY as well as WANT?

**Kai's hypothesis is CONFIRMED by all four dictionaries.** This is the strongest-evidenced answer in the document.

### 3a. FGB, verb `iarr` — sense 2 is literally "Attempt"

**OBSERVED** — https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/iarr

> **iarr, v.t. (vn. ~aidh, pp. ~tha).** 1. Ask. (a) Request, demand. … (b) Seek, search for. … (c) Expect. …
> **2. Attempt. Ag ~aidh rud a dhéanamh, trying to do sth.** Ná h~ an chloch sin a thógáil, don't attempt to lift that stone. **Bhí siad ag ~aidh a bheith ag magadh fúinn, they were trying to make fun of us.** Níor ~ sé imeacht, he made no attempt to leave. **Níl mé ag ~aidh éirí go fóill, I don't want to get up yet.** Ná h~ ina dhiaidh é! He takes the biscuit!
> 3. *Lit:* Call for, require. …

Read those last two examples together. **Within a single FGB sense**, `ag iarraidh + OBJ + a + VN` is glossed once as **"trying to do sth."** and once as **"I don't want to get up yet."** That is exactly the double reading Kai described, printed in Ó Dónaill.

Note also that the "want" reading is under sense **2 (Attempt)**, not under sense 1 (Ask) — i.e. Ó Dónaill himself treats want/try here as one undivided sense.

This is the cleanest single sentence in the whole file: an Irish-language dictionary defines the sense of `iarraidh` **using the phrase `iarracht a dhéanamh`, and illustrates it with `ag iarraidh éirí`**. ## 5. `ag tabhairt faoi` as "attempting/trying"

**ATTESTED, with a specific shade: *set about / undertake*, not general *try*.**

**OBSERVED** — https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/tabhair_faoi

> **tabhair faoi, v.t. & i.** 1. Bring under. Daoine a thabhairt faoi riail dlí, to bring people under the rule of law. … **2. Attempt, undertake, set about. ~t faoi rud a dhéanamh, to set about doing sth.** Thug mé faoi thuras brónach, I undertook a sorrowful journey. **OBSERVED** — focloir.ie lists `tabhair faoi` as a PhrV rendering of transitive TRY (§1e), with the example "you've only tried two questions — **níor thug tú ach faoi dhá cheist**", and again under ATTEMPT (https://www.focloir.ie/en/dictionary/ei/attempt): "attempt … *transitive* **iarracht a dhéanamh** VP · **triail** · **triail a bhaint as** · **tabhair faoi** PhrV · **iarraidh a thabhairt ar** VP".

## 6. "I want to try to X" and "I'm going to try to X"

**Honest scope note:** no dictionary consulted contains a sentence that is *exactly* "I want to try to X" or "I'm going to try to X". What follows are the closest **OBSERVED** sentences, plus **INFERRED** compositions built only from attested frames.

### 6a. "I want to try …" — OBSERVED nearest

> **I'd like to try it out for myself — ba mhaith liom féin triail a bhaint as**
> — focloir.ie, TRY OUT sense 2, https://www.focloir.ie/en/dictionary/ei/try+out

> **it's worth a try — is fiú é a thriail, is fiú triail a bhaint as**
> **I'll have a try — bainfidh mise triail as, féachfaidh mise leis, triailfidh mise é**
> — focloir.ie, TRY, https://www.focloir.ie/en/dictionary/ei/try

> **don't be afraid to try something new — ná bíodh eagla ort rud éigin nua a thriail, ná bíodh eagla ort triail a bhaint as rud éigin nua**
> — focloir.ie, TRY sense 5

**INFERRED** (composition of `bheith ag iarraidh` + `triail a bhaint as`, both attested; both are also the NEID's own headline VPs):
- *I want to try it* → **Tá mé ag iarraidh triail a bhaint as.**
- *I want to try to learn Irish* → **Tá mé ag iarraidh iarracht a dhéanamh Gaeilge a fhoghlaim.** — **flagged as clumsy**: this stacks two attempt-words. **INFERRED (high confidence):** the Irish would ordinarily collapse it — `Tá mé ag iarraidh Gaeilge a fhoghlaim` already covers both *want* and *try* (§3), which is precisely why the double is unnatural. **This is a methodology issue for the course, not just a translation one — see §9.**

### 6b. "I'm going to try to …" — OBSERVED nearest

> **if we fail we can try again — má theipeann orainn féadfaimid é a thriail arís, má theipeann orainn tig linn iarraidh eile a thabhairt air**
> **I'll try my hardest to be there — déanfaidh mé mo mhíle dícheall a bheith ansin**
> **I'll have a try — bainfidh mise triail as, féachfaidh mise leis, triailfidh mise é**
> — focloir.ie, TRY

> **Is fearr dúinn tabhairt faoin obair sin láithreach, we had better make a start at that work immediately.**
> — FGB, tabhair faoi

> **Try and finish it to-night, féach lena chríochnú anocht.**
> — de Bhaldraithe, try²

**INFERRED (medium-high confidence):** the natural rendering of "I'm going to try to X" is a **future**, not a periphrastic *going to* — `Bainfidh mé triail as …` / `Féachfaidh mé le … a dhéanamh` / `Déanfaidh mé iarracht … a dhéanamh`. The dictionaries offer no `ag dul a thriail` pattern; the one `ag dul a` example found is idiomatic weather ("it's trying to rain — **tá sé ag dul a chur**", focloir.ie TRY, Phrases). **Explicit gap:** I found no dictionary sentence rendering English periphrastic *going to* + *try*.

**Is `ba mhaith liom` a conditional/polite form that glosses as "I would like" rather than "I want"? — YES, OBSERVED.**
- Morphologically: `ba` is the **past/conditional** form of the copula (see §8).
- Glossing: FGB renders it **"I'd like" / "he would like" / "I would like them to be there"** — never bare "I want".
- Corroboration: focloir.ie files `ar mhaith leat seacláid?` under WANT sense 4 alongside `an bhfuil tú ag iarraidh seacláide?` — i.e. NEID treats it as a *polite alternate*, not the default (§7a).

**INFERRED (high confidence), course-relevant:** for a Connemara course, **`Tá mé ag iarraidh …` is the "I want" item and `Ba mhaith liom …` is the "I'd like" item.** Teaching `ba mhaith liom` as the known-side gloss "I want" would be a register mismatch and would also burn a copula construction on a learner who has not yet met the copula.

## 8. `ba mhaith liom` morphology — is it a copula construction?

**YES — OBSERVED.** It is `ba` (past/conditional copula) + `maith` (adj., lenited to `mhaith` by the past/conditional copula) + `le` + pronoun.

### 8a. FGB's complete copula paradigm

**OBSERVED** — https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/is

> **is¹, copula.** (**Present and future:** affirmative & direct *rel.* **is**, *neg.* **ní**, *interr.* **an**, indirect *rel.* **ar, arb**, *dep.* affirmative **gur, gurb**, *dep.* & *interr.* & *rel. neg.* **nach**; **past & conditional:** affirmative **ba**, becomes **b'** before vowel (*except prons. é, í, iad, ea*) and before *fh* followed by vowel, becomes **mba** after **dá**, direct *rel.* **ba**, becomes **ab** before vowel and before *fh* followed by vowel, *neg.* **níor, níorbh**, *interr.* & indirect *rel.* **ar, arbh**, *dep.* affirmative **gur, gurbh**, *dep.* & *interr.* & *rel. neg.* **nár, nárbh**; **present subjunctive:** affirmative **gura, gurab**, *neg.* **nára, nárab**. Forms ending in *b* are used before adj. or adv. beginning with vowel; forms ending in *bh* are used before vowel or *fh* followed by vowel. **Past and conditional forms lenite**; *ní* prefixes *h* to prons. *é, í, iad, ea*, and to adjectives and certain nouns beginning with a vowel.)

Two things fall straight out of that: **`ba` is the past & conditional form** (confirming §7e), and **past/conditional forms lenite** — which is why `maith` → `mhaith`.

FGB also gives, in the same entry: **"(Anticipatory) Ní hé nár mhaith liom é, it is not that I wouldn't like it."** — an OBSERVED attestation of the `nár mhaith liom` negative in a real sentence.

### 8b. An Caighdeán Oifigiúil, chapter 7 (The Copula)

**OBSERVED** — https://irishlanguage.ie/an-caighdean-oifigiuil-the-copula/ (fetched via a summarising fetch; raw HTML returned HTTP 403 to direct request, so quoting is limited to what the fetch returned verbatim)

> "*ba* is placed before a consonant or *fh* + consonant in every instance, e.g. *Ba mhúinteoir lách é; Ba fhreagra cruinn é.*"
> "*ba* is placed before a vowel or *fh* + vowel in the following instances: (i) the pronouns *é, í, ea, éard, eisean, ise, iad* and *iadsan*, e.g. *Ba iad a bhuaigh an corn*"
> "*b'* is placed before a vowel or *fh* + vowel in the following instances: (i) nouns, e.g. *B'innealtóir maith í*"
> "*níor* precedes consonants; *níorbh* precedes vowels."
> "*ar* precedes consonants; *arbh* precedes vowels (interrogative affirmative)."
> "*nár* precedes consonants; *nárbh* precedes vowels (interrogative negative)."
> "Lenition follows forms of the copula in the past tense, the imperfect tense and the conditional mood, e.g. *Ba dheas liom an seans a bheith faighte againn*"

**Gap declared:** irishlanguage.ie returns **HTTP 403** to a direct request, so the above is what a summarising fetch returned as verbatim quotation. The paradigm is independently confirmed by FGB (§8a), which I fetched in full.

### 8c. The `ba mhaith le` paradigm

The person inflection is on the **preposition `le`**, not on the copula. Prepositional pronouns of `le`: liom, leat, leis, léi, linn, libh, leo.

| | Form | Status |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative 1sg | **Ba mhaith liom** | **OBSERVED** — FGB: "Ba mhaith liom braon tae (a fháil), I'd like (to get) a drop of tea." |
| affirmative 2sg | Ba mhaith leat | **INFERRED** from paradigm |
| affirmative 3sg m | **Ba mhaith leis** | **OBSERVED** — FGB: "Ba mhaith leis labhairt leat, he would like to speak to you." |
| affirmative 3sg f | **Ba mhaith léi** | **OBSERVED** — FGB: "Is ann ba mhaith léi a hadhlacadh, it was there she wished to be buried." |
| affirmative 1pl | Ba mhaith linn | **INFERRED** |
| affirmative 2pl | Ba mhaith libh | **INFERRED** |
| affirmative 3pl | **Ba mhaith leo** | **OBSERVED** — focloir.ie WANT 4: "they wanted the house to be built there — **ba mhaith leo go dtógfaí an teach ansin**" |
| **negative** | **Níor mhaith liom / leat / leis …** | **OBSERVED** — de Bhaldraithe WANT: "I don't want that known, **níor mhaith liom** go mbeadh a fhios sin ag aon duine." FGB `is¹`: "Ní hé **nár mhaith liom** é, it is not that I wouldn't like it." |
| **interrogative** | **Ar mhaith leat …?** | **OBSERVED** — focloir.ie WANT 4: "do you want a chocolate? — **ar mhaith leat seacláid?**"; and "where do you want me to start? — cén áit **ar mhaith leat** go dtosóinn?" |
| **negative interrogative** | Nár mhaith leat …? | **INFERRED** from FGB paradigm (*neg. interr.* `nár, nárbh`); FGB gives the pattern with a different adjective: "Ba bhreá an bhean í, **nár bhreá**?" |
| **dependent affirmative** | gur mhaith leis … | **INFERRED** from FGB paradigm (*dep. affirmative* `gur, gurbh`) |
| **dependent negative** | nár mhaith leis … | **INFERRED**; FGB `is¹` gives "Ní hé nár mhaith liom é" |
| **after `dá`** | dá mba mhaith leis … | **INFERRED** from FGB: "becomes **mba** after **dá**"; FGB attests the shape at "**Dá mba mise thú**, if I were you." |
| **before a vowel** | b'fhearr liom (suppletive comparative) | **OBSERVED** — de Bhaldraithe: "**b'fhearr liom** nach mbeadh a fhios sin"; FGB: "**B'fhearr liom** ná rud maith (go), I dearly wish (that)." |

**Course-relevant note, INFERRED (high confidence):** this is an irregular copula paradigm with **three** distinct particles (`ba` / `níor` / `ar`), lenition, and person marked on a *separate* prepositional pronoun. That is a substantial grammatical load. `Tá mé ag iarraidh` — negated `Níl mé ag iarraidh`, questioned `An bhfuil tú ag iarraidh?` — reuses the `tá/níl/an bhfuil` machinery a learner already has.

## 9. What this means for the course

**All INFERRED**, but each inference is anchored to the OBSERVED material above.

1. **Reverse the `ag iarracht` ruling.** It has zero attestation in four dictionaries and is contradicted by the grammar database's own part-of-speech data for `iarracht`. `Tá mé ag iarracht foghlaim` should not ship.

2. **`ag iarraidh` is the Connemara answer for both TRY and WANT.** It is the NEID's headline VP for *want*, FGB's sense-2 gloss for *attempt/try*, An Foclóir Beag's own definition of the attempt sense, and the form de Bhaldraithe reaches for on both sides.

3. **⚠️ This creates a ZUT problem and it needs a ruling.** The rail is: one known prompt → exactly one target form. But `Tá mé ag iarraidh é a dhéanamh` is the target form for **two different known prompts** — "I want to do it" *and* "I'm trying to do it". That is the reverse direction from a ZUT breach (ZUT rejects one known → two targets), so it is not a breach as stated. It is nonetheless a real learner problem: a learner drilled on both prompts will produce the same Irish and cannot tell which they were being asked for. **Options, for Kai to rule on:**
   - (a) Teach `ag iarraidh` for **want** only, and use a distinct item for **try** — `triail a bhaint as` (concrete: *give it a go*) or `iarracht a dhéanamh` (abstract effort) or `féach le` (attempt to).
   - (b) Teach `ag iarraidh` for **try** only, and use `Ba mhaith liom` for want — **not recommended**: it makes the "I want" item a conditional copula glossing as "I'd like", which is a register mismatch and a heavy grammar load early (§8).
   - (c) Teach the overlap explicitly as a feature of the dialect. Methodologically expensive at beginner level.
   - **My reading:** (a). `Tá mé ag iarraidh …` = *I want to …*; `Tá mé ag iarraidh triail a bhaint as` / `Rinne mé iarracht …` for *try*. This keeps the known side clean and matches how the NEID splits its own headwords (WANT → `bheith ag iarraidh`; TRY → `triail` / `iarracht a dhéanamh`).

4. **Do not use `ag breathnú le`** — unattested (§4b).

5. **`tabhair faoi` carries an attack sense** (FGB `tabhair faoi` 3, `iarraidh a thabhairt ar dhuine` "to attempt to attack s.o."). Fine for *tackle a task*, wrong for a beginner's *I'm trying*.

6. **Separate the WANT items:** `Tá mé ag iarraidh` = *I want*; `Ba mhaith liom` = *I'd like*; `Teastaíonn … uaim` = *I need / … is wanted by me*. Glossing `ba mhaith liom` as "I want" on the known side is not supported by FGB's own glosses (§7d).
