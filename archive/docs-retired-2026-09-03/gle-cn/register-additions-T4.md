## 3. One structural addition — word order under the A1 "going to" future

**`ag goil a` + lenited verbal noun takes its object AFTER the verbal noun. Do not front it.**

The course's default for a verbal noun with a direct object is object-fronting
(`Gaeilge a labhairt`, S1). That fronting is **incompatible** with the A1 future frame: the `a` of
`ag goil a` already occupies the particle slot, so `*tá sé ag goil rud eicínt a inseacht` has two
`a`-particles competing and is not a sentence. I have therefore written, consistently:

- S227 · *That man is going to tell me something new* → **Tá an fear sin ag goil a inseacht dom rud eicínt nua**
- S243 · *I'm going to ask for the same thing to eat* → **Tá mé ag goil a iarraidh an rud céanna le n-ithe**
- S236 · *… she was going to try to help* → **… go raibh sí ag goil a dhéanamh iarracht cabhrú**

**Confidence: best attempt**, and S236 **genuinely uncertain** — it is the one where the
post-posed object is heaviest. A1 is a ruling about the *frame*; it says nothing about the object,
so somebody had to decide, and this is my decision recorded rather than made silently. If the
coordinator prefers a rewrite (`go ndéanfadh sí iarracht cabhrú`, "that she would try to help"),
that is a curriculum call, not a grammar one — but it abandons the "going to" the English carries.

## 4. Notes on the two calls I am least happy with

1. **"make sure that" → `a bheith cinnte go`.** This is literally *to be sure that*, not *to make
   sure that*. I chose it because every honest rendering of *make* here (`cinnte a dhéanamh de go`)
   is clumsy, and because it rides `cinnte`, which the course already fixed at S10. It shifts the
   English very slightly. Flagged rather than smoothed.
2. **"have a good time" → `am maith a bheith agat`.** A calque on the English. The idiomatic
   Connemara is likelier to be `bhain tú sult/taitneamh as …`, but `baint taitnimh as` is **spent**
   by register #27 (*enjoy*), and reusing it here would merge two distinct English prompts and break
   ZUT. I took the calque over the merge. **This is my number-one question for a speaker.**
