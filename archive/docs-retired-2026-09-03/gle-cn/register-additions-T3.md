> ### T3.1 — the one lexical call I want a second opinion on
> `bialann` is **0** in Ó Curnáin. The English loanword **`restaurant` is attested** — vol III cites
> its phonetic form in the loanword phonology, and vol IV's glossary lists it as a headword
> (`restaurant, n, 107`). That is the *same class of evidence* the register used to rule
> **`an pub`** over `teach ósta` (#169).
>
> I did **not** follow the `pub` precedent, for one reason: `pub` scores **106 in running speech**,
> whereas `restaurant`'s two hits are both **metalinguistic** — a phonology example and a glossary
> index entry — not a Connemara speaker using the word in a sentence. That is much weaker, and
> writing `restaurant` into a learner's Irish sentence on that basis felt like over-reading it.
> **If Kai or a speaker says Connemara just says *restaurant*, S156 is a one-row flip and costs
> nothing** (no audio exists). Flagging rather than smoothing.

## D. Structures the register did not rule on

### T3.18 — "I want you to …" / "do you want me to …?" / "I'd like you to …"
`tá mé ag iarraidh go` + **conditional**, extending register #71 and the live S15 (`go labhrófá`).

- *what do you want me to do?* → **Céard atá tú ag iarraidh go ndéanfainn?**
- *do you want me to help you look for it?* → **An bhfuil tú ag iarraidh go gcabhróinn leat é a chuartú?**
- *I'd like you to tell me what you need* → **Ba mhaith liom go n-inseofá dom céard atá ag teastáil uait.**

**Confidence: best attempt.** The frame is S15's; only the person of the conditional is new.

### T3.19 — the relative progressive, "what I'm saying" / "what you're looking for"
`an rud atá + SUBJ + a + verbal noun`. Register #209 already has the interrogative
(`Céard atá tú a chuartú?`); this is the same shape under `an rud`.

- *That isn't what I'm trying to say* → **Ní hé sin an rud atá mé ag iarraidh a rá.**
- *can you understand what I'm saying?* → **An bhfuil tú in ann an rud atá mé a rá a thuiscint?**

**Confidence: confident** on the shape; **best attempt** on S174, which stacks two fronted verbal
nouns and is heavy. See report.

### T3.20 — the conditional perfect (register §20 explicitly left this open)
`bheadh sé + past participle + agam` — an analytic perfect, **not** `dá mbeinn tar éis`.

- *I would have done it differently if I had known what you wanted* →
  **Bheadh sé déanta agam ar bhealach difriúil dá mbeadh a fhios agam céard a bhí tú ag iarraidh.**
- *I wouldn't have said it in exactly the same way* →
  **Ní bheadh sé ráite agam ar an gcaoi chéanna go díreach.**

**Confidence: genuinely uncertain**, exactly as §20 anticipated. Both are S152/S153, which are mine.
The `dá mbeadh a fhios agam` protasis is solid (register #85 + #8); the apodosis is the guess.

### T3.21 — "to take [a person] to [a place]" is `tabhairt chuig`, NOT `tógáil`
⚠️ **This is a ZUT wrinkle the register does not cover and the coordinator should look at.**

Register #200 fixes *to take* = `tógáil` (S27, `an iomarca ama a thógáil`). But S181 is
*"I have to take my mother to the doctor"*, and **`mo mháthair a thógáil chuig an dochtúir` is not
Irish** — conveying a person somewhere is `tabhairt`. I wrote
**`Ach caithfidh mé mo mháthair a thabhairt chuig an dochtúir.`**

So English *take* now has two Irish forms in the course, split by sense (time/amount = `tógáil`;
person-to-a-place = `tabhairt`). **Each English string still has exactly one Irish form, so ZUT is
technically intact**, but the split is invisible to any automated check and needs recording.
`tabhairt` is also already spent on *to give* (#199). **Confidence: confident on the Irish, flagged
on the collision.**
