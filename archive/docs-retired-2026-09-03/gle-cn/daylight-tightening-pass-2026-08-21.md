# Daylight tightening pass — the parallel-build seams, seeds 124 and 127

**Logged 2026-08-21 by the 111–131 band coordinator, on Tom's instruction. NOT to be rebuilt at
night.** Both items are **structural consequences of building bands in parallel, not worker errors.**
Worker #612 built each correctly under the rules as they stood, flagged both honestly rather than
burying them, and named them as the weakest English in its band. The form that was missing in each
case **now exists**, which is the only reason a tightening is possible at all.

Nothing here is broken. It is all grammatical and all passes the gates. It is a **naturalness**
pass, and it is small.

---

## Item 1 — seed 124: past matrix verb, present complement (6 lines, not 4)

**What happened.** Seed 124 teaches *"I thought"* → `bhí mé ag cheapadh`. The only complement clause
taught anywhere in the course at that point was the **present** `go bhfuil sé go maith`
(*"that it's a good thing"*, seed 47). The past `go raibh` did not enter the course until **seed
125 — one seed later**. So every practice line pairing the two reads *"I thought that it's…"*.

#612's report says four lines. **The live count is six.** Listed in full so the pass is surgical:

| English | Irish |
|---|---|
| I thought that it's a good thing | `bhí mé ag cheapadh go bhfuil sé go maith` |
| I thought that it's a good thing to learn | `bhí mé ag cheapadh go bhfuil sé go maith foghlaim` |
| I thought that it's a good thing to make mistakes | `bhí mé ag cheapadh go bhfuil sé go maith botúin a dhéanamh` |
| I thought that it's a good thing to relax | `bhí mé ag cheapadh go bhfuil sé go maith scíth a ligean` |
| I thought that it's a good thing to speak Irish | `bhí mé ag cheapadh go bhfuil sé go maith Gaeilge a labhairt` |
| I thought that it's a good thing to come back later on | `bhí mé ag cheapadh go bhfuil sé go maith teacht ar ais ar ball` |

**Note the Irish carries the same seam as the English** — `bhí … go bhfuil`. Whoever takes this
should tighten both sides together or neither; fixing only the English would leave a worse mismatch
than the one it started with.

**Defensible as it stands** for a belief still held. Tighten only if the pass agrees it reads oddly
in sequence — six near-identical lines in one seed make the seam more visible than any one line
would.

**What is now available:** `go raibh` entered at seed 125 (`125.2 "that your idea was"` →
`go raibh do smaoineamh`). **Check it tiles before rewriting** — the chunk taught is
`go raibh do smaoineamh`, not a bare `go raibh`, and the tiler works from whole taught chunks.
That check is the first job of this pass, not an afterthought.

## Item 2 — seed 127: elliptical English (3 lines)

**What happened.** Seed 127 teaches *"that isn't why"* → `ní hé sin an fáth`. The only full
complement clause available at that seed was `nach raibh a fhios aige`, so two lines use it and the
rest lean on ellipsis:

| English | Irish |
|---|---|
| that isn't why, but I'm not sure | `ní hé sin an fáth, ach níl mé cinnte` |
| that isn't why, and I'd like to relax | `ní hé sin an fáth, agus ba mhaith liom scíth a ligean` |
| that isn't why, but I don't want to stop | `ní hé sin an fáth, ach níl mé ag iarraidh stopadh` |

**Grammatical in reply context** — this is how people actually answer — but #612 calls it the least
natural English in its band and I agree with that self-assessment.

**The open question for a speaker,** already on the Connemara list: does bare `Ní hé sin an fáth`
stand on its own, or does it always want a clause after it? **If it stands, these three lines are
fine as they are and this item closes with no edit.** Ask before rewriting.

## The general lesson, which outlives both items

**Parallel bands cannot see each other's vocabulary.** Every worker on this build was told —
correctly — to write only from what is taught *before* its own seeds, so a band builds against a
palette frozen at its start and cannot use a form a neighbour lands an hour later. Worker #613
reported the same effect from the other side: its seeds 128–131 lean harder on older vocabulary
than they would have, because 115–127 were still unbuilt while it worked.

That is the correct conservative behaviour and it is what stops a band from depending on a
neighbour that dies. **The cost is paid at the seams, in naturalness, and it is cheap to collect
afterwards** — which is what this pass is. It is not a reason to serialise the build.
