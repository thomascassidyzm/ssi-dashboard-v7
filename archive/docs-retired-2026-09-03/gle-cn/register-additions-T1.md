### Notes on the three I am least happy with

**T1-3 `feabhsú` conflicts with register #228.** The register maps *to improve* to
`feabhas a chur ar`, which is **transitive and needs an object**. Seed 44 is *"or if I need to
improve"* — intransitive, no object. `feabhas a chur ar` cannot be written there without inventing an
object the English does not have. I have used `feabhsú` and filed it as a **second** entry rather
than overwriting #228, because the two English strings genuinely differ (*improve X* vs *improve*).
**This is an objection to the register filed under its own rule 3, not a silent divergence.**

**T1-6 `spraíúil`.** Seed 64 is *"learning Irish isn't easy but it is fun"*. I did not probe ÓC for
`spraoi`/`spraíúil` and I am not going to claim a count I did not take. The live spoken candidate is
`craic` (*ach tá craic ann*), which I rejected only because it reads as slang inside a beginner
course — that is a judgement, not evidence. **A speaker should be asked this one directly.**

**T1-7 `indéanta`.** Register §5 forbids introducing `féidir` as a second ability verb for *could
you*. *"It wasn't possible"* is impersonal and has no subject to be able, so `in ann` will not carry
it, and `níorbh fhéidir é` would put `féidir` back in play. `indéanta` avoids both but is bookish.

## 2. One structural ruling I had to make

### T1-S1 — the governing preposition is **dropped** before an indirect question in `cén chaoi a`

Four of my seeds have a verb that demands a preposition (`smaoineamh **ar**`, `cuimhneamh **ar**`,
`iontas orm **faoi**`) followed by an English *how* clause. Stacking them gives `ar cén chaoi a …`,
which is not how Irish introduces an indirect question. I have consistently dropped the preposition:

- 43 · *I wasn't thinking about how to answer* → **Ní raibh mé ag smaoineamh cén chaoi a dtabharfainn freagra.**
- 56 · *so I can remember how to say a few words* → **Le go mbeidh mé in ann cuimhneamh cén chaoi a ndéarfaidh mé cúpla focal.**
- 57 · *I can't remember how to say what I wanted to say* → **Níl mé in ann cuimhneamh cén chaoi a ndéarfaidh mé an rud a bhí mé ag iarraidh a rá.**
- 77 · *I'm surprised at how quickly I'm starting to understand* → **Tá iontas orm chomh tapa is atá mé ag tosú ag tuiscint.**

**This is not an A2 violation.** A2 forbids *splitting a verb-demanded preposition off as its own
tile and inventing a gloss for it*. Here the preposition is not glossed, tiled or re-used — it is
simply absent from the surface, exactly as it is absent in `Níl a fhios agam cén chaoi …`. Whoever
decomposes 43/56/57 must therefore **not** expect an `ar` to tile against.

**confidence: best attempt.** Flagged for a speaker.

## 3. The one that most needs a coordinator decision tonight — `ag goil a` vs `ag dul a`

Addendum **A1** rules *"I am going to"* = **`ag goil a` / `ag dul a` + lenited verbal noun** and
offers **both**. That is two forms for one English, which is precisely what ZUT forbids, and four
workers are translating in parallel right now.

Seed 82 is the only *going to* in my band and I wrote:

> 82 · *I'm not going to wait for you. Why not?* → **Níl mé ag goil a fhanacht leat. Cén fáth nach bhfuil?**

as well. **If any other band wrote `ag dul a`, the course now has both.** 22 of the 668 English seeds
carry *going to*, so this is a 22-seed sweep, not a one-off. There is no audio, so the sweep is free.
