## What a Connemara speaker should be shown first — in this order

1. **Seed 132** — *that's less exciting than what she was saying* → `Níl sé sin chomh corraitheach
   leis an rud a bhí sí a rá`. **`corraitheach` is 0 in all four volumes of Ó Curnáin** (controls
   passed, so that zero is real). It is FGB's word for *exciting* and nothing contradicts it, but it
   is the single weakest word I wrote. Also worth asking: Irish has no adjectival *less … than*, so I
   used the *not as … as* frame. Would they?
2. **Seed 122** — *it's starting to feel easier and I'm excited about how it's going* → `Tá sé ag
   tosú ag mothú níos éasca agus tá mé ar bís faoin gcaoi a bhfuil sé ag dul`. Two problems in one
   sentence. `ar bís` is 1 in the corpus (I took it over `sceitimíní`, which is 0). And *"it's
   starting to feel easier"* puts the register's `ag tosú ag mothú` pattern on an inanimate subject,
   where it reads like English wearing Irish clothes. **Ask: how would you say "it's getting
   easier"?**
3. **Seeds 114 and 115** — *I feel as if …* → `tá mé ag mothú amhail is go …`. These are the
   register's own #43, which its author labelled genuinely uncertain and called a calque of the
   English. I used it because the register is binding. Two seeds ride on it, so one answer fixes both.
4. **Seed 133** — *you get to know someone very well* → `Cuireann tú aithne an-mhaith ar dhuine`.
   `aithne` itself is 144 in the corpus, but the collocation `aithne a chur ar` is 0. FGB gives it as
   its own frame, so I believe it, but I would like it confirmed.
5. **Seed 104** — *we need to change what we're doing* → `Is gá dúinn an rud atá muid a dhéanamh a
   athrú`. Grammatically I stand over it, but it stacks two `a`-particles and I suspect a speaker
   would restructure it.
6. **Seed 126** — *this work is changing the shape of my brain* → `Tá an obair seo ag athrú cruth
   m'inchinne`. I left `cruth` in the nominative after a progressive verbal noun, which is what
   Connemara usually does in speech; a purist would want `chrutha`.
7. **Seed 92** — *I'd like to keep on doing this for a while* → `Ba mhaith liom coinneáil orm ag
   déanamh an ruda seo go ceann tamaill`. Long. `go ceann tamaill` is the register's #123 and is 0 in
   the corpus (`tamall` alone is 15).

## Objections I filed but obeyed

Per §6 rule 3, I used the register's choice and am recording the disagreement rather than diverging.

- **`mar sin` now does two jobs.** The register fixes it for *so (= therefore)*. Seed 102 needs
  *"it's not like that"*, whose natural Irish is also `mar sin`. ZUT is not broken — each English
  string still has exactly one Irish — but one Irish shape now answers two English prompts.
- **Seed 106 drops the English *just*.** *"we just need to work hard"* → `is gá dúinn obair go crua`.
  The faithful rendering (`níl le déanamh againn ach obair go crua`) would have abandoned the
  `is gá dúinn` frame in the very sentence that had just used it for *"we don't need to"*. **I chose
  ZUT with seed 104 over the discourse particle.** One-line change if the coordinator disagrees.
- **Seeds 122 and 132 use unrelated words for *excited* and *exciting*.** If a speaker rejects
  `corraitheach`, the paired repair moves both onto `ar bís`, at the cost of rewording seed 132's
  English reading. That is a coordinator's call.

## Three things that surprised me

1. **`scripts/gle-cn/q.cjs` ignores its argument.** The query string handed to every translation
   worker in the dispatch brief does nothing — the script is a fixed course-status probe and prints
   seeds 1 and 2 no matter what you pass it. It is easy not to notice, because it *does* print seed
   rows. I wrote my own reader in `scripts/gle-cn/w-T2/` (gitignored) rather than trusting it. **Any
   other worker who followed the brief literally may have been looking at the wrong seeds.**
2. **The native Irish translation memory does not reach my band.** `.a108-gle/base-tm.json` is
   documented as exact-matching about two thirds of the 668 shared English seeds. Across seeds
   90–142 it matched **1 of 53** — and that one (seed 91) offered `chun freagra a thabhairt`, a
   `chun` construction the addendum's A1 rules against. I used the register's version instead.
3. **`sceitimíní` is 0 in Ó Curnáin and `ar bís` is 1.** I expected the opposite. It changed seed 122
   after I had already written it once.
