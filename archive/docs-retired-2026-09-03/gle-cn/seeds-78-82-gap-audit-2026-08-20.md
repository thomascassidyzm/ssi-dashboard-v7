# Seeds 78–82 were not a gap — they were already decomposed. What the audit found instead.

**Course:** gle_cn_for_eng (Connemara Irish) · **Band:** seeds 78, 79, 80, 81, 82 · **Date:** 2026-08-20

## The premise was stale

The brief said these five fell in a gap left by a coordinator that died mid-run. They had not.
All five were decomposed and banked at **22:46–22:49 UTC on 20 August**, roughly forty minutes
before this audit opened. Every one is structurally complete:

| Seed | English | Teaching units | Phrases |
|------|---------|----------------|---------|
| 78 | I don't understand what you said | 2 | 16 |
| 79 | when did you start to learn? | 2 | 16 |
| 80 | I'm not sure when I'll be ready | 2 | 16 |
| 81 | when do you want to start? | 1 | 8 |
| 82 | I'm not going to wait for you. Why not? | 3 | 24 |

Ten teaching units, eighty practice phrases, an exact 3 BUILD / 5 USE set behind every unit.
The five-point checkpoint passes clean: no word used before it was introduced, no bare frames,
dialect forms consistent, **zero audio rows**. Nothing was rebuilt and nothing was overwritten —
re-decomposing them would have clobbered another worker's landed night.

## The real finding: the course teaches two contradictory ways to say "going to"

This is the thing worth a later sweep, and it is not confined to this band.

The ruling is that *"I am going to"* is **not** `tá mé chun` — no attestation — and that the
form to use is `ag goil a` plus a softened verbal noun. Seed 82 obeys it exactly: it teaches
`níl mé ag goil a` and builds eleven phrases on it.

But `chun` is already **baked into the course spine**. It is a taught teaching unit twice over —
`tá mé chun` at **seed 5** and `an bhfuil tú chun` at **seed 25** — and it carries **83 practice
phrases** across seeds 5 through 81, plus five seed sentences of its own (5, 8, 12, 23, 25).

So as the course now stands, a learner meets `tá mé chun` for "I'm going to" at seed 5, drills it
for seventy-odd seeds, and then at seed 82 is taught a different construction for the same English.

Three of those 83 instances are inside this band and they are **annotated but deliberately left
alone**:

- seed 79 — *when are you going to learn?* → `cén uair a bhfuil tú chun foghlaim?`
- seed 79 — *when are you going to come back?* → `cén uair a bhfuil tú chun teacht ar ais?`
- seed 81 — *are you going to start?* → `an bhfuil tú chun tosú?`

They are left alone because at seeds 79 and 81 they are **correct given what the learner holds**.
`ag goil a` is not taught until seed 82. Rewriting these three to `ag goil a` would hand the
learner a sentence they could not possibly produce — the governing test fails, and the
decomposition would become the wrong one, not the right one.

**The defect is upstream, at seeds 5 and 25, and it is a course-wide decision.** Fixing it means
re-decomposing the spine and migrating every learner's progress under the standing content-change
protocol. That is emphatically not a five-seed patch, which is why this band did not attempt one.

## The "try" count: one instance, and it follows the house majority

Exactly **one** phrase in these five seeds glosses "try":

> seed 78 — *I'm trying to learn but I don't understand* → `tá mé ag iarraidh foghlaim ach ní thuigim`

It uses `ag iarraidh`. Since the word for "try" is still open, the census matters more than the
single instance: course-wide, **167 phrases** gloss try/tries/tried, split
**139 `ag iarraidh` / 23 `iarracht a dhéanamh` / 5 other**. So `ag iarraidh` is what the course
overwhelmingly already uses, and this band matches it rather than inventing a third answer.

Worth the higher-tier job knowing: `ag iarraidh` is doing double duty as both *want* and *try*,
and both readings are live in this band — seed 81 drills `ag iarraidh` as **want** eight times
while seed 78 uses it as **try**. Whatever is decided, the decision has 139 phrases behind it.

## Two smaller notes

**"To wait" is taught four separate times.** Seed 82's unit is the *lenited* `fhanacht leat`,
correct in its own sentence because `ag goil a` lenites what follows. Nothing is stranded — seeds
155, 269 and 276 later teach `fanacht cúpla nóiméad`, `fanacht le` and bare `fanacht` as fresh
units — but the course does end up teaching one verb four times because the first teaching was
locked to a mutated environment. Not a defect to fix here; a pattern to watch when a unit's first
appearance is a mutated one.

**Two English prompts read stiffly** and deserve a human ear, since no tool judges naturalness:
*"when are you learning his name?"* and *"when did you start to learn his name?"* (both seed 79).
The Irish behind them is fine; the English is what an English speaker would rarely say.

## What was not done

No content was written or altered. No teaching unit, phrase, or seed row was touched. **No TTS was
generated — the course holds zero audio rows, before and after.**
