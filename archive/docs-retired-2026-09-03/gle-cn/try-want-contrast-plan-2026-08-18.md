# `ag iarraidh` / `ag iarracht` — the delay, and the drilling that has to follow it

Binding design for `gle_cn_for_eng`, from Kai's two rulings of 2026-08-18:

> "no, not iarracht a dheanamh, just **ag iarracht**"

> "The separation should help ag iarraidh solidify before we add a similar-sounding word in
> there. Then once the new one comes in, we drill them a lot. Drill trying, drill wanting,
> drill them together in the same phrases. Make sure both get used a lot in later seeds."

The delay is **not** avoidance. It is stage one of a two-stage teaching design, and stage
two is mandatory. This file exists because the seeds that carry stage two are not built
yet, and the plan must not be lost between runs.

---

## The pair, and why it is sharp

| | |
|---|---|
| want | `ag iarraidh` |
| trying | `ag iarracht` |

Both are `ag` + noun. They differ by one syllable. **Time separation is the only thing
protecting predictability** — there is no structural cue to lean on, which is exactly why
`ag triail` was reachable for and exactly why reaching for it again is not allowed.

The sharpest confusable is a genuine minimal pair the course will produce on its own:

```
Níl mé ag iarraidh stopadh     I don't want to stop      (taught: seed 15)
Níl mé ag iarracht stopadh     I'm not trying to stop    (arrives: seed 50)
```

That pair must be drilled **deliberately and side by side**, never met by accident.

---

## Stage 1 — seeds 1–45: `ag iarraidh` alone, until it is solid

`ag iarracht` appears nowhere before seed 46. **Currently 0 occurrences course-wide.**

**Measured in the banked seeds 1–20:** `ag iarraidh` carries **189 rows** — 8 seed
sentences, 6 legos, 175 practice phrases — and touches **19 of the 20 seeds**. It is
already taught in six person-forms:

| seed | lego |
|---|---|
| 1.1 | I want → `tá mé ag iarraidh` |
| 12.1 | he wants → `tá sé ag iarraidh` |
| 13.1 | she wants → `tá sí ag iarraidh` |
| 14.1 | we want → `tá muid ag iarraidh` |
| 15.2 | I don't want → `níl mé ag iarraidh` |
| 16.1 | you want → `tá tú ag iarraidh` |

**Requirement on the unbuilt stretch 21–45:** `ag iarraidh` must appear in **at least 20 of
those 25 seeds**. If it falls below that, the gap has not done its job and the introduction
moves out further rather than the drilling being weakened.

---

## Stage 2 — seed 46 onward: introduce, then contrast-drill hard

### Where "try" lands, and the separation it gives

**"try" debuts at seed 46. "want" debuts at seed 1. Separation: 45 seeds.**

It sits four seeds before the English corpus's own next demand for the concept (seed 50,
`I'm not trying to finish as quickly as possible`), so the concept is taught immediately
before it is needed rather than 48 seeds early, which is what the original ordering did.

### The introduction block

| seed | known | status |
|---|---|---|
| 46 | I'm trying to learn | `Tá mé ag iarracht foghlaim` |
| 47 | I'm trying to remember a word | `Tá mé ag iarracht cuimhneamh ar fhocal` |
| 48 | I want to try as hard as I can today | **BLOCKED** — see §"What is blocked" |
| 49 | I'm going to try to explain what I mean | **BLOCKED** |
| 50 | I'm not trying to finish as quickly as possible | untranslated; **this is the minimal-pair seed** |

**Gloss lock.** The lego is `trying → ag iarracht`, glossed with the `-ing` and never as a
bare "to try" while the progressive is the only sanctioned form. Against seed 1's
`want → ag iarraidh` that gives one known prompt per target in both directions, so ZUT
holds and the learner has a choosable cue. This is the "unambiguous gloss" half of the
ruling; the 45-seed gap is the other half.

### Drilling quota for seeds 46–70 — a requirement, not a hope

1. **Every seed from 46 to 70 carries at least 2 USE phrases containing `ag iarracht`.**
2. **`ag iarraidh` is re-drilled at no less than its stage-1 rate across the same stretch** —
   it is not finished merely because it was taught at seed 1. Kai: *"drill wanting."*
3. **At least 12 USE phrases across 46–70 carry BOTH forms in the same phrase.** This is the
   phrase shape that makes the pair learnable; isolation does not.
4. **The minimal pair `Níl mé ag iarraidh …` / `Níl mé ag iarracht …` is drilled explicitly
   at seed 50**, as a pair, in adjacent phrases.
5. Neither form may be introduced and dropped. Checkpoints at seed 100 and seed 200: both
   forms must still be appearing.

### Same-phrase pairings — all six buildable from seeds 1–20 vocabulary alone

These are not sketches. Every lego in them is already banked, so they can be written the
day seed 46 lands.

| known | target |
|---|---|
| I want to speak Irish but I'm trying to remember the whole sentence | `Tá mé ag iarraidh Gaeilge a labhairt ach tá mé ag iarracht cuimhneamh ar an abairt ar fad` |
| I'm not trying to stop, I want to speak more | `Níl mé ag iarracht stopadh, tá mé ag iarraidh labhairt níos mó` |
| she wants to find out what the answer is and she's trying to remember | `Tá sí ag iarraidh fáil amach céard é an freagra agus tá sí ag iarracht cuimhneamh` |
| he's trying to say what he wants | `Tá sé ag iarracht a rá céard atá sé ag iarraidh` |
| you want to come back but you're trying to finish quickly | `Tá tú ag iarraidh teacht ar ais ach tá tú ag iarracht críochnú go tapa` |
| we want to meet this evening and we're trying to speak Irish all day | `Tá muid ag iarraidh bualadh le chéile tráthnóna inniu agus tá muid ag iarracht Gaeilge a labhairt an lá ar fad` |

The fourth is seed **222**'s own English shape (`he's trying to tell me what he wants`) —
the corpus supplies a native same-phrase pairing, and it should be used as one.

### Natural recurrence backbone — the concept is not dropped

After the block, the English corpus demands "try" at **17 further seeds**: 102, 103, 140,
146, 159, 195, 205, 213, 222, 226, 236, 372, 407, 491, 541, 579, 638. Seeds 102 and 103 are
adjacent, giving a second dense contrast cluster ~50 seeds after the first. No extra
scaffolding is needed to keep the form alive; the quota above only has to be met, not
invented.

## What is blocked, and why I did not paper over it

**`ag iarracht` is a progressive. 8 of the 22 "try" seeds in the corpus need a
non-progressive "to try", and Kai has not ruled a form for those.**

| fits `ag iarracht` (14) | needs a non-progressive form (8) |
|---|---|
| 46, 47, 50, 102, 103, 140, 159, 195, 205, 213, 222, 226, 372, 638 | **48, 49**, 146, 236, 407, 491, 541, 579 |

The two blocked ones sit **inside the introduction block**:

- **48 — "I want to try as hard as I can today."** `Tá mé ag iarraidh ag iarracht …` is
  ungrammatical. This seed is simultaneously the *ideal* contrast phrase — it carries both
  concepts in one sentence, natively — and the one the sanctioned form cannot express.
- **49 — "I'm going to try to explain what I mean."** `Tá mé chun ag iarracht …` is
  ungrammatical for the same reason.

**Both seed translations are now blank.** They previously carried `iarracht a dhéanamh`,
which Kai has rejected; leaving a rejected form sitting in the draft would be a landmine, and
inventing a substitute would be deciding something that is his. Blank is the honest state —
it is what 648 other untranslated seeds look like.

**Two ways forward, for Kai:**

1. **Rule a non-progressive partner form** for "to try" (the natural candidates are the ones
   already in play, so this is really a question of whether `ag iarracht` is the *only*
   form or the *progressive* form of a pair), or
2. **Move seeds 48 and 49 out** to sit with the other six non-progressive seeds, leaving the
   introduction block as 46, 47, 50 — all progressive, all clean. This costs one more
   permutation of the same kind already applied and can be done in a single run.

I have not chosen between them. Nothing is banked on either.
