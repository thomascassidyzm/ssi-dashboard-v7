## B. Where I overturned the inventory — Fork 10

The inventory recommended `taitníonn … liom` for "I like" and keeping `is maith liom` out of the
course entirely, to dissolve the `is`/`ba`/`níor mhaith liom` collision. **I measured what the
native base actually does with both words and it settles the fork the other way.**

| form | base corpus | what the English side says |
|---|---|---|
| `is maith liom` | **82** | *I like Irish · I like tea · I like to speak Irish* — "like", exclusively |
| `taitníonn` | **14** | *they enjoy · who enjoy · people who enjoy speaking Irish* — "enjoy", exclusively |

**The native already made the split, and it is not the one the inventory proposed.** Assigning
`taitníonn` to "like" would leave "enjoy" homeless — and the English corpus demands "enjoy" at
seeds **51, 55, 101, 206**, with 53 "enjoy" items in the base. So the inventory's option does not
dissolve one collision; it moves it onto a concept the course also has to teach.

Rule 1 (*evidence outranks taste, corpus outranks prescription*) decides it: 82 against 14, and the
native's own gloss lock.

**And the collision Fork 10 was afraid of is a learnable one.** `is maith liom` / `ba mhaith liom`
maps one-to-one onto a distinction **English already makes** — *like* vs *would like* — which is
exactly the argument that won Fork 6. That is not `iarraidh`/`iarracht`, which mapped onto nothing.

**Consequence for seed 12, which the inventory asked to review:** seed 12's banked gloss
*"I wouldn't like"* → `níor mhaith liom` **stands**. Ó Curnáin's observation that Connemara
`níor mhaith liom` can also read "I don't like" is a comprehension footnote, not a production
defect, and under this ruling nothing else in the course competes for that slot.

**The drilling design transfers here, as Kai instructed**, and is binding:
- `ba mhaith liom` debuts at **seed 11** (banked). `is maith liom` may not arrive before **seed 26**
  (the corpus's own first demand) — 15 seeds of separation, and no earlier.
- Once both are in, same-phrase contrast pairs are compulsory in the seed that introduces the
  second: `is maith liom labhairt` / `ba mhaith liom labhairt`; `is maith liom bualadh leat` /
  `ba mhaith liom bualadh leat`.
- Neither form is introduced then dropped: both must recur inside 10 seeds of the pairing.
- `taitníonn le` is reserved for "enjoy" and must not be glossed "like" anywhere.

## C. Fork 3 in detail — why `iarracht a dhéanamh` may sit at seed 8

This is the ruling I most expect Kai to challenge, so the reasoning is written out.

Under Forks 2 and 3 the course has two renderings of English "try":

| English shape | Irish | first seed |
|---|---|---|
| progressive — *I'm trying to X* | `tá mé ag iarraidh X` | 2 |
| after `chun` / `ag iarraidh` — *going to try to X*, *want to try to X* | `iarracht a dhéanamh` | 8 |

**They are in complementary distribution, and the cue is structural, not a matter of taste.** After
`tá mé chun` you cannot stack `ag iarraidh` — `tá mé chun ag iarraidh` is ungrammatical. So in every
sentence exactly one of the two is possible, and the learner never chooses.

**Seed 7 pre-teaches the frame one seed earlier.** Seed 7 (*"I want to try as hard as I can today"*)
is `Tá mé ag iarraidh mo dhícheall a dhéanamh inniu` — the `[noun] a dhéanamh` frame, with no
`iarracht` in it. Seed 8's only genuinely new piece is then the noun `iarracht` dropped into a frame
already met.

**Honest against it:** two "try" forms seven seeds apart is tight by the standard Kai set for
try/want (45 seeds). If he disagrees, the reversal is **one seed** — un-bank seed 8 and move it to
the corpus's next non-progressive demand at seed 146. Nothing else depends on it.

**`mo dhícheall a dhéanamh` (seed 7) has ZERO base-corpus support** — `dícheall` appears 0 times in
15,904 items, and the corpus has no "as hard as" English at all. It rests on **one** source, de
Bhaldraithe: *"I'll try my hardest to be there — déanfaidh mé mo mhíle dícheall a bheith ansin."*
Conf **B**. Flagged.

> **PARTLY CLOSED LATER THE SAME RUN — see §G.** Ó Curnáin vol IV carries the headword
> `deoicheall, (dícheall). m. Utmost.` with a transcribed example, so the *word* is attested in the
> target dialect. But the attested *frame* is `mo dhícheall a **thabhairt**`, and
> `dícheall a dhéanamh` is 0. Seed 7 may need `a thabhairt`. Native-ear list.

## D. The three provisional rulings, and what reversing costs

Ordered by how much rests on them — the order a native speaker should be asked.

### 1. Fork 7 — `labhraím` for "I speak" (HIGHEST STAKES)
**The question for a native:** for *"I speak a little Irish now"* (seed 9), would you say
`Labhraím beagán Gaeilge anois` or `Tá beagán Gaeilge agam anois`? One forum contributor called
`An labhraíonn tú Gaeilge?` *"utter nonsense"* for the ability sense. No source I reached states
that synthetic 1sg `labhraím` is specifically Connemara — the argument is from what sources did
**not** say.

**Reversal cost today: 3 seeds (9, 13, 14), ~60 phrases, 2 legos.** Cost at seed 100: roughly ten
times that, because "speak" is the course's spine concept. **This is the one to ask first, and it
gets more expensive every seed we build.**

### 2. Fork 11 — objectless `cén chaoi le labhairt` (seed 3)
**The question:** does `cén chaoi le labhairt chomh minic agus is féidir` sound right with no
object, or would you restructure? `cén chaoi le` has **0** hits in the base corpus — the base uses
`conas`, which is a defect we refuse — so this construction is supported only by `cén chaoi a
bhfuil tú` from corpas.ie, which is a different frame.

**Reversal cost: 1 seed (3), 3 legos, 17 phrases**, plus any later `cén chaoi le` re-uses. Cheap
now; it becomes the course's standard "how to" frame, so it compounds.

### 3. Fork 10 — `is maith liom` in Connemara
**The question:** in Carna/Cois Fharraige, do people actually say `is maith liom X`, or has
`ba mhaith liom` swallowed it? Ó Curnáin records `ba mhaith liomsa gubáiste` = *"I am fond of
cabbage"* with **present** meaning.

**Reversal cost: ZERO today** — "I like" is not demanded until seed 26 and nothing is built.
Cheapest of the three to reverse, which is why it is ruled rather than deferred.

## E. Binding build rules (carried forward, unchanged)

- **ZUT**: one known prompt → exactly one target. Two known prompts sharing one target is legal and
  is the point of Fork 2.
- Never chunk a function word into a lexical lego if it will recombine (`to remember → cuimhneamh`,
  with `ar …` as its own glossed piece).
- Teach verbal-noun legos **object-fronted**: `Gaeilge a labhairt`, `a ainm a fhoghlaim`,
  `céard atá i gceist agam a mhíniú`.
- Consult the base translation memory; do **not** inherit its defects: `conas`, `táimid`,
  `teastaíonn` for want, `níl fhios agam`, `labharófá`, `nó` for "No", `ag triail`.
- `chomh minic agus is féidir` stays as **one unsplit chunk** glossed *"as often as possible"* —
  `is féidir` is never a lego of its own, so Fork 5 leaves no competitor.
- Copula appears in the first 50 seeds only as frozen, individually-glossed chunks
  (`ba mhaith liom`, `níor mhaith liom`, `céard é`). Never as a paradigm.
- **ZERO TTS. ZERO SPEND.** Course stays `status=draft` / `new_app_status=not_available`.

## G. NEW EVIDENCE mid-run — Ó Curnáin Volume IV, mined for the first time

The inventory listed vols I, III and IV as unmined and named vol IV — the transcribed-speech and
glossary volume — as the obvious next target. **I mined it.** `/tmp/iorras4.pdf`, 5.5 MB, extracted
to 2.2 MB of text with a hand-written zlib/PDF reader (this sandbox has no PDF tooling; extractor

### It CONFIRMS Forks 1, 2, 5 and 10, from the dialect monograph itself

**Fork 2, and this is the best single citation in the whole file.** Ó Curnáin's own headword entry
for the target dialect:

> **`iarraidh, f, request, trying, turn, blow`**

He glosses `iarraidh` as **"trying"** himself. And two entries later:

> **`iarracht, f, attempt, try`**

Feminine noun, exactly as Fork 1 says. All 13 `iarracht` hits in vol IV are compound nouns
(`tréaniarracht` *intense attempt*, `móriarracht` *great effort*). **Zero `ag iarracht`.**

**Fork 5 confirmed hard:** the Connemara spoken form `i ndan` appears **112** times against `in ann`
31. `is féidir liom` does not carry the concept in this dialect.

**Fork 10 confirmed and upgraded from PROVISIONAL to sourced.** `is maith liom` is live, everyday
transcribed Connemara speech, not a schoolbook form:

> *"Is maith liom go dtáinigeamar beo"* · *"Ní maith liom héin iad a ithe"* ·
> *"Ní maith liom teilibhisean"* · *"ní maith liom na buidéil dhubha sin"* ·
> *"níor mhaith liom bheith in ~ léithi"*

**But one honest correction to my own Fork 10.** Ó Curnáin's headword is
**`taitnigh, v. Like.`** — he glosses `taitnigh` as *"Like"*, not *"enjoy"*
(*"Taitníonn leat, a Sheáin, a dtaitníonn?"*). So the dialect does **not** keep like/enjoy apart the
way my ruling does. The `is maith liom` = like / `taitníonn le` = enjoy split is therefore
**arbitrary-but-consistent — exactly like Fork 8 — and must be declared as such, not defended as a
distinction Irish makes.** It is still the right course design (it follows the base's own gloss lock
and gives each English prompt one predictable answer); it is just not a fact about Irish.

**Seed 7's `mo dhícheall` gap is closed.** The inventory had `dícheall` at 0 in the base corpus,
resting on de Bhaldraithe alone. Vol IV has the headword and a transcribed example:

> **`deoicheall, (dícheall). m. Utmost. (a) … tá mo dh-~ tugthaí am, I have done my utmost.`**

⚠️ **But the attested Connemara frame is `mo dhícheall a THABHAIRT`** (give my utmost), and
`dícheall a dhéanamh` occurs **0** times. Seed 7 currently reads `mo dhícheall a dhéanamh`. That is
standard Irish and de Bhaldraithe's own frame — but it is **not** the frame this dialect uses, and it
goes on the native-ear list.

### It REFUTES Fork 11 — so I reversed it

| probe | corpus | count |
|---|---|---|
| `conas le` | base, 15,904 items | **0** |
| `chaoi le` | Ó Curnáin vol IV | **0** |
| `conas [object] a [verbal noun]` | base | **20** |
| `cén chaoi a` + finite verb | Ó Curnáin vol IV, transcribed speech | attested ×5+ |

The native's frame carries **no preposition**: *"I want to learn how to speak Irish"* →
`tá mé ag iarraidh foghlaim conas Gaeilge a labhairt`. And real Connemara speech uses `cén chaoi a`
+ verb: *"Cén chaoi a ndéantaí anseo sa taobh seo tíre é?"*, *"cén chaoi a mbíonn mo chroí"*,
*"cén chaoi ar imigh sí"*.

**Two corpora, zero hits each, for the one word Fork 11 turned on.** The `le` is ours, not the
dialect's. So:

**RULING (revised): the `le` comes out. The "how" frame is —**
1. object present → `cén chaoi [object] a [verbal noun]` — seed 4 becomes
   `cén chaoi rud éigin a rá i nGaeilge`
2. finite verb → `cén chaoi a [verb]` — *"how do you feel"* → `cén chaoi a mothaíonn tú`
3. no object at all → bare `cén chaoi [verbal noun]` — seed 3 becomes
   `cén chaoi labhairt chomh minic agus is féidir`

Case 3 is the weak one: its only support is a single base item, `conas foghlaim`
(*"she'd know how to learn"*), 1 hit in 15,904. **It stays on the native-ear list** — but note that
dropping `le` is strictly better evidenced than keeping it, whatever a native says about case 3.

**Cost of the reversal:** seeds 3 and 4, plus every phrase line carrying `cén chaoi le`. Applied as
a snapshotted post-pass, since two workers were already mid-flight when the evidence landed.

### What vol IV could NOT settle — Fork 7, and the calibration that matters

`labhraím` is **0** in vol IV. **That is not a refutation, and anyone who reads it as one is wrong.**
Control probe, comparable 1sg present habitual forms in the same volume: `deirim` 6, `tuigim` 4,

doubts. So this volume's noise floor for a 1sg present habitual sits at 0–6 hits, and `labhraím`'s
zero is indistinguishable from it. Vol IV is a glossary; it indexes the unusual.

**Fork 7 therefore stays PROVISIONAL and stays the top native-ear question.** What vol IV *does* give
is indirect support for the paradigm's other half: the base has `Conas a labhraíonn tú Gaeilge go
han-mhaith?` — `labhraíonn tú` 84 items — so the 2sg is sourced even though the 1sg is not.

The inventory rejected `féach le` as the non-progressive "try" partly because *"Connacht has largely

the base course choosing it for our exact seed 407) — but it stands on fewer legs than it did this
morning. `breathnaigh` for "look" is still fine: the dialect form `breathú` is right there.

**3. `caithfidh` and `ceapaim` cannot be counted by string search in this volume** — `caith` has 175

**4. The second unsourced spec ban is STILL unsourced.** `is dóigh liom` as Munster: `dóigh liom` 0

a different word from our "can". So excluding it is correct, for a better reason than "it's Ulster",
which remains unverified.
