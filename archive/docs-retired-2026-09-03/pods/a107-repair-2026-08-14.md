### The 20 that dropped

Every drop is a sentence the learner heard that **no longer exists verbatim** in the new canon.
Most are the rewording class rule 6 exists for:

- "One. Two. Three. White. Black." → "1. 2. 3. White. Black."
- "Can I see the wine list?" → "Could I see the wine list?"
- "I'm learning Northern Welsh" → "I'm learning Welsh"
- "practise more" → "practice more", "twelve pounds fifty" → "twelve pound fifty"

Same sentence to us, a different thing to hear. They drop to unseen rather than carry, which
costs a little re-listening and never credits the unheard.

## The bound Tom asked me to choose and state

**The corresponding scene, and no more than 8 sentence positions within it** — where
*corresponding* is derived by content, not by scene number.

I picked it by measurement, not by fiat. Over all 4,062 content matches in all 37 candidate
courses:

- An **index window is wrong**: the pod grows 142 → 232, so every survivor shifts. Median
  |Δposition| is 10, reaching 90. Any window tight enough to mean anything rejects real
  survivors wholesale. A fractional-position window fails the same way — median 0.125.
- The **scene is the invariant**: 4,057 of 4,062 — 99.88% — stay in their corresponding scene.
  Within it, sentence position moves by 0 in 4,020 cases and by exactly 7 in 37, never more.

So 8 is the observed maximum plus one place of headroom: today it binds on nothing, and the
scene correspondence does all the work. Content-derived correspondence is what lets old scene
15 legitimately become new scene 22 when seven scenes are inserted ahead of it.

The five matches the bound rejects estate-wide are all the same numbers-drill line —
"100,000. 60. 70. 1 o'clock. 11 o'clock." — which stayed at literal scene 15 in five courses
while the rest of its scene moved to 22. A real relocation to a different dialogue, and exactly
what the bound is for.

## Progress cannot have gone backwards

By construction, not by care. `exposures` is a per-sentence maturity counter, and both doors
write back `effective + 1` where `effective` floors on the derived main-flow value, so a
dropped row cannot put a learner below what the main flow already knows. Course progress rides
`course_enrollments.completed_pod_rounds`, an independent ratchet this work never touched.

## Independently verified

Job #527 was briefed to **refute** this repair, not to confirm it, and re-derived every step
from scratch — its own audit-log replay, its own normalisation, its own scene-correspondence
and position bound, without reference to the tool's code. All four attack points hold:

- **The reconstructed canon is right.** Its independent replay produced exactly 142 rows per
  course, **id-for-id and text-for-text identical** to the separately-produced
  `pod0-welsh-prealign-archive-2026-08-06` snapshot — 0 mismatches in either direction.
- **The instant is right.** The last write to either pod before T was 2026-07-17 13:44, so the
  content was stable across the entire 2026-07-18 → 08-06 window learners actually used. And —
  the fact worth having — **zero learner exposures were ever recorded during the 2026-08-10
  buggy-content window** on either course, so the accidental Generate that day cannot have
  contaminated any row.
- **No row was misjudged.** Independent per-row comparison across all 136 rows: **0
  disagreements**. Nothing dropped that should have survived, nothing kept that should not have.
- **The arithmetic closes.** Before-CSV plus the applied log equals the live table exactly:
  115/115 rows, zero drift, zero exposure mismatches.

## Explicit gaps

1. **Four `cym_n` rows had mixed exposures and I could not split them.** Learner
   `7434474c` kept listening after the 11 August swap, so those rows — 37, 33, 17 and 11
   exposures — accrued partly on the old sentence and partly on the new. `learner_pod_state`
   keeps no history and `content_audit_log` holds nothing for it, so there is no way to
   separate them. I dropped them, per rule 6: they re-meet four sentences they have partly
   heard. The other direction would have credited a learner at rung 37 for something they had
   heard perhaps three times.

2. **The protocol is unexercised on genuinely finished new content**, because no language has
   any yet — every staged pod still has ~110 untranslated, unrecorded sentences, which is job
   #481's work. `pod-switchover.cjs` refuses on those preconditions, so it cannot go early by
   accident. Verified today: `fra_for_eng` plans its migration correctly — 146 carried, 10
   dropped, 15 mis-credits prevented — and then refuses the flip on 1 untranslated, 111 draft
   and 112 missing-audio sentences.

3. **Matching keys on the English known side** as the shared identity of a sentence across
   languages. True for the whole `*_for_eng` estate; it would need restating for a course whose
   known side is not English.

4. **A bug I introduced and caught before applying anything.** My first canon reconstruction
   trusted `content_audit_log` alone, which records UPDATE and DELETE and never INSERT — so
   rows created after the target instant survived the replay as though they had always existed.
   That produced a 232-row "old" Welsh canon holding both the original scene 15 and the scene
   22 it later became, 12 duplicate texts, and a matcher that refused to match any of them.
   Excluding rows by `created_at` recovers the true 142-row canon. The dry-run caught it; the
   fix and the trap are documented in the tool and the protocol.

## What is still Tom's

The protocol is written, running in code, and was **adopted as the standing rule by Tom on
2026-08-16** — plate item A-111, ruled "Approve" — alongside the short rules page. The one
open question in it stays open, and blocks nothing: progress is filed under a sentence's
*position* because the slot key *is* the sentence id. Filing it under *content* would make
this class of problem impossible rather than merely survivable. Costed, not done.
