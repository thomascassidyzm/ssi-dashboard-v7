# Northern Sami — questions for a native speaker

**Course:** `sme_for_eng` (Northern Sami for English speakers) · **2026-08-15**

Nobody on the team that built this speaks Northern Sami. Everything below is a point where
we made, or avoided making, a judgement we are not qualified to make. Each question is
written so a Sami speaker can answer it **without knowing anything about our system**.

Two are marked **BLOCKING**: the course currently teaches something we are guessing about,
and a wrong guess will be drilled into learners hundreds of times.

---

## A. BLOCKING — "doesn't want": which spelling is right, `háliid` or `háliit`?

Across the 668 translated sentences, the negative of "want" is written **two different
ways in the identical grammatical slot**. Both follow a negative word (`in` / `it` / `ii` /
`eat`), both translate English "don't/doesn't want to…".

**Spelling 1 — `háliid`** (4 sentences, all early):

| # | English | Sami |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | But I don't want to stop talking. | muhto mun in **háliid** heaitit hupmamis |
| 34 | He doesn't want to be quiet when other people are here. | son ii **háliid** leat jaska go eará olbmot leat dáppe |
| 35 | She doesn't want to read anything this afternoon. | son ii **háliid** lohkat maidege odne eahketbeaivvi |
| 36 | We don't want to interrupt the story. | mii eat **háliid** gaskkalduhttit muitalusa |

**Spelling 2 — `háliit`** (12 sentences, all later): sentences 241, 251, 269, 300, 302,
304, 399, 439, 473, 474, 500, 538 — e.g. "I don't want to give it to him" → *mun in
**háliit** addit dan sutnje*.

**Question: which one is correct Northern Sami? Is the other one simply wrong, or are both
acceptable?** If both are acceptable we still need to pick one and use it everywhere,
because a learner who hears two forms for one meaning stops being able to produce either.

Why it is blocking: sentence 19 is inside the block we are building. Whichever way you
rule, twelve or four sentences need correcting.

## B. BLOCKING — "we": the two-of-us form is being used for plain English "we"

Northern Sami distinguishes **two people** (`moai`) from **three or more** (`mii`), with
different verb endings. Our English sentences say only "we", and the translation has used
**both**, apparently without the English distinguishing them:

| # | English | Sami | form |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | **We** want to meet at six o'clock this evening. | **mii** háliidit deaivvadit… | three or more |
| 36 | **We** don't want to interrupt the story. | **mii** eat háliid… | three or more |
| 102 | **We're** trying to say that it's not like that. | **moai** geahččaletne dadjat… | two of us |
| 104 | **We** need to change what we're doing. | **moai** dárbbašetne rievdadit… | two of us |
| 109 | **We** must work hard to learn a lot of new words. | **moai** fertejetne bargat… | two of us |

`moai` is used in 18 sentences, `mii` in 52. Sentence 501 likewise uses the two-of-you form
`doai … stoahkabeahtti` for English "you … together".

**Questions:**
1. If an English speaker says just "we", with no clue how many people, **which form should
   a learner produce?** We assume the plural `mii` is the safe default — is that right?
2. Is there anything **wrong** with the sentences that use `moai`, or are they fine Sami
   that simply happens to mean "the two of us"?
3. If we want to teach `moai` deliberately later, is *"the two of us"* a fair English
   way to prompt it?

Why it is blocking: a learner cannot guess which one to say. As it stands, English "we"
has two different Sami answers, which is the one thing our method must never allow. This
does not affect the sentences built so far (they stop at 10) but it stops anyone continuing
past roughly sentence 100.

## C. Does sentence 7 say "as hard as I can" or "as well as I can"?

| English | Sami |
|---|---|
| I want to try **as hard as I can** today. | mun háliidan geahččalit **nu bures go sáhtán** odne |

`bures` looks to us like "well", not "hard". We have taught this phrase to learners as
**"as well as I'm able to"**, because that is what we think the Sami actually says, rather
than pretend it says "as hard as".

**Question: is *nu bures go sáhtán* the natural way to say "as hard as I can" in Sami, or
does it really mean "as well as I can"?** If Sami has a separate way of saying "as hard as
I can", we would rather use that and keep the English honest.

## D. Is `eará olbmuin` "with someone else" or "with other people"?

| English | Sami |
|---|---|
| I'm going to practise speaking **with someone else**. | mun áiggun hárjehallat hupmat **eará olbmuin** |

We cannot tell whether `olbmuin` here is one person or several.

**Questions:**
1. Does *eará olbmuin* mean "with one other person" or "with other people"?
2. If a learner wants to say "**with another person**", singular, is *eará olbmuin* what
   they should say?

## E. Are these four sentences natural things a person would actually say?

These are sentences we composed ourselves from the pieces the course has taught, so nobody
has ever checked them. They will be repeated to learners many times, so we would rather
delete them than teach something odd. **For each: does it sound like something a Sami
speaker would really say? If not, how would you say it?**

1. mun háliidan oahppat mo hupmat davvisámegiela — *"I want to learn how to speak Northern Sami"*
2. mun geahččalan čilget maid mun háliidan — *"I'm trying to explain what I want"*
3. mun háliidan muitit mo dadjat juoidá davvisámegillii — *"I want to remember how to say something in Northern Sami"*
4. mun in leat sihkkar maid mun geahččalan čilget — *"I'm not sure what I'm trying to explain"*

Specifically: **is the word order right**, and does putting `mo` ("how") or `maid`
("what") in the middle of a sentence like this work, or does Sami need something else there?

## F. Two words for "Northern Sami" — have we got the split right?

The translation uses two forms, and we have taught them as two separate things:

| We teach | as | seen in |
|---|---|---|
| davvisámegiela | "Northern Sami" (the thing you speak or learn) | *I want to speak Northern Sami* |
| davvisámegillii | "in Northern Sami" (the language you say something in) | *how to say something in Northern Sami* |

**Question: is that the right division?** I.e. would a learner correctly say
*mun háliidan oahppat davvisámegiela* for "I want to learn Northern Sami", and
*dadjat juoidá davvisámegillii* for "to say something in Northern Sami"?

---

## G. Do these two go together correctly?

We have taught `sáhtán` ("I'm able to") and, separately, `sáhtán go` ("if I'm able to" /
"am I able to"), from sentence 10: *mun in leat sihkkar, sáhtán go muitit olles cealkaga*.

**Question: is adding `go` after `sáhtán` the normal way to turn it into "whether/if I
can"?** And is *sáhtán go muitit sáni* — "if I can remember a word" — correct as it stands?
