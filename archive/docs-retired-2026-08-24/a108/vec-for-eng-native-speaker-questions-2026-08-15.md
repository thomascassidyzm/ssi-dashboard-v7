# Questions for a Venetian speaker — vec_for_eng

**2026-08-15.** Nobody on this build speaks Venetian. Each question below is a
judgement that genuinely needs a native speaker; none of them was fudged. You do
**not** need to know anything about how our system works to answer — just read the
question and say what a Venetian speaker would actually do.

Answers to A–F change built content. G–J affect the 638 seeds not yet built.

---

## A. Is `ła` really both "the" and "she"?

We teach `ła risposta` = *the answer* and `ła vol` = *she wants*, i.e. the same little
word `ła` doing two jobs. **Is that right, or have we got one of them wrong?**

## B. `catarse` vs `conoser` — do these both mean "to meet"?

Seed 18 has `volemo catarse a łe sie` for *we want to meet at six o'clock*.
Seed 22 has `vojo conoser xente che parla vèneto* for *I want to meet people who speak Venetian*.

We have taught **`catarse` = "to meet"** and **`conoser` = "to get to know"**, on the
guess that `catarse` is meeting *up* with someone by arrangement and `conoser` is
meeting someone *new*. **Is that distinction real, and is "to get to know" a fair
English label for `conoser`?**

## C. `ti` vs `te` — when do you use which "you"?

We teach `co ti` = *with you* but `te parli` = *you speak* and `te vol` = *you want*.
So "you" comes out as `ti` in one place and `te` in another. **Is that correct?
What is the rule, in your own words?**

## D. `no vedo l'ora de` for "I'm looking forward to"

Seed 29 uses `no vedo l'ora de parlar mejo` for *I'm looking forward to speaking better*.
Literally this looks like "I don't see the hour to…". **Is this the natural everyday way
to say you're looking forward to something? Would a Venetian speaker say it out loud in
conversation, or does it read as written/bookish?**

## E. Seed 21 says "her name" but the Venetian says `el so nome`

The English seed is *Why are you learning **her** name?* and the Venetian is
`parcosa situ drio a inparar **el so** nome?`. But seed 20 uses the **same** `el so nome`
for *his name*. **Does Venetian genuinely not distinguish his from her here?** If it
does distinguish, seed 21's Venetian is wrong and we need the correct form.

*(We avoided the word "her" in all practice phrases rather than guess.)*

## F. `sentirme` glossed as "feeling"

Seed 26: `me piaxe sentirme come se fuse squasi pronto par andar` = *I like feeling as if
I'm nearly ready to go*. We teach `sentirme` = **"feeling"**. **Is that a fair label, or
does `sentirme` really mean something closer to "to feel myself" / "to feel"?**

## G. The spelling question — which one is the house style?

(the unified spelling): `ła`, `paroła`, `xe`, `bixogno`, `tenpo`. But **16 words are
spelled two different ways** across the corpus. Please pick the one you'd want learners
to see for each:

## H. Is `ç` right in `scominçiar`, `façilmente`, `çito`, `atençion`?

It appears in only 7 of 668 seeds, always before a front vowel. **Is `ç` the spelling you
want, or should these be `s` / `z` / something else?**

## I. Which Venetian is this?

Venetian varies a lot between Venice, Padua, Verona, Treviso, Vicenza and the coast.
**Which variety do these translations read as?** We need to know before casting a voice,
because a learner should hear one consistent variety.

## J. Do the future forms sound natural?

We lean on the one-word future a lot: `farò`, `provarò`, `scominçiarò`, `podarò`,
`giutaràtu`. **In everyday speech, would a Venetian speaker use these, or would they
more often say something like "I'm going to…" with a helper verb?**

## The honest limit

Our automated checks can only catch a pairing that **contradicts another pairing** in the
same course. They cannot catch a pairing that is wrong **consistently everywhere** —
nothing contradicts it, so nothing flags it. Everything above is in that blind spot.
**A clean automated check does not mean this content is verified correct.** Only these
answers close that gap.
