# A Small Experiment: Japanese Course Optimisation

*A proposal for discussion - January 2026*

---

## Part 1: What We Know Works

The SSi methodology is proven:

- **The 668 seeds** capture the communicative patterns that matter
- **"No copying, no repeating, all creating"** forces the learner to do the cognitive work
- **LEGO recombination** means learners produce sentences they've never heard
- This creates durable memories because the learner builds it themselves

This is the foundation. It works. It's been refined over 17 years.

---

## Part 2: What the Seeds Actually Are (A Layer Analysis)

Looking at the 668 seeds, they cluster into natural **communicative layers** - the things humans need to do when they interact:

### Layer 0: The Learning Conversation (Meta)
*"I want to practice with you" / "How long have you been learning?" / "I don't worry about mistakes"*

This is unique to SSi. From seed 1, learners can talk about what they're actually doing. This is immediately true, immediately useful, and puts them in a forgiving context where native speakers will be patient.

**Seeds in this layer**: ~50 (S0001-S0010, S0033, S0038, S0046-S0048, S0061, S0064, S0072-S0079, S0090, S0101, S0114-S0122, S0137, S0160, S0174...)

### Layer 1: Basic Needs
*"I'm tired" / "I need more time" / "Can I have something to drink?"*

Survival communication. Express immediate physical and emotional states.

**Seeds in this layer**: ~35 (S0039-S0041, S0093, S0096, S0139, S0144, S0192-S0193, S0473, S0548, S0595-S0596, S0623-S0632...)

### Layer 2: Contributing to the Moment
*"That's a good idea" / "What do you think?" / "No problem"*

The ability to participate in a conversation without having to lead it. React, agree, add, redirect. Be present.

**Seeds in this layer**: ~55 (S0112, S0123, S0141-S0143, S0158, S0162-S0165, S0172-S0173, S0187-S0191, S0196...)

### Layer 3: Self and Perspectives
*"I like..." / "I think that..." / "I don't agree with..."*

Meaningful self-expression. Not biographical trivia ("I have two brothers") but actual engagement - opinions, preferences, reactions that matter in context.

**Seeds in this layer**: ~90 (S0026-S0027, S0051, S0083-S0084, S0120, S0125, S0135, S0145, S0180, S0486, S0491, S0503, S0536-S0537, S0571, S0615...)

### Layer 4: Involving Others
*"He said..." / "She thinks..." / "My friend told me..."*

Bringing other people into the conversation. Reported speech, other perspectives, talking about people who aren't present.

**Seeds in this layer**: ~110 (S0016-S0017, S0034-S0035, S0052-S0053, S0070, S0105, S0130, S0132, S0147-S0148, S0176-S0177, S0197-S0200, S0511, S0533, S0589, S0597, S0604, S0622...)

### Layer 5: Sophisticated Expression
*"I would have done it differently if I had known" / "If only I could..." / "However much I tried..."*

Complex grammar, hypotheticals, conditionals, abstract ideas, nuanced perspectives.

**Seeds in this layer**: ~180 (S0152-S0153, S0469, S0481-S0485, S0490, S0501, S0518, S0544, S0557, S0563-S0566, S0593, S0599, S0606-S0608, S0621...)

### Layer 6: Register and Formality
*"...sir" / "...madam" / "you all"*

Politeness markers, formal address, plural forms.

**Seeds in this layer**: ~30 (S0639-S0668)

---

### The Glue: Questions, Responses, Statements

Across ALL layers, learners need to:
- **Ask questions** about the topic
- **Respond** to questions about the topic
- **Make statements** about the topic

The seeds provide all three forms throughout. This is the conversational glue.

---

### What This Analysis Shows

The 668 seeds aren't random. They're a carefully assembled toolkit for human communication, organised (roughly) by what people need to do:

1. First, talk about what you're doing (meta)
2. Then, express basic needs
3. Then, participate in conversation
4. Then, share perspectives
5. Then, involve other people
6. Then, express complex ideas
7. Finally, adjust for formality

**The seeds ARE the concepts. They're universal.**

What varies by language is *how* those concepts are expressed - and therefore *what order* makes sense for building up the patterns efficiently.

## Part 3: An Observation from Japanese

When we built Japanese, we noticed something: the order that works beautifully for English (and languages close to English) created longer stretches before learners could say much.

Not because the seeds were wrong - the *communicative needs* are universal. But because Japanese expresses those needs with very different structures, and some English patterns require Japanese grammar that takes time to build up to.

The result: a longer runway before the learner feels "I can actually say things."

### Why This Happens

Consider: English front-loads "I want to..." patterns early. In Spanish, "Quiero hablar" works almost identically - same structure, cognate vocabulary, easy transfer.

But in Japanese, "I want to speak" = 話したいです (hanashitai desu). To say this, the learner needs:
- The verb stem (話し)
- The desire suffix (たい)
- The polite copula (です)

That's three new concepts for one seed. Whereas "This is X" = これはXです requires only:
- This (これ)
- Topic marker (は)
- Copula (です)

Same layer (meta/basic), but radically different cognitive load.

**The insight**: For English→Japanese, we might front-load patterns that are *structurally simple in Japanese*, even if they appear later in the English-optimised seed order.

### What's Simple in Japanese?

- **です/ですか** (is/is it?) - stick on anything
- **は** (topic marker) - topic anything
- **ます form verbs** - regular, polite, safe
- **か** (question marker) - turn any statement into a question

Five items. Massive recombination. Low cognitive load per item.

### What's Complex in Japanese?

- Conditional forms (if X then Y)
- Reported speech (he said that...)
- Relative clauses (the person who...)
- Desire forms (want to...)

These require stacking multiple grammar points. They might need to come later, even if the *communicative concept* appears early in the English seed order.

## Part 4: The Adherence Question

We know the first 30 days matter for retention. Learners who feel early progress stick around. Learners who feel stuck often don't realise that languages are genuinely hard - they just feel like they're not getting anywhere.

What if we could give them more "I can say this!" moments earlier?

Not by making it easier. Not by removing the cognitive work (that's what makes it stick). But by choosing *which* patterns to introduce first based on what gives maximum speakability in Japanese specifically.

---

## Part 5: A Simple Experiment

**Proposal**: Build one Japanese course (MVP-260 seeds) where the order is optimised for Japanese.

Same seeds. Same methodology. Same LEGOs. Same "create, don't copy" principle.

Just reordered so that:
- Patterns that are simple in Japanese come early
- Patterns that require complex Japanese grammar come later
- Each new item builds on what's already there
- Learners can recombine and create from day one

---

## Part 6: What This Isn't

- It's not abandoning the seeds (they're the foundation)
- It's not changing the methodology (that's the magic)
- It's not saying the current approach is wrong (it works for most languages)

It's asking: for languages that are structurally distant from English, can we get learners to speakability faster by thinking about the order from the *target language's* perspective?

---

## Part 7: Why Low Risk

- One course, one language
- MVP-260 seeds (not a massive investment)
- We can compare adherence/progress against the standard approach
- If it works, we learn something valuable
- If it doesn't, we've lost very little

---

## Part 8: The Hypothesis

Learners in the reordered course will:
- Feel "I can say things" sooner
- Have higher 30-day retention
- Cover the same ground, just in a different sequence

The seeds remain the gold. The methodology remains the magic. We're just asking if the *path through* the seeds can be optimised per language.
