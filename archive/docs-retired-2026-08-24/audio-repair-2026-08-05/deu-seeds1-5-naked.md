## The step that did it is already gone

The amputation came from `repairTailDefect`, which trimmed every clip its detector flagged and re-padded it. That detector was 9% precise by ear and fired on 70% of brand-new TTS output, so it was cutting good audio at scale. **It was deleted from the render path on 4 August** — not disabled behind a flag, deleted, so no environment can turn it back on.

Every deployed clip in seeds 1-5 was made before that deletion. So this is a repair backlog, not a live bug: the thing to stop doing has already been stopped, and what remains is audio that was damaged while it was still running.


### What the pipeline actually runs now, split the way you asked

| step | what it is for | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `acompressor` 8:1 below -24dB | tames peaky xAI voice clones so the loudness target is reachable | **gain — keep**, though it is what squashes a natural decay from 620ms to 180ms; worth revisiting for its own sake |
| `volume=NdB` | hits -16 LUFS so voices match | **gain — keep**. This is the legitimate normalisation you described |
| `alimiter` at -1.5 dBTP | stops the gain clipping | **gain — keep** |
| 8ms fade in at each end | kills the encoder click at the boundaries | **time-domain, but 8ms** — it removes no word. Keep unless you want it gone on principle |
| `flagTailDefect` | reports suspects for human ears | **read-only, mutates nothing.** 9% precise, and it says so wherever it appears |
| ~~`repairTailDefect`~~ | trimmed and re-padded flagged clips | **deleted 4 August. This was the amputator** |
| ~~`verifyTrimKeepsText`~~ | guarded the trim | **deleted with it** |

There is no silence-stripping step and no trim step left in the chain. The only remaining time-domain operation is the 8ms fade.

## The two things you floated

**Rebuild the mastering tool from scratch.** The evidence does not ask for it. The chain that remains is measurement, gain, a limiter and an 8ms fade, and running the raw clips through it kept every word: file lengths came out within 5ms of raw. What it does do is flatten the natural decay — 620ms becomes 180ms on "Ich will jetzt Deutsch lernen" — because the compressor lifts the noise floor. That is a taste question about how a clip breathes, not a damage question, and it is a one-constant change rather than a rebuild. My read: keep the chain, and if you want the breath back, drop the compressor for xAI voices — `normalizeAudioClean` already exists and does exactly that.

**Pad xAI clips with silence before processing.** Nothing in the current chain needs headroom, because nothing in it trims. Padding was already tested against the old detector and changed 0 of 48 paired verdicts. My read: no, it adds bytes and buys nothing now that the trimmer is gone.
