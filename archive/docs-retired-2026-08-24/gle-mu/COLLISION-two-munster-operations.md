## The good news: the two specs mostly agree

This is not two different languages. Independently derived, both landed on:

- **`Gaelainn`**, not `Gaeilge`, for the language
- **`as Gaelainn`**, not `i nGaeilge` — and both noted this *inverts* the Connemara ruling
- **`ná fuil`** for the negative subordinator, not `nach bhfuil`
- **`teastaíonn uaim`** for "want" — both rejected `ag iarraidh` as the want-word
- **object + `a` + lenited VN** for the object rule
- **`go bhfuil Gaelainn acu`** for "people who have Irish"

That convergence from two separate evidence bases is worth something: the core dialect calls are
probably right.

## Where they differ — the actual decision

| | my spec (#545) | their locked spec | who has the better evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| "trying to" | `ag iarraidh` + VN | **`táim d'iarraidh`** + VN | **theirs** — cited to Ó Sé §686. I read their form as eye-dialect and was wrong; I checked and withdrew that. |
| "something" | `éigin` | **`éigint`** | **theirs** — they show Ó Sé's *quoted speech* says `éigint` and that his `éigin` hits are his own metalanguage, plus `ar éigin` inflating the count. Better argued than mine. |
| "today" | **`inniubh`** | `inniu` | **mine** — measured `inniubh` 1374 vs `inniu` 48 *inside* the Kerry spoken source, and Connemara inverts (103 vs 813). |
| "able to" | **`ábalta`** + bare VN | `ábalta ar` + VN | **mine** — the corpus shows a bare VN: *"bhí sé ábalta rudaí a fheiceáil"*, *"ní raibh sé ábalta imeacht"*. |
| "unless" | `muna` | `mura` | **mine, weakly** — `muna` 378 vs `mura` 106 within Kerry, but `muna` is also the Ulster form, so it is not a unique Munster badge. |

Neither spec is a clean winner. The sane outcome is **one spec, chosen deliberately, with the best
call taken from each** — not the current mixture, which has `ag iarraidh` and `táim d'iarraidh` both
live in the same course and therefore breaks ZUT.

## Findings from my side that are additive regardless of who owns the course

These came out of five corpus-evidence workers and I have not seen them in the other spec. They are
worth keeping whichever way the decision goes:

- **`sinn`, never `muid`** — 342 : 34 in Kerry, and Connemara is 169:1 the other way. But 1pl subject
  is normally carried by the synthetic verb ending with no pronoun at all.
- **The `-gh-` spellings lose.** `chugham`/`chughat` are a 4–6% minority *within Kerry's own speech*
  (`chugam` 229 : `chugham` 15). They are folk-belief, not a dialect marker. Write `chugam`.
- **`do`/`de` keep bare `d-`** — `dom`/`duit`/`dó` beat `dhom`/`dhuit`/`dhó` about 6:1 in Kerry, and
  Connemara inverts. `domh` is Ulster, 1 hit in Kerry.
- **`fé` yes, but `fém` does not exist** — the contraction happens with the article (`fén` 771), not
  with the possessive. Write `fé mo`.
- **`ní fheadar`** for "I don't know" — 887 : 3, the largest single dialect ratio measured.
- **Emphasis goes on the verb** (`táimse`, `bhíosa`), not on a free `mise`. And `aigesean`,
  `leisean`, `airsean` are **0** in Kerry — use `aige sin` or don't emphasise.
- **Don't write the preverbal past `do`** — authentic but marginal, on 2–7% of past-tense verbs.
  Writing `do chuas` throughout would represent the dialect at ~20× its real rate.
- **Don't write `thá`** — 7,558 hits in the Kerry spoken source, but **0** in Baile Mhuirne and 3 in
  Cork Irish. It is the transcriber capturing aspiration, not written Munster.
