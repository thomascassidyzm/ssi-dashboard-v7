## The worst thing I found: a sweep deleted three `yes` answers

Seeds 386, 425 and 448 each carried a correct verb-echo answer at 22:28 and had lost it by 22:32:

| seed | English | was | is now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 386 | yes I agreed with her | `d'aontaigh, d'aontaigh mé léi` | `d'aontaigh mé léi` |
| 425 | yes they need to make sure they understand | `tá, tá orthu a chinntiú…` | `tá orthu a chinntiú…` |
| 448 | yes they'll be happy to help | `beidh, beidh siad sásta cuidiú` | `beidh siad sásta cuidiú` |

The pattern is exact: the echo collapsed **only** where the echoed verb is identical to the first
word of the following clause — `d'aontaigh, d'aontaigh` → `d'aontaigh`. That is a duplicate-word
de-duplication acting on a construction where the duplication is the whole point. In every one of
the three, the English `yes` now has **no Irish at all**.

Measured course-wide on the same snapshot, not estimated: 58 seeds carry a bare English yes/no,
53 carry an echo, seed 141 (`no problem`) and seed 508 (`there's no point`) are the legitimate
idiom exceptions ruling (c) allows — **leaving exactly these three, all three inside my range.**
So the damage is currently contained, and it is a three-line fix.

## The other two defects

- **396** — `níl orainn seasamh go dtí go bhfuil achan duine eile réidh`. Present indicative after
  `go dtí go` where the reference is future. → `go dtí go mbíonn`. Not a dialect matter; Donegal
  takes the same forms.
- **598** — `faoi caidé a bhí á dhéanamh acu`. "what they were doing" here is a **free relative**,
  not an embedded question, so it needs `an rud a`, which is exactly what the same block does at
  606, 648 and 663. → `faoin rud a bhí á dhéanamh acu`.

## Does it sound like Donegal?

**Yes — and not by word-swapping.** The dialect is carried in the *grammar*, which is the test that
matters. `tá orainn` rather than `caithfidh muid` for "need to" across ten seeds; `de dhíth ar` for
need-a-thing; `cionn is go` for because; `thiocfadh le` as a whole conditional paradigm rather than
`d'fhéadfadh`; `tá mé ag déanamh go` for "I suspect" at 597; `i ndiaidh` as the perfect right
through 600–622; the analytic `tá muid` / `sula stadann muid` without a single synthetic slip; and
the preposition+article lenition applied consistently rather than sprinkled.

The vocabulary follows the same discipline — `achan`, `uilig`, `inteacht`, `caidé`, `domh`,
`iontach`, `cuidiú`, `amharc`, `múscailt`, `dóigh`, `úr`, `cuartaigh`, `druid`, `gasúr`, `girseach`,
`scéala`, `cluinte` — but the point is that removing every one of those words would still leave
Donegal grammar underneath. That is the opposite of the failure mode the spec was written to
prevent.

**One honest reservation.** The 30-seed sir/madam/you-all cluster at 639–668 is by design a drill of
near-identical lines, and it is where the register is thinnest: correct, consistent, but flat. It is
the only stretch of my 188 that reads like a course rather than like speech. That is a property of
the English seeds, not of the translation.

future negatives (446, 450) could not.
