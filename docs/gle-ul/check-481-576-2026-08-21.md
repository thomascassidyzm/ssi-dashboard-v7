# Ulster Irish correctness pass — seeds 481–576, the missing fourth block

**Why this pass exists.** Four correctness workers were dispatched overnight on 2026-08-20 over the
668 translated seeds of `gle_ul_for_eng`. Three reported and published: 1–192, 193–384, and
385–480 + 577–668. The fourth, on 481–576, hit an account rate limit at 22:30 and never produced a
check file — not a late result, a failed one. Kai read those 96 seeds himself in the interim and
said plainly that his own read is weaker coverage than an independent pass, because he wrote the
spec they were judged against. This pass is that independent re-check, run against the same spec
(`docs/gle-ul/ulster-dialect-spec.md`) and the same brief (`scripts/gle-ul/check-brief.md`) the
other three workers used.

**Scope.** All 96 seeds, 481–576 inclusive. No sampling — every seed got a verdict, `ok` rows
included, so coverage is demonstrated rather than asserted. Full row-per-seed detail is in
`scripts/gle-ul/checks/check-481-576.json` (gitignored, per this repo's `scripts/` convention —
this document is the durable record).

## Defects

**`caidé` instead of `goidé` — three seeds missed by the normalisation sweep.** Spec §1e reversed
an earlier ruling on measured evidence (`goidé` UL 1,477 vs `caidé` UL 45 — Donegal writes `goidé`
33× as often) and requires it uniformly; 43 seeds were normalised course-wide. Seeds **493, 494 and
572** in this window still use `caidé` for "what":

| seed | English | current | should be |
|---|---|---|---|
| 493 | what's going to come next? | `caidé a thiocfas ansin?` | `goidé a thiocfaidh ansin?` |
| 494 | what happens after the first part of the show? | `caidé a tharlaíonn i ndiaidh an chéad chuid den tseó?` | `goidé a tharlaíonn i ndiaidh an chéad chuid den tseó?` |
| 572 | have you forgotten what happened the last time they visited us? | `...caidé a tharla an uair dheireanach...` | `...goidé a tharla an uair dheireanach...` |

This is the one hard finding this pass adds beyond what the block-6 build report's own self-check
already named. Kai's self-check commentary says plainly that letting `caidé` "through" was exactly
the blind spot an author-as-checker has — this confirms it happened, at these three seeds
specifically.

**`dada` instead of `a dhath` — seed 485.** Same class of error, same spec section (§1e-bis):
`dada` is the forbidden Connacht form for "nothing"; Donegal corpus evidence gives `dhath` UL 596 /
CO 22 / MU 1 against `dada` UL 6 / CO 40 / MU 5.

| seed | English | current | should be |
|---|---|---|---|
| 485 | nothing would make me happier than to get away | `ní chuirfeadh dada níos mó áthais orm ná éalú` | `ní chuirfeadh a dhath níos mó áthais orm ná éalú` |

**Discontinuous predicate — seed 552.** `tá an séipéal ag an cheann eile den tsráidbhaile gránna`
("the church at the other end of the village is ugly") separates the predicate adjective `gránna`
from its subject `an séipéal` with the entire locative phrase in between, reading as though the
phrase itself is what's ugly rather than the church. Better: `tá an séipéal atá ag an cheann eile
den tsráidbhaile gránna`. This is a genuine word-order defect, independently flagged in the block-6
build report's own self-check as well — this pass confirms it rather than discovering it fresh.

## Doubts

- **Seeds 481, 482 — `fíordhóchas`.** Not an Ó Dónaill headword; a live FGB lookup
  (`scripts/gle-ul/fgb.cjs fíordhóchas`) returned no dictionary section. It's a transparent `fíor-`
  compound, not obviously wrong, but it is an unattested coinage for "real hope" and the translator's
  own note already flags the uncertainty. A Donegal speaker should look rather than either of us
  guessing.
- **Seed 493 — `a thiocfas`.** Alongside the `caidé` defect above, this seed's relative future uses
  the `-as`/`-eas` ending (`thiocfas` for standard `thiocfaidh`). It is the *only* instance of this
  ending anywhere in the 668-seed course, and it's textbook-associated with Munster relative futures
  rather than Ulster ones; neither the spec nor the Wikipedia Ulster Irish article it draws on
  confirms or denies it for Donegal, so this is a doubt, not a defect.

question is still open, so this is neutral, and there is no over-application onto subordinate
`nach`/`nár` sites); no bare yes/no polarity-answer seeds fall in this range, so the §2d echo rule
doesn't apply here. The required Ulster lenition-after-simple-preposition-plus-singular-article
feature (§2d) is applied correctly and consistently where it should be — seeds 488, 498, 514, 515,
521, 552 and 559 all lenite where the standard would eclipse, and none of those are reported as
errors. `tá muid` / `muid` analytic forms, `sílim`/`bogaim` synthetic-1sg-present, the `tig
le`/`ábalta` split, `domh`, `achan`, `uilig`, `amharc`, `madadh`, `cad chuige`, `cionn is (go)`, and
the `fosta`-family forms are all present, used correctly, and used consistently across the 96
seeds — none of these had to be flagged.

## Honest read: does this sound like Donegal?

Yes, and more strongly than a standard-plus-vocabulary swap would. The dialect markers are load-
bearing grammar, not decoration: the lenition-after-preposition feature above, the analytic `tá
muid` past/present throughout, `cionn is go` for "because" used consistently at seeds 502, 547 and
549, and `madadh` (not `madra`) at 546. The two `caidé` misses and the one `dada` miss are real, but
they're leftover residue from the normalisation sweep rather than evidence the block was translated
to the wrong target — the same sweep fixed 43 other `caidé` instances and 4 other `dada` instances
course-wide, and these three plus one simply weren't among them.
