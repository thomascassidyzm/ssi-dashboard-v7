### One sub-decision I had to make, and you should see it

The ruling gave one example sentence, which has nothing after the verbal noun. Nine of the seventeen
rows have a trailing word — *inniu*, *anois*, *i nGaeilge*. Moving the clause to the end by brute
string-shuffling would have put that word after the clause:

> *tá mé chun a mhíniú **céard atá i gceist agam i nGaeilge***

which now reads "what I mean **in Irish**" — a fresh ambiguity, swapping one misparse for another.
So I kept the little word next to the verb it modifies and sent the heavy clause to the end:

> *tá mé chun a mhíniú **i nGaeilge** céard atá i gceist agam*

That is still "clause after the verbal noun", which is what you ruled. If you would rather have the
literal string move, it is nine rows and a minute's work.

## Ruling 1 — "I am going to". NOT APPLIED. Here is why.

**Your premise is right, and I confirmed it myself rather than taking it on trust.** Re-probing all
four volumes of Ó Curnáin:

- *chun* followed by a softened verbal noun: **0**
- *tá mé / tá sé / tá tú / tá muid …* + *chun*: **0**
- *chun* at all: 59 tokens, against 121 for *Gaeilge* and 521 for *duine* — a rare word to begin with

The calibration controls passed exactly (Gaeilge 121, duine 521, bhí 3,133), so those zeros are real
zeros. **Nothing in this document argues for keeping *tá mé chun*.** It is not Connemara.

### The problem is the replacement, not the diagnosis

The ruling names the replacement as *ag goil / ag dul **a** + softened verbal noun*. I went looking
for that form to copy its shape, and found something that changes the job. Because this cuts against
the ruling, I did not trust my own reading — I commissioned **#564** to try to demolish it. It could
not, and it came back with more than I had.

Every single attestation of *ag goil a* + softened verbal noun in the four volumes has its object
thrown to the **very front of the clause** — relativised or topicalised:

> *talamh a bheitheá **ag goil a chur*** — Ó Curnáin's own example, vol III §8.85
> ***CÉARD** tá muid **ag dul a dhéanamh** anois?* — 866E
> ***NA FATAÍ** tá tú **ag goil a ghearradh*** — speaker 01P
> *mar a dhíonthá le **caora** bheitheá **ag goil a bhearradh*** — speaker 11C

The course cannot make that shape. Its sentences are plain statements, and its whole tile
architecture puts the object immediately before the verbal noun — *Gaeilge a labhairt*,
*labhairt a chleachtadh*, *rud eicínt a rá*. So a straight swap would produce:

> *tá mé **ag goil Gaeilge a labhairt***

and **that shape — an object sitting between *ag goil* and the softened verbal noun — is zero in
all four volumes.** The only two raw hits are adverbs, not objects (*ag goil thart a bhearrthadh*,
*ag goil thair a chéile*). Applying the ruling literally would replace one form the dialect does not
use with another form the dialect does not use.

What Connemara actually says in a plain statement is ***ag goil ag* + the plain verbal noun, with
the object AFTER it** — 56 instances, several carrying an object:

> *tá mise **ag goil ag ól é*** — vol II, speaker 85M
> *ag dul **ag inseacht an scéil seo*** — Éamann a Búrc
> *níl mé **ag goil ag codladh aon néal*** — vol III
> *ag goil **ag díonamh na hoibre** ceart* — 892M

And Ó Curnáin polices the distinction himself. At vol III §8.108 he singles out one deviation:
*"there is one example of ag goil aL for usual ag goil ag in: sagairt … **ag goil a chuir** ola ar
dhaoine"*. In other words, in exactly the environment the course needs, he calls the *a* form the
odd one out and *ag goil ag* the usual one.

**Four things #564 added that I did not have:**

1. **The zero is not a hole in the corpus.** The identical shape with a *different* head verb is
   common — *ag iarraidh* takes an object before the verbal noun about 19 times (*ag iarraidh
   chuile shórt a phlúchadh*, *ag iarraidh tada a dhéanamh*), and there is a bare declarative,
   *Bhíodar i ndan **Gaeilge a léabh** go leor acub* (11C). So object-before-verbal-noun is perfectly
   good Iorras Aithneach. It just never happens under *ag goil*. One corpus, one convention, opposite
   results — that is a syntactic fact, not a sampling accident. This is the strongest single piece of
   evidence in the whole job.
2. **My probe could have been lying and wasn't.** My pattern looked for a *visibly* lenited verbal
   noun — which would silently miss *rá* and *labhairt*, the course's own two verbs, because r- and
   l- show no lenition in spelling. #564 re-ran it four ways including those verbs explicitly. Still
   zero.
3. **The form genuinely means "going to", not "going somewhere to".** This was the thing that could
   have sunk the whole plan. Reading all 53 instances by hand: **33 near-future intention, 14 motion,
   6 undecidable** — and the ones that settle it have subjects that cannot walk anywhere: *dhá mbeadh
   **cogadh** ag goil ag tosaí* ("if a war were going to start"), *níl fhios ad **céard atá ag goil ag
   éireachtáil***, *ní raibh **ag eascainí** ag goil ag díonamh na hoibre ceart*. And with a person:
   ***tá mise ag goil ag labhairt leat anocht*** — 11C, the course's own verb, in the course's own
   frame.
4. **Eleven of those intention instances carry a post-verbal object**, which is exactly the shape the
   course would need: *tá mise **ag goil ag ól é***, *dhá mbeitheá **ag goil ag rá rud** leis*, *níl
   mé **ag goil ag codladh aon néal***, *ag dul **ag inseacht an scéil seo***.

So Connemara does have a way to say "I am going to". It is ***tá … ag goil ag* + plain verbal noun,
object after**. It is just not the shape the ruling names, and not a shape this course can currently
produce.

### Why that is a curriculum decision and not an edit

The corpus-correct form re-cuts the course's tiles. Today seed 5 teaches *I'm going to* = *tá mé
chun*, and it combines cleanly with the object-fronting tiles the learner already owns. Under
*ag goil ag*, "I'm going to speak Irish" becomes *tá mé ag goil ag labhairt Gaeilge* — and the tile
*Gaeilge a labhairt* has vanished from the sentence. The learner is taught one shape and shown
another.

I sorted all 89 affected rows by which frame they are in:

| Frame | Rows | Under *ag goil ag* |
|---|---|---|
| Object fronted before the softened verbal noun (*tá mé chun Gaeilge a labhairt*) | 46 | **breaks the tile** |
| The two teaching tiles plus their two component rows | 4 | **breaks the tile** |
| Verbal noun + preposition (*chun cuimhneamh ar an abairt ar fad*) | 10 | clean |
| Bare verbal noun, no object (*chun labhairt*, *chun críochnú*) | 15 | clean |
| "what's going to happen" (*céard atá chun tarlú*) | 10 | clean, and directly attested |
| *a bheith ag caint* | 1 | clean |
| "I'm going to **go**" (*tá mé chun dul*) | 3 | **needs a different frame entirely** — see below |

Half the exposure is in the frame that breaks.

### "I'm going to go" needs its own answer

*ag goil ag goil* is not a construction — the one string match in the corpus is a duplicated
typographic line in the vol IV glossary, not a double verbal noun. #564's answer is
***tá mé le dhul***, which Ó Curnáin glosses as intention in his own words: *"an raibh tusa **le
ghoil** ag an Aifreann, a Bhraidhean?"* — "were / are you intending to go to Mass?" There are 38
tokens of *le dhul / le ghoil*. The cost is that it puts a *second* "going to" frame into the
course — which is honest, because Connemara has two, and the *goil + goil* collision is precisely
why.

### A finding from #564 that is outside my remit but you should see tonight

**Seed 5's own teaching tile may be built on sand.** *cleachtadh* occurs 15 times in Iorras
Aithneach and is **only ever a noun** — "practice, habit" (*tá cleachtadh mhaith am air*). Transitive
"to practise something" is unattested. So *labhairt a chleachtadh*, "to practise speaking", the tile
at the heart of seed 5, has no Connemara model at all.

Worse, and larger: **"speak Irish" has no attested collocation in either word order.** *Gaeilge a
labhairt* = 0, *ag labhairt Gaeilge* = 0, *ag caint Gaeilge* = 0, *Gaeilge a chaint* = 0. The course's
single most central sentence has no Iorras Aithneach model whichever way you turn it. That is a gap
in the corpus rather than a verdict against the sentence — but it means that even option 1 below
rests on frame-attestation plus a general rule, not on a sentence anyone can quote. I am not acting
on either of these. They are yours, and they are bigger than tonight's two rulings.

### And I cannot do the clean half only

That was my first instinct and it is wrong. If the ten "what's going to happen" rows move to
*céard atá ag goil ag tarlú* — which is beautifully attested, the corpus has *céard atá ag goil ag
éireachtáil* and *céard atá ag goil ag tarláil* almost verbatim — while the 46 tile rows stay on
*chun*, then the English "going to" points at two different Irish answers in one course. That is
the exact defect we must never create.

**And the ZUT checker will not catch it.** I ran it on that partial state and it returns zero,
because it compares identical English strings and "I'm going to speak" and "I'm going to speak
Irish" are different strings. The collision is in the teaching, where the checker cannot see. I am
reporting it rather than trusting the green light.

### What I would need in order to finish this

One decision from you, on the sentences:

1. **Take the corpus form and re-cut the tile.** *I'm going to* = ***tá mé ag goil ag***, and
   "I'm going to speak Irish" becomes ***tá mé ag goil ag labhairt Gaeilge***. This is the
   defensible Connemara. It costs you the clean join between the "I'm going to" tile and every
   object-fronting verb tile from seed 5 onward — 46 rows change shape, not just wording — and
   seed 25 needs *tá mé le dhul* for "I'm going to go".
2. **Keep *chun* for now** and revisit when a native speaker can be put in front of it — the
   counter-argument you already weighed and rejected, restated only because the replacement turned
   out to be more expensive than it looked.
3. **Something I have not thought of** — this is the point at which a Connemara ear is worth more
   than another thousand corpus queries, especially now that *labhairt a chleachtadh* and
   *Gaeilge a labhairt* are themselves unattested.

Option 3 is what I would actually ask for. If you want movement tonight without a speaker, option 1
is the honest one and I can do it in a single pass — but it is a curriculum change to seed 5, and I
would want you to have said so in those words first.

  shape 19 times) — an absence with a positive control is worth much more than a bare absence.
