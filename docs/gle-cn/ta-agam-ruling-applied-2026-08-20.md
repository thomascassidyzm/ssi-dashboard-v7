# "I have Irish" — Kai's ruling applied to the Connemara course

**20 August 2026.** Course: Connemara Irish for English speakers (draft, hidden, no learner can reach it).
The released Irish course was not touched.

**Zero audio. Zero spend.** The Connemara course had **0 audio clips before this work and 0 after** —
checked directly, both times. Nothing was recorded, nothing was queued, nothing was deleted.

---

## What Kai ruled, and what I did with it

Four sentences in the course used the present tense of the verb *labhair* — "I speak", "you speak",
"do you speak", "who speak". Kai's ruling: three of them move to the possessive idiom
**tá … agam**, and the English side moves with them, translated literally into Hiberno-English —
*"I have Irish"*, not *"I speak Irish"*. The fourth does not move, because it is genuinely about
habit and "do you have Irish all day" is nonsense.

Kai was explicit that this is **not an error correction**. The old form was methodologically sound
and a native speaker had passed it. It changes because every learner of Irish has already been told,
by everyone, that you say *tá Gaeilge agam* — and a course that contradicts what they were taught
loses their trust before it can teach them anything.

## The four sentences, before and after

### Seed 9 — moved

| | English | Irish |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** | I speak a little Irish now | Labhraím beagán Gaeilge anois |
| **After** | I have a little Irish now | Tá beagán Gaeilge agam anois |

### Seed 13 — moved

| | English | Irish |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** | you speak Irish very well | Labhraíonn tú Gaeilge go han-mhaith |
| **After** | you have very good Irish | Tá Gaeilge mhaith agat |

### Seed 22 — moved

| | English | Irish |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** | because I want to meet people who speak Irish | Mar tá mé ag iarraidh bualadh le daoine a labhraíonn Gaeilge |
| **After** | because I want to meet people who have Irish | Mar tá mé ag iarraidh bualadh le daoine a bhfuil Gaeilge acu |

### Seed 14 — did NOT move, as ruled

| | English | Irish |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** | do you speak Irish all day? | An labhraíonn tú Gaeilge an lá ar fad? |
| **After** | do you speak Irish all day? *(unchanged)* | An mbíonn tú ag caint i nGaeilge ar feadh an lae? |

This one is a real habit, so it takes the dialect's habitual verb *bíonn* and the dialect's word for
talking, *caint*. The English stays about speaking, exactly as ruled. Confidence is high on *bíonn*
and on *i nGaeilge*; *ar feadh an lae* is a best attempt. Two forms that are simply absent from
2,700 pages of Connemara — *as Gaeilge* and *an lá ar fad* — are now at **zero occurrences in the
whole course**, where before this pass *an lá ar fad* appeared once, in this very sentence.

## The "very" at seed 13 — the judgement Kai left me

I kept Kai's English exactly as he wrote it: **"you have very good Irish"**, against
**Tá Gaeilge mhaith agat**. So "very" is carried on the English side with no separate Irish word
behind it.

Why: *Gaeilge mhaith* is what Connemara speakers actually say, and *an-mhaith* in this frame is
unattested. Dropping "very" from the English would have made a tidier one-to-one match, but it
would also have made the sentence read like faint praise, which is not what the sentence is for.

The cost, stated plainly so nobody trips over it later: when seed 13 is eventually broken into
teaching tiles, **"very good Irish" must be a single tile against "Gaeilge mhaith"**. If anyone
tiles it as "very" + "good" + "Irish", the course will be teaching that "very good" means *mhaith*,
and that will collide the moment *an-mhaith* is taught properly.

## The curriculum work at seed 9 — what I split, and why

This was the part that was more than a text edit.

**Before**, seed 9 taught two things: the verb *labhraím* ("I speak") and the pair *beagán Gaeilge*
("a little Irish"). The verb carried the whole meaning.

**After**, following Kai's instruction that "I have Irish" is one teaching unit and "a little" is a
separate unit introduced afterwards, seed 9 teaches:

1. **"I have Irish" → Tá Gaeilge agam** — taught as one whole, indivisible piece.
2. **"a little" → beagán** — taught afterwards, on its own.

**No bare frames**, as Kai required. The learner is never shown *tá … agam* as a pattern with a hole
in it. Unit 1 is a complete, sayable sentence and nothing less.

And there is no competing way to say the same thing at seed 9. The course's existing "to speak
Irish" (*Gaeilge a labhairt*) is a different meaning — an action, not a proficiency — so the two do
not compete. The other candidate, *bíonn mé ag caint*, is deliberately **not** introduced here; it
arrives later, at seed 14, where it belongs.

## Three things that surprised me, and one I could not do

**1. The new sentence is a conversational dead end at seed 9, and that is why unit 1 has only two
practice lines.** I expected to write eight. *Tá Gaeilge agam* is a complete clause, and the only
thing the learner has been given that can attach to a complete clause is "now". Everything else in
seeds 1–8 — wanting, trying, going to, how to, after you — attaches to *actions*, and possession is
not an action. There is no "and" yet, no "because" yet, no way to embed one clause in another yet.
So the construction gets its real drilling through unit 2's sentences, four of which repeat
*tá … agam* in longer company. I would rather report that honestly than pad the list with sentences
a Connemara speaker would not say.

**2. The two units interlock rather than sitting side by side.** In "Tá beagán Gaeilge agam anois",
the word *beagán* lands **inside** unit 1 — between *Tá* and *Gaeilge agam*. The English does the
same thing ("I have **a little** Irish"), so it is not a fault in the Irish; it is just the shape of
the sentence. Worth knowing because it means the tiles for seed 9 are not simply laid end to end,
and anyone building the on-screen tile display for this seed needs to expect that.

**3. Seed 9 is marked "released" while everything under it is marked "draft".** Inside a course
nobody can open, this is harmless — and with no audio anywhere there was nothing to break. But it is
an inconsistency someone should tidy before this course goes anywhere near a learner.

**The gap — stated as a gap, not worked around.** The brief asked for practice to be rebuilt on all
four seeds. **It could only be rebuilt on seed 9, because seeds 13, 14 and 22 have no teaching tiles
and no practice sentences at all yet** — they are still one-line shells awaiting decomposition. So on
those three, the sentence is now correct and the *tá … agam* idiom is properly introduced before them
at seed 9, but there was no practice there to rebuild. When they are decomposed, seeds 13 and 22
should treat the idiom as revision, not as new material — and seed 13 will need to introduce *agat*
and seed 22 *acu*, which the learner will not have met before.
