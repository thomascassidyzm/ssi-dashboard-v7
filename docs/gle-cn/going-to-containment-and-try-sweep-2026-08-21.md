A learner meets one construction at seed 5, drills it for seventy seeds, and meets a different one

- seed 79 — *when are you going to learn?* → `cén uair a bhfuil tú **chun** foghlaim?`
- seed 81 — *are you going to start?* → `an bhfuil tú **chun** tosú?`
- seed 82 — *I'm not going to speak* → `níl mé **ag goil a** labhairt`

**This is not a defect any single band can fix.** The repair is upstream at seeds 5 and 25 and means
re-teaching the spine. That is Kai's call and it is queued.

## 2. The containment rule, until he rules

1. **Do NOT introduce `ag goil a` into any seed that does not already carry it.** Every new instance
   makes the eventual repair bigger and the contradiction worse for the learner.
2. **Where a seed's audited translation already carries the OLD form, decompose it as written and
   annotate it. Do not rewrite it.**
3. **Keep annotating both forms** so the sweep is surgical and sized.

### Why rule 2 is not a compromise

A worker who "corrected" seeds 79 and 81 to `ag goil a` would have handed the learner a sentence
they **cannot produce**: the new form is not taught until seed 82. **The governing test decides,
always** —

> *Could a learner holding ONLY what the course has taught so far produce this sentence from the
> English prompt alone?*

A form that is more "correct" but untaught **fails that test**. Consistency with what the learner
has actually been taught beats consistency with a ruling that has not reached the spine yet. The
worker who left 79 and 81 alone was right.

## 5. A gap in the `cabhrú` repair list, for job #635

A `cabhr*` pattern **does not match `cabhair`**, and two decomposed seeds carry it:

- seed **212** — *they wanted to ask for help* → `Bhíodar ag iarraidh **cabhair** a iarraidh`
- seed **231** — *I know an old man who wanted to ask for help* → `… ag iarraidh **cabhair** a iarraidh`

Both are live legos (`cabhair a iarraidh`). **If the twelve-seed repair list was built from a
`cabhr*` pattern, these two are missing from it.**

**They are a different and milder question, and should not be lumped in.** `cabhair` is the *noun*
and is attested 11 times in Ó Curnáin against `cúnamh` 42 — it is not the forbidden `cabhrú`
(verbal noun, 1 hit). The issue at 212/231 is ZUT rather than dialect: the English word *help* is
`cúnamh` at seeds 25, 74 and 161, and `cabhair` at 212 and 231. Worth a decision, not an emergency.
