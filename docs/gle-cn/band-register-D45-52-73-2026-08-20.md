## Conflict 3 — seed 47's `I think` tile, and a checker that changed underneath us

**This finding was DOWNGRADED mid-run and the correction matters more than the original claim.**

At 23:11 the 50-seed checkpoint flagged **8 phrases at seed 47** as R5 breaches: they teach
`tá mé ag cheapadh go labhrófá Gaeilge liom` under *"I think **you'd** speak Irish with me"*, and
`you'd` appeared in no taught gloss. Acting on that, this band snapshotted seed 47 into
`seed_redo_snapshots`, unbanked it, and attempted three repairs. **All three are structurally
illegal**, and that part of the finding stands:

1. **Merging the two tiles** into `tá mé ag cheapadh go bhfuil sé go maith` is rejected by the
   endpoint: **11 syllables against an 8-syllable lego cap**.
2. **Letting L1's phrases use L2's chunk** is refused — a lego may use chunks from *earlier* legos
   of its own seed, never later ones. All eight phrases came back untileable.
3. **Any other complement**: there is none. Of every chunk taught before seed 47, only
   `go labhrófá` (S15) and seed 47's own L2 can grammatically follow `ag cheapadh`.

Seed 47 was then **restored byte-for-byte** — 3 legos, 24 phrases, all 8 original lines present and
verified by reading them back out of the database.

**Then the ground moved.** At 23:23 another session fixed `vocab.cjs`: an Irish demutation rule was
being applied to the *English* side, rewriting `think`→`tink`, `thank`→`tank`, `she`→`se`, and it
never expanded contractions. The corrected tokenizer reuses the submit endpoint's own expansion, so
`you'd` now resolves to `you` + `would` — **both taught** (`would` via *"I wouldn't like"* at S12).
The checkpoint over 45–73 now passes clean.

So **the word-level R5 breach was an artefact of a broken checker and is gone.** The unbank/repost
was, in hindsight, unnecessary — it cost nothing because the restore was faithful, but it was
motivated by a false positive.

**What survives is milder and still worth a look:** the same target `go labhrófá` now carries two
English glosses — *"you to speak"* at S15 and *"you'd speak"* at S47. That is a gloss-consistency
question for Kai, not a vocabulary breach, and it is **not urgent**.

**What survives and IS urgent is the structural constraint**, because worker #610 hit the identical
dead end independently at seed 72: a tile whose only legal complement is its own seed's later tile
can never reach the required phrase count, and the 8-syllable cap blocks the obvious merge.
**Neither local pre-checker enforces the syllable cap — only the live endpoint does.**

Confidence: **confident**, having verified the tokenizer change and re-read seed 47 out of the
database after restoring it.
