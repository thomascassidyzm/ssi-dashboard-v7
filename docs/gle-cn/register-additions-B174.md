## Two things the coordinator should decide, not me

**1. `agam le` + verbal noun, and whether the verbal noun lenites.**
I wrote `ní raibh am agam le cabhrú` (and `le teacht ar ais`, `le labhairt a chleachtadh`,
`le Gaeilge a labhairt`, `le tú a fheiceáil`) — 8 phrases at S178.
`am agam le` is **0** in the corpus, but the construction itself is attested with a different noun,
vol III: *"cén bealach atá agam le dhul ann"*. Note that example **lenites the verbal noun after
`le`** (`le dhul`, not `le dul`). If that lenition generalises, my forms should be `le chabhrú`,
`le theacht ar ais`. I could not establish that it generalises from one hit, so I left them
unlenited and am flagging it rather than guessing. **This is the first item to put in front of a
Connemara speaker.**

**2. `a thuiscint` vs `tuiscint` — a ZUT-adjacent split I was forced into.**
S74 already maps *"to understand"* → `tuiscint` (bare, no object: *"tá mé ag iarraidh tuiscint"*).
S174's own sentence needs the **object-fronted** form, `an rud atá mé a rá a thuiscint`. The ZUT
gate rejected reusing the gloss *"to understand"*, and the whole clause is 9 syllables, over the
8-syllable lego cap. So S174 L2 is glossed **`understand` → `a thuiscint`**, sitting alongside
S74's **`to understand` → `tuiscint`**.
The Irish is right in both places and the distinction is real (it is the same distinction as
`labhairt` vs `Gaeilge a labhairt`, which this course already makes). What is weak is the *gloss*:
a bare `understand` is a thin learner prompt. If the coordinator has a house convention for
labelling object-fronted infinitives, S174 L2 should be renamed to it.
