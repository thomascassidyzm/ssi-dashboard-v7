## The "how to" instances — **two**

Both were unavoidable: the English embeds the clause and there is no natural rewrite that keeps the
sentence honest.

| seed | English | what I wrote |
|---|---|---|
| 202 | nobody was sure **how to** answer the question | Ní raibh duine ar bith cinnte **cén chaoi** a dtabharfadh sé freagra ar an gceist |
| 208 | I didn't want to ask you **how to** say it | Ní raibh mé ag iarraidh fiafraí díot **cén chaoi** a ndéarfainn é |

Both use `cén chaoi` with **no `le`**, and both take a particle + finite verb rather than a bare
verbal noun — which is the shape Ó Curnáin's corpus actually supports (48 of 48). 202 is the
register's own worked sentence, reproduced exactly. Neither uses any present tense of `labhair`;
208 uses the conditional `ndéarfainn`, from `abair`, not `labhair`.

## What a Connemara speaker should be shown first

In this order.

1. **Seed 214 — "Did you have a good time at the weekend?"** → *An raibh am maith agat ag an
   deireadh seachtaine?* This is a calque on the English and I know it. The natural Connemara is
   probably *ar bhain tú sult as…* — but `baint taitnimh as` is already spent on *enjoy* (seed 206),
   and reusing it would merge two different English prompts into one Irish form. **I chose the
   awkward calque over the invisible merge.** Ask a speaker how they'd really say it.
2. **Seed 236 — "…she was going to try to help"** → *…go raibh sí ag goil a dhéanamh iarracht
   cabhrú.* Longest and heaviest thing I wrote. The new "going to" ruling and the object-fronting
   the course uses everywhere else pull against each other here; see the structural note in my
   register-additions file.
3. **Seed 233 — "your sister"** → *do dheirfiúr.* Not my call — the register already flags
   `deirfiúr` as its one unsettled kinship term. Worth putting in front of the same speaker while
   they're looking at 234, which has *do dhreatháir* (brother) in the matching slot. **Ask for the
   pair together.**
4. **Seed 248 — "complete rubbish"** → *go dona ar fad.* Plainer than the English. If Connemara has
   a proper dismissive here, this is where to get it.
5. **Seed 212 — "they wanted to ask for help"** → *Bhíodar ag iarraidh cabhair a iarraidh.* Same
   word twice in two senses, in one short sentence. The register predicted this exact seed and ruled
   it deliberately. Ask whether a speaker would restructure it entirely.

## Two objections I am filing rather than acting on

Both places where I used the register's Irish anyway, as the rules require.

**Seed 203 — "what would you do if I asked you to help me?"** The register gives
*Céard a dhéanfá dá bhfiafróinn díot cabhrú liom?* and I wrote it. But `fiafraigh de` is *ask a
question of somebody*; *ask somebody to do something* is `iarr ar`. The register's own sentence
mixes them. I did not fix it, because a course that is consistently wrong in one spot is repairable
and an inconsistent one is not. Flagging it so it can be swept.

**Seed 225 — "he would give you an answer if he could."** The register's worked example writes
`freagra **dhuit**`, but §18.1 of the same document instructs translation workers, in bold, to write
`duit`. Those contradict each other. I followed the instruction (`duit`), not the example, on the
grounds that the instruction is addressed to me and the example is illustration. **This is worth a
five-second confirmation** — if it goes the other way, §18.1 is the sweep that turns the whole
course Carna, and it is cheap while there is no audio.

## Things that surprised me

**The `ag iarraidh` collision is worse than the register warned.** The register flags seed 212. It
does not flag **seed 222** — *"he's trying to tell me what he wants"* — which comes out as
*Tá sé ag iarraidh inseacht dom céard atá sé ag iarraidh*: the same two words, twice, inside eleven
words of Irish, in two different senses (*trying* and *wants*). That is not a ZUT break — each
English string still maps to exactly one Irish — but a learner meeting it will think something has
gone wrong with the page. It is a direct consequence of seed 1 and seed 2 both banking `ag iarraidh`,
and it is now load-bearing, so I have not touched it. **Add 222 to the same review list as 212.**

**The new "going to" ruling has a word-order consequence nobody wrote down.** `ag goil a` + lenited
verbal noun cannot take a fronted object, because its own `a` already fills the particle slot that
the fronting needs. So the course's default shape (`Gaeilge a labhairt`) inverts inside this one
frame. Three of my seeds hit it — 227, 236, 243. I decided it consistently and filed the decision
rather than making it silently; details in `register-additions-T4.md` §3.

**Seed 207 came out heavier than its English.** *"You've done what you needed to do"* is seven
plain words; the Irish is *Tá an rud a bhí gá duit a dhéanamh déanta agat*, with `déan` appearing
twice. It is correct, it uses only forms the course has, and I could not make it lighter without
changing the English, which is not mine to change.

**The register did more of the work than I expected.** Its worked examples covered eleven of my
fifty-three sentences verbatim, and its lexis decided most of the rest. The parts
where I had to invent something are the parts where the English left the register's territory —
jobs, employers, football, Sunday — and those are all now filed as additions.
