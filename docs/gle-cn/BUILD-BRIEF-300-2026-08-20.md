## 2. The seven binding rulings

These are Kai's own, all made 20 August 2026. **None of them is open. Re-litigating one wastes the
night.** If your instinct disagrees with one, your instinct is overruled — say so in your report and
move on.

**R1 — Dialect as actually spoken beats standard, always.** "Not in the standard dictionary" is
**not** an objection: learners google the form and find discussion of it. Already settled and in the
course: `amáireach` (not `amárach`), `eicínt` (not `éigin`), `chuile`, `céard`, `tá muid` (not
`táimid`), `aríst` when *again* first appears, `i nGaeilge` (**never** `as Gaeilge`).

**R2 — No bare frames.** Never teach a pattern with a slot in it. No `"I want to ___"`. Whole
sentences that happen to contain the structure, always in context.

**R3 — One new unit at a time.** *"I have Irish"* is one unit; *"a little"* is a separate one
afterwards. Do not introduce two new things in one tile.

**R4 — Hiberno-English on the known side where it teaches the structure.** *"I have Irish"*,
*"people who have Irish"*, *"you have very good Irish"*. The English is doing teaching work — do
**not** smooth it into standard English. But it must still be **grammatical and natural**. Rare is
fine; stilted is not. If you cannot write it naturally, write the standard English instead and flag
it.

**R5 — The learner knows only what the course has taught them.** Never use a word in a practice
sentence before it has been introduced. **This is the single most important rule in the build.
Check it on every phrase you write.** Section 5 tells you how.

**R6 — `labhairt`, the verbal noun, is correct and stays.** Only the *finite present tense* of
`labhair` was ever wrong (`labhraím`, `labhraíonn`). **Never reintroduce it anywhere.** The verbal
noun, the past, the future, the conditional (`go labhrófá`) are all fine.

**R7 — Seed 13: "very good Irish" is ONE tile.** Never split into *very* + *good*. Seed 13
introduces `agat`; seed 22 introduces `acu`.

## 3. The methodology rails

- **ZUT — one known prompt maps to exactly one target form.** If the same English appears twice in
  the course it gets the same Irish, every time. This is why §4 exists and why it is binding.
- **The known side is a controlled language too.** Never use an English word or structure the
  learner has not been given yet. Hiberno-English under R4 is an *addition* to this rule, not an
  exemption from it.
- **Vocabulary is `known` / `target` / `seed`.** Never say "source".
- **Quality over throughput.** This is craftsmanship. A short honest build beats 300 padded seeds,
  and Kai has said so in writing. If you are running out of night, **stop and report** — do not
  thin the work to hit a number.

## 4. Naming your authority, and labelling your confidence

**Never normalise by feel.** For any non-obvious choice, name the authority:

- **Ó Dónaill, *Foclóir Gaeilge–Béarla* (1977)** — the standard, via teanglann.ie. This is also the
  dictionary a learner of this course will actually own and look words up in.
- **Ó Curnáin, *The Irish of Iorras Aithneach, County Galway* (DIAS 2007)** — four volumes on the
  Carna dialect, i.e. deep mainland Connemara. This is our attestation source for what Connemara
  actually says.
  - **His `*` means "non-attested, or impermissible/very doubtful".** An asterisked form is his own
    statement that the dialect does not have it. That is stronger than any frequency count.
  - ⚠️ **Volumes I–III cannot be searched with `grep`** — their phonetic transcription extracts as
    control bytes, so grep calls them binary and prints "Binary file matches" instead of counting.
    A zero from grep on those volumes is **false**. Read them in Python; use
    `tools/gle-cn/ocurnain-probe.py`, which refuses to be trusted until its controls pass.
  - **A bare zero is not evidence.** The noise floor on this corpus is real: ordinary words like
    `deirim`, `ólaim`, `feicim` score between 2 and 9 in ~2,700 pages, and `ceapaim` scores 0 only
    because the index abbreviates it `~aim`. Do not build a ruling on a single zero.

**Every non-obvious sentence you write carries a confidence label, honestly:**

- **confident** — attested, or the dictionary's own frame.
- **best attempt** — regular and not contradicted, but not directly attested.
- **genuinely uncertain** — you are guessing at the wording and a speaker should look at it.

> **"This needs a native speaker" is NOT an acceptable terminal answer.** We have none, and Kai has
> ruled that we attempt it and flag it. Write the sentence, label it, move on.

## 5. Rule 5 in practice — how to actually check "no word before its introduction"

This is where builds fail, so here is the mechanic.

The learner's known vocabulary at seed N is **every `known_text`, every component `known`, and every
practice-phrase `known_text` from seeds 1 … N-1**, plus the new tiles of seed N itself. Same on the
target side.

**Before you write practice phrases for a seed, pull the live vocabulary as of that seed:**

```
node scripts/gle-cn/vocab.cjs <seed_number>
```

That prints the exact known-side and target-side word lists the learner has met. **Every content
word in every practice sentence you write must appear in that list, or be a new tile of the seed you
are building.** Function words that ride inside a tile you are introducing are fine; a new content
word is not.

Two traps specific to Irish, both of which have already bitten this build:

- **Initial mutation breaks naive word-matching.** `focal` → `fhocal`, `Gaeilge` → `nGaeilge`,
  `duine` → `dhuine`, `maith` → `mhaith`. The checker strips these, but *you* must not be fooled
  into thinking a mutated form is a new word — or into thinking a genuinely new word is old because
  it looks like one you know.
- **Do not smuggle vocabulary in through the English.** A practice sentence whose Irish is entirely
  known but whose English introduces *"however"* still breaks R5.

## 6. The translation-choice register — ZUT across parallel workers

Multiple workers are translating disjoint bands of seeds **at the same time**. If two of them
independently pick different Irish for the same English, ZUT is broken and the break is invisible
until it reaches a learner.

**So the recurring structures are decided once, centrally, and written down in**
`docs/gle-cn/translation-register-2026-08-20.md`.

**Rules for every translation worker:**

1. **Read the register before you translate a single seed.** If your English is in it, you use the
   register's Irish. No exceptions, no improvements.
2. **If your English is *not* in the register and you judge it will recur** — any common verb,
   modal, connector or time expression — **do not just pick one.** Add it to the register in the
   same commit, with your authority and confidence, so the worker on the next band inherits it.
3. **Never silently diverge.** If the register's choice looks wrong to you, use it anyway and file
   the objection in your report. A consistent course that is wrong in one place is fixable; an
   inconsistent one is not.

## 7. How to submit — the only sanctioned write paths

The course-builder API is **already running on `http://localhost:3471`**. Do not start another copy;
port 3471 is taken and a second one dies with `EADDRINUSE`.

**Translation** — one call per seed:

```
curl -s -X PATCH http://localhost:3471/api/seed/gle_cn_for_eng/<seed_number> \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"target_text":"<your Irish>"}'
```

**Decomposition + practice phrases** — markdown, one call per seed, atomic:

```
curl -s -X POST http://localhost:3471/api/seed/complete?course=gle_cn_for_eng \
  -H 'Content-Type: text/markdown' --data-binary @seed042.md
```

The endpoint validates tiling, ZUT, vocabulary and phrase counts **atomically** and assigns phrase
IDs deterministically. **Never set a phrase ID yourself.** If it rejects your seed, the rejection is
information — read it and fix the content. Do not work around it by writing to the database directly.

The markdown format, exactly:

```
# Seed 42
Known: I don't need to know everything
Target: Ní gá dom chuile shórt a bheith ar eolas agam

## L1 [M] "I don't need" → "ní gá dom"
Components: I don't → ní, need → gá dom

BUILD:
- I don't need to speak → ní gá dom labhairt
- I don't need to learn → ní gá dom foghlaim

USE:
- I don't need to speak Irish now → Ní gá dom Gaeilge a labhairt anois [8]
- I don't need to learn a word today → Ní gá dom focal a fhoghlaim inniu [8]
```

`[M]` is a multi-word tile with components, `[A]` is atomic. The number in `[7]` on a USE line is the
target syllable count. **BUILD lines are stepping stones; USE lines are whole, natural sentences** —
and under R2 every one of them is a real sentence, never a frame with a hole in it.

## 8. Checkpoints — every 50 seeds, stop and check

This is a long unattended run and Kai wants it watched. **At each 50-seed boundary, stop before
continuing** and verify all five:

- **(a)** no word used before it was introduced (R5) — run the checker, do not eyeball it
- **(b)** no bare frames (R2)
- **(c)** dialect forms consistent — `amáireach`, `eicínt`, `chuile`, `céard`, `tá muid`, `i nGaeilge`,
  and **no finite present of `labhair` anywhere** (R1, R6)
- **(d)** the English reads naturally to a native English speaker (R4)
- **(e)** still **zero** rows in `course_audio`

```
node scripts/gle-cn/checkpoint.cjs <from_seed> <to_seed>
```

**If a checkpoint fails, fix it before continuing.** Report the result briefly as you go, so a fault
is caught at seed 100 and not at seed 300.
