## 2. The sweep was keyed on the English, as instructed

Every row was admitted only if **its English contained *try / tried / trying* AND its Irish
contained *iarracht***, and the rewrite was refused if the retired word survived it. Nothing was
matched on *iarraidh* at any point.

That is not ceremony. This phrase was in the set and shows why:

> *I'm not happy any more since we tried **but I don't want to stop***
> → `níl mé sásta níos mó ó **d'iarr muid** ach níl mé **ag iarraidh** stopadh`

The same string *ag iarraidh* sits in the second half meaning **want**, and it was left untouched
while the try-form in the first half changed. A sweep keyed on the Irish would have rewritten it
and nothing downstream would have complained.

## 3. What changed

**Seed 8** — *I'm going to try to explain what I mean*
→ `Tá mé chun **iarraidh** a mhíniú céard atá i gceist agam`

**Seed 146** — *nothing seems to be working since we tried to fix it*
→ `Is cosúil nach bhfuil tada ag obair ó **d'iarr muid** é a dheisiú`

**Two building blocks** — *to try* → **`iarraidh`**, and *since we tried* → **`ó d'iarr muid`**.

**Twenty-five practice phrases** across seeds 8, 25, 146, 190 and 191. Seeds 190 and 191 landed on
the bare form the ruling predicted for the *mind trying to…* frame —
`an miste leat **iarraidh** cuimhneamh?`

Seeds 2, 6 and 7 were not touched, as ruled. `ag triail` and `traíáil` are still at **zero** in the
course. Seed 7's *mo dhícheall a dhéanamh* stays and stops counting as a try-word.

## 4. The clash check

**Run with the checker as it stands, not widened.** The rail is one known prompt → one target; two
prompts sharing one target is allowed and is what the language does.

- **Both changed building blocks: no conflict.** Neither *to try* nor *since we tried* has any
  competing target anywhere in the course.
- **No phrase-level collision was created** on any seed the sweep touched.
- **Two prompts now share `iarraidh`** — *to try* (seed 8) and *to ask for* (seed 243). Allowed,
  and it is exactly the merge the ruling accepted with its eyes open. Recorded, not fixed.

**One pre-existing split sits on a seed the sweep touched, and was deliberately left alone.** The

It is one of the three splits already on the books. The sweep's edit at seed 25 was to *"are you
going to try"*, a different prompt, so this was neither created nor repaired here. **It is still

For the record, a course-wide pass found **13** one-known-to-many-target splits in total, all

## 5. THE GAP: seed 236 still carries the retired word

**One seed, one building block, eight practice phrases were deliberately not changed.** The ruling
flagged this seed as "the one substitution I would not do mechanically". It was right, and there is
now evidence for *why* rather than a hunch.

> *I know someone who said that she was going to try to help*
> → `… go raibh sí **ag goil a dhéanamh iarracht** cabhrú`

The course renders *going to* here as **`ag goil a`**, which needs a lenited, consonant-initial
verbal noun after it — `ag goil a dhéanamh`, `ag goil a bheith`, `ag goil a tharlú`. **The try-word
begins with a vowel, and the course has no vowel-initial instance anywhere to copy.**

**Checked against the Connemara corpus, calibrated** (the three known-frequency controls all passed,
so the zeros below are real). *ag iarraidh* returns **173**, matching the ruling author's own count:

- **`ag goil ag` + verbal noun is the attested Connemara shape — 57 instances** (`ag goil ag rá`,
  `ag goil ag caint`, `ag goil ag imeacht`). But with the try-word that gives **`ag goil ag
  iarraidh`**, which is the *want* string with *ag goil* in front of it. Unusable.
- **`a d'` + a vowel-initial verb looked like the answer and is a homograph trap.** It returns 9
  hits and **every one is the past tense** — *céard **a d'ól** sé?*, "what did he drink?". It is the
  past-tense prefix, not a verbal-noun particle. Writing `ag goil a d'iarraidh` would produce
  something that reads as a finite past verb.
- **`ag goil` followed by the try-word is unattested in any shape** — zero for every spelling tried.

**And the other repair is blocked by a standing rule.** Rewriting the seed to the `chun` future —
which is what the released Irish course does — would push a seed that already carries `ag goil a`
back onto the older form, and the containment rule of 21 August forbids moving seeds between the two
*going to* constructions until the spine is ruled on.

**It also cannot be done as a text edit at all.** These eight phrases tile against building blocks
that themselves end in `ag goil a` — *that man is going to* and *that she was going to*. Changing
the phrase text alone would break the tiling. **Seed 236 needs re-decomposing, not sweeping** —
which is the same conclusion the ruling reached, and the building block is independently malformed
there anyway (it bundles *to help* into the try-word).

**So seed 236 is queued, not forgotten: 1 seed, 1 building block, 8 phrases still on `iarracht`.**
It needs Kai's spine ruling on *going to* first, and then a decomposition pass rather than an edit.

## 7. For the word register

The permanent cost the ruling named should travel with it: **in Connemara, want, trying and asking
for are now one Irish string.** No future audit can separate those senses by looking at the Irish.
**Every future check on this vocabulary must key on the English gloss.**
