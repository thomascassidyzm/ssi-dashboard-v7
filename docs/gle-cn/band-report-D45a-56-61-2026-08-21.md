## The checks

The build's own checkpoint passes for 56–61: no word used before it was introduced, no bare frames,
dialect forms consistent, and audio still zero. Two things it caught and this band fixed before
finishing:

- One practice sentence used the contraction *"you're"*, which the learner has not been given. Fixed
  to *"you are"*.
- One sentence in seed 60 used *"yet"* one unit before the seed introduces it. Rewritten.

Two things the offline pre-checker does **not** cover, worth passing to whoever maintains it:

- **The 8-syllable limit on a teaching unit is not checked offline** — it only fires on submission.
  It cost this band one rejected seed. A one-line addition would catch it.
- **The one-English-to-one-Irish check never looks forward.** It compares a new seed only against
  lower-numbered ones, so a clash with a higher-numbered seed that is already built passes silently.
  That is exactly how Conflict C below got in.

Beyond the automated checks: no preposition was split off as its own unit with an invented English
gloss anywhere in this band, and every unit appears in its own seed's sentence.

## The word for "try" — count is 2, and both are annotated

Confirmed by reading the English rather than assuming: **no seed in 56–61 asks for "try".** The band
register was right about that.

Two **practice sentences** this band wrote do use *"I'm trying"*, both at seed 56, both rendered with
the live course form `tá mé ag iarraidh`, exactly as instructed:

1. seed 56 — *"I'm trying to remember a word so I can say something in Irish"*
2. seed 56 — *"I'm trying to remember how to say something now"*

Both are annotated in the seed source. `ag iarracht` was never written anywhere.

**A warning for whoever runs the swap.** A sweep that mechanically replaces `ag iarraidh` will
destroy this band, because the same Irish is also the course's word for *want* and *wanted* —
including a whole teaching unit at seed 57, `an rud a bhí mé ag iarraidh` ("what I wanted"), and
dozens of *want* sentences. The swap has to be driven from the **English** side, not the Irish.

## Three findings, in the order a speaker should be shown them

**1. "How to" is now two different Irish structures in one course.** Seeds 56, 57, 59 and 60 were
translated with one form; 36 practice sentences already live at seeds 3–13 use a different one. Both
were already there before this band; both are unattested in the corpus. Every instance this band
touched is marked so a sweep can be surgical. **This needs a translation-side decision, and it is
already sitting with Kai as an open call.** Show a Connemara speaker these two sentences side by
side first — one from each pattern — and ask which one a person from Carna would actually say.

**2. "Enough words" hides a possessive that must agree with the subject.** Seed 58's Irish literally
says *"your fill of words"*, and the corpus confirms the possessive tracks the subject. Every
practice sentence in that unit was deliberately written with *you* as the subject. Anyone later
rewriting them to *I* must change the possessive too, or the Irish becomes wrong in a way no check
will catch.

**3. "I don't know" now maps to two Irish forms.** Seed 60 teaches the fact-knowing one; seed 85,
already built, uses the same English for the person-knowing one. Both Irish forms are right for their
own sentence — the English gloss is the defect. Seed 85 is outside this band, so it was reported
rather than edited. The suggested repair is in the register file.

## What surprised me

The forward-blindness of the consistency check. It is easy to read a clean pre-check as "this seed is
consistent with the course", and it is not — it means "consistent with the seeds below it". With
several workers building non-contiguous bands at once, every band above yours is invisible to you.
Finding 3 arrived that way and only because a hand-written probe went looking for it. That is worth a
single course-wide sweep at the end of the night, and it will be cheap: one pass over the English
glosses looking for any that map to more than one Irish.
