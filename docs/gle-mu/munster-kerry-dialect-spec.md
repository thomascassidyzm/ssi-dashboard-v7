# Munster (Kerry) Irish — dialect spec for `gle_mu_for_eng`

**Set 2026-08-20, BEFORE the first seed was translated.** Corca Dhuibhne / West Kerry.

Evidence base: the corpas.ie **CNG** corpus, split by dialect-diagnostic source, where
`RnaG (An Saol ó Dheas)` — RTÉ RnaG's southern desk at Baile na nGall, Corca Dhuibhne — is the
primary witness of transcribed Kerry speech, measured against `RnaG (Iris Aniar)` (spoken
Connemara) and `RnaG (Barrscéalta)` / `An tUltach` (Ulster). Plus **Diarmuid Ó Sé, *Gaeilge Chorca
Dhuibhne* (ITÉ 2000)**, full text, consulted directly for the verb morphology.

All counts below are **MU spoken : CO spoken** unless marked. The two spoken sources are the same
size to within ~10%, so raw counts are directly comparable.

---

## 0. The governing line

> **Orthography = normal written Irish that a Kerry reader recognises as their own. Grammar, lexis
> and verb morphology = Corca Dhuibhne as actually spoken.**

Kai's rail (2026-08-20) binds: **the dialect form as actually spoken beats the standard spelling,
always**; the "a learner can't look it up" objection is REJECTED. But we draw the line at narrow
phonetic transcription. So:

**IN** (dialect forms, normal orthography): `táim`, `bhíos`, `dhein`, `raghaidh`, `chíonn`, `dúrt`,
`ansan`, `anso`, `fé`, `cad`, `conas`, `faic`, `ana-`, `sara`, `ná fuil`, `Gaelainn`, `inniubh`.

**OUT** (transcriber's phonetics, not orthography): `thá` for `tá` · `a' d'iarraidh` for
`ag iarraidh` · `n'` for `ní` · `a'` for `ag` · `deunamh` · `conus`.
Rationale: `thá` is 7,558 hits in the Kerry spoken source but **0** in *Paróiste Baile Mhuirne* and
3 in *Cork Irish* — written Munster writes `tá`. It is aspiration, not spelling.

**Also OUT — the preverbal past `do`.** Authentically Munster, authentically marginal: the Kerry
source writes it on **2–7%** of past-tense verbs (`do bhí` 460 / `bhí` 14,384). Writing `do chuas`
across 668 sentences would represent the dialect at ~20× its real rate.

### The four rules that fall out of it

1. **1pl is ALWAYS synthetic, every tense, no exception.** Munster has no `muid` — `tá muid` = **1
   hit** in the Kerry source against 1,189 in Connemara. Never `tá muid`, `bhí muid`, `beidh muid`,
   `bheadh muid`, `rinne muid`.
2. **1sg is synthetic everywhere EXCEPT the future.** `táim`, `nílim`, `bhíos`, `bheinn`, `bím` —
   but **`beidh mé`**, not `bead` (25:111 *within Kerry speech*). Ruling with the levelling here.
3. **3pl is synthetic in the PAST and CONDITIONAL only.** `bhíodar` 1179:39, `bheidís` 166:14 win;
   present `táid` 579:1051, future `beid` 7:306, habitual `bíd` 0:329 all lose.
4. **2sg present positive is `tánn tú`; 2sg present negative is `níl tú`.** Measured in opposite
   directions on the same corpus. Do not regularise them to each other. `tánn` is 337:0:0 —
   Munster-exclusive.

**Signature checks.** `dhein` **732 : 3** vs `rinne` 16 : 1,178 — a near-perfect discriminator; a
course writing `rinne` is not a Kerry course. Dependent form is **`ndein`** (49/0), never `ndearna`.
`raghaidh` **260 : 0** (Ó Sé §546: `rachaidh mé` survives only in songs). `chíonn`/`chím`/`chífidh`
beat `feiceann`/`feicim`/`feicfidh` 191:8, 104:6, 49:2 — **but** the non-finite forms stay `feic-`
(`feiscint`, `feicthe`), and the **dependent past 1sg is bare `ní fheaca`**, no ending.

**Rejected by measurement** (folk-expectations that the corpus overturns):
`bhéarfaidh` is **Ulster** (0/0/79) — Kerry future of *tabhair* is `tabharfaidh`. `airím` is
**Connacht** (2/26; `airigh` 3/162) — Kerry says `cloisim`/`chuala`. `in acmhainn` is Connacht-
leaning (8/44) — Kerry "able" is **`ábalta`** (551:13). The direct relative `-(e)anns`
(`deireanns`, `cheapanns`) is **Connacht only** — Ó Sé states it is not used in Corca Dhuibhne.

## 3. THE `ná` SYSTEM — the highest-leverage ruling in the spec

Touches **123 that-clauses + 155 negatives + every tag question**.

| pattern | MU | CO |
|---|---|---|
| **`ná fuil`** | **1161** | **1** |
| `nach bhfuil` | 209 | 1987 |
| **`ná raibh`** | **581** | 52 |
| `nach raibh` | 146 | 730 |
| **`ná beadh`** | **239** | 7 |

> **RULE 3.1.** The negative subordinator / negative relative particle is **`ná`**, never `nach`.
> **RULE 3.2 — AND IT DOES NOT ECLIPSE.** `ná` takes the **bare dependent form**: `ná fuil`,
> `ná raibh`, `ná beadh`. **Not** `ná bhfuil` (40 hits, 3% — noise). This is the mechanical trap: a
> translator who knows "Munster says ná" will still write `ná bhfuil` by analogy. That is wrong.
> **RULE 3.3 — THE ASYMMETRY.** The *interrogative* particle `an` **DOES** eclipse (`an bhfuil`,
> 821). The *negative* `ná` does not. Applying "Munster drops the eclipsis" across the board wrecks
> all 109 questions.

`ná` does four jobs: negative that-clause, negative relative, negative after `nuair`, and the
conversational tag **`ná fuil?`** ("isn't it?"). A fifth, unrelated `ná` links clefts
("'sé an rud ná …" = "namely"); don't confuse it with the negative.

**Positive that-clause takes `go` + eclipsis, with the SYNTHETIC embedded verb**:
**`go bhfuilim`** / `go bhfuilimid` / `go bhfuilid` — **288 : 5** against Connemara.

## 5. WANT — the 114-sentence decision, and why ZUT settles it

Measured: Munster `teastaíonn`-family **345** vs `ag iarraidh` **288** (≈55/45). Connemara:
240 vs **1884** (11/89). So `ag iarraidh` is **5.7× more frequent per token in Connemara** — it is a
genuine Connacht signature — but it is live in Kerry too.

One evidence worker recommended splitting the 114 roughly 50/50 to reproduce measured Kerry usage.
**Rejected, on methodology.** The ZUT rail is one known prompt → exactly one target form; a course
that renders "I want to…" two different ways teaches the learner that their answer was wrong when it
wasn't. So:

> **RULE 5.1. WANT = `teastaíonn ó`.** `Teastaíonn uaim …`, `Teastaíonn uait …`.
> **RULE 5.2. TRY = `ag iarraidh` + object + `a` + VN.** This is not a collision — it is the reason
> there isn't one. Because Kerry does not use `ag iarraidh` for *want*, it is free to carry *try*,
> and the corpus shows it doing exactly that: *"bím i gcónaí **ag iarraidh** é sin a bhrú ar aghaidh"*.
> **RULE 5.3. Different-subject want takes a `go`-clause** in either construction:
> "I want you to speak Irish with me" → `Teastaíonn uaim go labhrófá Gaelainn liom`.
> **RULE 5.4. WOULD LIKE = `ba mhaith liom`** (pan-dialectal, and keeps `teastaíonn` for plain want).

Write `teastaíonn`, not the corpus's `theastaíonn` (195:150) — that lenited onset has no grammatical
trigger and is the transcriber capturing a phonetic feature.

## 6. SYNTAX

- **Object rule.** VN with a direct object → **object + `a` + lenited VN**: `Gaelainn a labhairt`,
  never `labhairt Gaelainne`. No object → bare VN: `ag iarraidh foghlaim`.
- **Purpose = `chun`** + [obj + `a` +] VN, not `le`. `chun`+VN is **352 : 19** MU:CO — `chun` is
  nearly absent from Connemara. (`le` is not *wrong* in Kerry, just not the marking choice.)
- **Direct relative** = `a` + lenition. **Indirect** = `go` + eclipsis is the Munster habit
  (`daoine go bhfuil Gaelainn acu`); standard `a bhfuil` runs at ⅙ the Connemara rate.
- **Conditionals:** open "if" = `má` + lenition + indicative; hypothetical = `dá` + eclipsis +
  conditional. "unless" = **`muna`**.
- **"When"** = `nuair a` (pan-dialectal, 3956:3424); negative `nuair ná`.
- **Echo answers** — Irish has no yes/no. `An bhfuil…?` → **`Táim` / `Nílim`**. `An raibh…?` →
  **`Bhíos` / `Ní rabhas`**. `An ndein…?` → **`Dheineas` / `Ní dheineas`**. Copula → `Is ea` / `Ní hea`.
- **Copula, classificatory** — the Munster `ea` cleft: **`Múinteoir is ea é`**, `Fear ana-dheas is ea
  é`, past `Múinteoir ab ea é`. Measured `X is ea é` **898 : 1**. Identificatory stays `Is é an fear é`.
- **`ní` + lenition** for the copula negative; `níor` for past regular verbs; but **`bí` never takes
  `níor`** — past negative is `ní raibh` / `ní rabhas`. `faigh` takes `ní bhfuair-`.

## 8. Open questions a Kerry speaker should see first

Carried forward into the build report. Ranked by how many sentences ride on them:

1. **`teastaíonn uaim` as the sole WANT** — 114 sentences. Ruled inferentially, from the collapse of
   `ag iarraidh`, not from a large positive count.
2. **`ná` vs `nach`** — 85% within Kerry, but `nach` is 209 hits, not zero.
3. **`Múinteoir is ea mé`** for 1sg self-classification — the construction is 898:1, but `is ea` +
   `mé` is only 7 hits (broadcast interviews describe others, not the speaker).
4. **`níl tú` vs `níleann tú`** (32:14) — 196 second-person sentences ride on it.
5. **`Gaelainn` vs `Gaeilge`** — a course-identity call, made above on the dialect rail.
6. **`inniubh`** — 96% within Kerry, but it is the boldest orthographic step in this spec.
7. **`thánag` vs `thána`** ("I came") — corpus 14 : 48, Ó Sé attests neither `-g` form.
