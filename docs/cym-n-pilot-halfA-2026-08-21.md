## 4. ZUT collisions against the kept range

### 4.1 The conditional stem — bydd- vs bas-. Systematic, hard, the biggest finding in this half.
Seeds 1–267 teach the conditional on the **bydd-** stem: *byddai fo* "he would" (S0065L01), *bydden ni'n licio* "we'd like" (S0187L01), *byddet ti'n deud* "you'd tell me" (S0255L01), *byddwn i'n deu'* "I'd tell you" (S0256L01), *fyddwn i ddim* (S0223L02), *fyddet ti'n licio* (S0212L04).

The canon tail uses the **bas-** stem throughout: *basai hi* (312), *tasai fo / fasai fo* (352), *fasen ni* (612), and *Baswn* as the answer-form (272). Both are good northern Welsh; the kept range is already internally mixed (it also carries *'swn i*, *'set ti*, *fysat ti*). But English "would" now maps to two Welsh stems course-wide, which is a ZUT break in the production direction.

**Hit rate: 4 of my 20 seeds (272, 312, 352, 612) — 20%.** Extrapolated to the 401-seed tail that is roughly 80 seeds. This needs one ruling from Tom before phase 2 starts, and whichever way it goes it is a *policy* fix, not 80 seeds of hand-work.

### 4.2 "everything" — popeth vs bob dim
Kept S0171L01 maps everything → *popeth*. Canon 412 uses *bob dim*. Hard collision, CONSOLIDATE to one form.

### 4.3 Canon 472 is kept seed 264, duplicated
Same English sentence, different Welsh for "that we don't": kept *nad ydan ni ddim* (S0264L03) vs canon *bod ni ddim*. This is one of the six exact-English collisions the scout already identified (464/259, 465/260, 466/261, **472/264**, 474/265, 477/267). Resolution costs nothing to build — drop the canon seed and keep the taught form — but it does mean the canon tail is not 401 net-new seeds.

### 4.4 Orthographic drift — five classes, six instances in twenty seeds
*Nest/wnest* (372), *Ddeudon/ddudon* (452), *oedden/oeddan* (452), *dwytha/diwetha* (572), *gwydraid/gwydrad* (632), *fory/yfory* (312). None of these is a conceptual collision — every one is the same word spelled two ways across the kept/canon boundary. But every one of them breaks a string-level ZUT check and would show up as a false violation in any automated sweep, and every one of them would show a learner two spellings of one word. A single normalisation pass over the canon tail, before build, is far cheaper than fixing them seed by seed. **Note: the canon Welsh is a machine batch of 2026-07-16 never proofread by a human — this is exactly the class of thing that survives that process.**

### 4.5 Soft items (log, no action)
*dŵad/dod* (292), *i chdi / i ti* (432), "end" → *pen/diwedd* (552), *dal* "still" vs *dal* "hold" (512), "main"/"big" → *fawr/mawr* (592), *newydd* "just" vs "new" (332). All are either reverse-direction convergence (harmless) or genuine sense-splits resolvable by making the English naturally specific. None needs Tom's ear except possibly *i chdi / i ti*, which is a register/dialect taste call.

## 5. Where I was unsure — the error bar

Twelve of the seventy-seven legos (16%) were genuine judgement calls where "already taught" hinges on whether a word *seen inside a molecular lego* counts as taught:

*syniad* (inside S0167L01) · *stafell* (inside S0259L01) · *newydd* as adjective vs S0015L01's "just" · *plentyn* vs taught plural *plant* · *gadael* vs taught imperative *gad* (S0261L01) · *nôl* (inside S0205L01, glossed "get") · *arall* (inside S0028L01) · *diddorol* (inside S0031L04) · *mawr* (inside S0262L01) · *gwydraid/gwydrad* · *dwytha/diwetha* · *bob dim* judged new against *popeth*.

I resolved each toward **NEW** where the known-side gloss differs from the gloss it was taught under, and toward **TAUGHT** where it is the same word in the same sense in a different form. That is the rule the brief sets, applied consistently, but it is a rule with a soft edge.

- If all twelve flipped to already-taught: **57 → 45 new**, 2.25/seed.
- If my "taught" calls are the wrong ones instead: up to **~62 new**, 3.10/seed.

**Error bar: 2.85 new legos/seed, −0.6 / +0.25.** The downside is larger than the upside, because most of the uncertain calls sit on the taught side of the line.

## 6. Verdict on the crude word-coverage approximation

**The crude measure over-counted coverage by 2–4×, and it over-counted it in a way that gets worse the more complex the sentence.**

- Crude prediction: 85 of 401 canon seeds fully covered = **21%**.
- Measured on half A: **1 of 20 = 5%**, or 2 of 20 = 10% if you count canon 472, which is only "covered" because it is a duplicate of a kept seed rather than because the vocabulary reaches.

Three distinct reasons, in order of size:

1. **Welsh legos are molecular and person-locked.** *dach chi angen* (652), *nos fory* (312), *y tro dwytha* (572) are each built entirely from atoms the kept range already teaches — and each still costs a new lego row, because a lego is a known-side intention bound to one target form, not a bag of words. Word-coverage scores these as fully covered. They are not. This is the single largest source of the gap: **23 of my 57 new legos (40%) contain no new vocabulary at all.**
2. **Known-side construction gaps.** 7 of 20 seeds (35%) need an English construction the kept range has never debuted, at +10 legos across the half. Word coverage cannot see the known side at all — it is looking at the wrong language.
3. **Orthographic and stem drift between the canon batch and the kept course.** *Nest/wnest*, *basai/byddai*, *gwydraid/gwydrad*. Some of these make a word look new when it is taught (under-counting coverage) and some make a seed look coverable when it carries a hard ZUT break (over-counting). Net effect in this sample was small but the noise is real.

So the crude measure is not merely imprecise — it is biased in one direction, because every mechanism above pushes the same way except (3), which is small. **For sizing phase 2–4 spend, do not use 21%. Use 5–10% fully covered and 2.85 (+0.5 for construction debuts) new legos per seed.**

### What this implies for the build spend — with one caveat that matters

Straight extrapolation of half A to the 401-seed tail: **~1,340 new legos** (1,143 from the per-seed rate + ~200 for construction debuts), at the methodology floor of 4 BUILD + 5 USE per lego = **~12,000 new phrases minimum**.

**Caveat, stated plainly, because it is the largest single uncertainty in that number and my method cannot close it.** I measured every seed against the *seeds 1–267 inventory only*. I did not credit legos introduced by *other pilot seeds*, because the sample is stratified across the whole tail and no two of my seeds are adjacent. In a real build, seed 512's new lego is available to seed 532. So **2.85 is an upper bound on the marginal rate**, and the true figure — and therefore the ~1,340 — will be lower, by an amount I cannot estimate from a stratified sample.

**How to close it:** run the same decomposition over a *contiguous* run of 40 canon seeds (say 300–339) and count *distinct* new legos rather than per-seed new legos. The ratio between that number and 40 × 2.85 is the reuse factor, and it converts this upper bound into a real build estimate. That is a one-worker job and it is the highest-value next measurement.

## 7. Explicit gaps in this deliverable

- **Reuse factor unmeasured** (§6 caveat). This is the known limitation of a stratified sample and it means the headline extrapolation is an upper bound, not an estimate.
- **Phrase-count cost not measured.** I sized legos, not phrases. The ~12,000 figure is the methodology floor (4 BUILD + 5 USE) multiplied out, not a measured phrase count.
- **The canon Welsh was taken as given.** All 20 of my seeds had 10/10 agreement across the ten `*_for_cym` copies, so I did not second-guess the Welsh — but that agreement means ten copies of one machine batch agree with each other, not that a human has proofread it. Three items in my sample (*Baswn* as a bare answer-form at 272, *bob dim* at 412, the *bas-* stem throughout) are places where I would want a human northern-Welsh ear before building.
- **Half B not examined.** These numbers are half A only. The two halves were interleaved so they are directly comparable — combining them is the point of the exercise.
