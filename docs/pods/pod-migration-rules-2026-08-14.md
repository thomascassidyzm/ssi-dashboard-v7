# What happens to existing learners when the pods change

One learner on Welsh has listened to pod sentences 965 times. On 11 August their pod was
swapped for the new version. Their progress didn't vanish — but three of their sentences
quietly changed underneath them. The clearest one:

> They listened to **"Thank you very much. Goodbye."** five times.
> They are now credited with having heard **"Do you have any food?"**

They have never heard it. And because they're marked as knowing it, it now gets served late
and rarely, so they may never properly learn it.

That's not a hypothetical — it is live right now, on Welsh, because the swap matched
sentences **by their position** in the pod. The new pods add sentences in the middle of
scenes, so position 3 in scene 3 is a different sentence than it used to be.

Across all languages, swapping the pods the same way would do this to **538 sentences of
learner progress, covering 4,837 listens, for 39 learners.**

## The rules, adopted as standing doctrine on 2026-08-16 by Tom's ruling on plate item A-111

1. **Match by the sentence itself, never by its position.** Position is the thing that
   broke Welsh.
2. **A sentence that survives into the new pods keeps its exact ladder position.** The
   learner carries on from where they were.
3. **A genuinely new sentence arrives unseen**, at the bottom of the ladder, like any
   sentence they've never met.
4. **A removed sentence just drops, with no penalty.** Nothing is deducted for it.
5. **A sentence that changed even slightly counts as new, not as surviving.** "Five. Ten.
   Fifteen." becoming "5. 10. 15." is the same sentence to us and a different thing to hear.
   Crediting a learner for something they haven't heard is the harm we're avoiding, so when
   in doubt we treat it as unheard.
6. **Nobody's progress can go backwards.** A learner's overall progress is worked out from
   the sentences they've completed, so removing sentences can't reduce it, and the count of
   what they've done never falls.
7. **Anyone listening at the moment of the change finishes their current round undisturbed**
   and picks up the new pods on the next one.
8. **Welsh gets fixed too.** The three Welsh learners already affected get corrected by the
   same rules, retrospectively.

One thing I'd like your steer on eventually, but which doesn't block anything: progress is
currently filed under a sentence's *position*, which is what made this possible. Filing it
under the sentence's *text* instead would make this whole class of problem impossible
forever. It's more work now and it touches live learner progress, so I've costed it but not
done it.

Roughly three-quarters of each old pod survives. All 37 languages are in the detailed notes.

"The same sentence" means identical after ignoring capitals, spacing, and the curly-quote
and ellipsis characters that vary in our files. No punctuation is ignored. Helpfully, no
sentence text appears twice in either the old or the new pods in any language, so matching
is never a guess.

## What I couldn't determine

Whether the rules produce the right result on genuinely finished new content — because no
language has finished new content yet. Every staged pod currently has around 110 of its 232
sentences still untranslated and unrecorded, which is what job #481 is doing now. The
switchover refuses to run until that's complete, so it can't go early by accident.

Separately, and worth knowing: the offline download packs up the unfinished working copy of
the pods as well as the live one. No learner is hearing it — nothing in the app actually
reads the pods out of that download yet — so it's a trap waiting rather than a fire. But it
would become a fire the moment offline listening is switched on, so I've closed it with a
one-line filter as part of this work.
