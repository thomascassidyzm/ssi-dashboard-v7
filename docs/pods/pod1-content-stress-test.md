# Pod-1 stress-test — the day-76 lens, and a rival frame

*2026-07-14. Adversarial content review of the 14 DRAFT pod-1 scenes seeded in
`canonical_pod_scenarios` (`pod_slug='pod-1'`, 196 sentences — full text read from the DB, not
the review pack). Docs only; no audio, no status changes. Companion: `pod-ladder-proposal.md`
§4 slate, §9 independent-meaning rule; `pod1-scene-review.md` review pack.*

**The lens:** the learner at day 76 — just turned genuinely interactional, now standing in a
real conversation with a real human. Pod-1's job is the social glue that keeps them there.
Every scene below is judged on one question: *which conversational moves does this actually
put in that learner's mouth and ears?*

---

## Verdict in one paragraph

The set is good — better than good in places — but its hidden shape isn't the one it fears.
These aren't transactions in a social costume; they're **interviews in a social costume**.
Five of the fourteen scenes have one speaker mining the other for information while
contributing nothing of themselves. And the whole pod lives at one emotional temperature:
everyone is pleasant, everyone says yes, every plan gets made, nothing is misheard, and
nobody ever has bad news. The single biggest failure is the missing move the whole pod
ladder exists to serve: **repair**. In 196 sentences, not one person says *"sorry, I didn't
catch that"*. For the day-76 learner, that is THE move — the one that keeps them in the
conversation when comprehension fails, which at day 76 it will, constantly.

Score: **10 keep · 3 rework · 1 kill.**

### The two structural findings

**1. The interview problem.** Scenes 4, 10, 12 — and to a softer degree 6 and 8 — are
one-way extraction: A asks, B performs. The transactional trap was avoided; the interview
trap wasn't. A day-76 learner drilled on these learns to *answer questions about themselves*
— a skill they already have from the course — not to *trade*. The fix is cheap: every asker
gets one line of their own life per scene.

**2. The emotional monotone.** Fourteen scenes, one register: pleasant. Nobody declines an
invitation, cancels a plan, mishears a word, hunts for a word, delivers bad news, or receives
it. Real social conversation is half graceful friction. The pod teaches the sunshine and
none of the weather.

## The missing moves

Ranked by day-76 value. The first one is the finding of this review.

| Missing move | Why it matters | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Repair** — "sorry, I didn't catch that", "could you say that again, slower?", "what does that word mean?" | The survival move of the whole enterprise. The day-76 learner WILL lose the thread in every real conversation; repair is how they stay in the room instead of smiling and drowning. It's named in the pod-1 north star. | **0 instances in 196 sentences.** |
| **Saying no gracefully / cancelling** — "I'd love to, but…", "I'm going to have to cancel" | Nobody in pod-1 ever declines anything. Real social life is half graceful refusal, and the learner who can't say no stops getting asked — or stops answering. | Every invitation in the pod is accepted. |
| **Bad news and real commiseration** — "I've got some bad news, I'm afraid" / "I'm so sorry to hear that" | Good News exists; its harder, higher-value mirror doesn't. Sympathy at stakes is the move that turns an acquaintance into a friend. | Scene 5's sore throat is the pod's emotional ceiling. |
| **Vagueness and word-hunting** — "what's the word… it'll come to me", "you know the place I mean" | How natives hold the floor while thinking — and how learners survive their own gaps without surrendering the turn. Doubles as licensed thinking-time. | Fillers exist; floor-holding doesn't. |
| **The graceful exit** — "anyway, I'd better go", "I'll let you get on" | Getting OUT of a conversation warmly is as load-bearing as getting in. It appears twice incidentally, never drilled. | Scenes 3 and 14, one line each. |

On teasing: the ramp table defers it to pod-2, and the north star lists it as pod-1 glue.
My read: the ramp is right for irony, wrong for warmth-teasing — scene 4's "which feels like
justice" is exactly the pod-1-safe register, and there should be more of it. One for your ear.

## Part 2 — The rival frame: pod-1 by MOVE, not situation

**Organising principle:** each scene is one move family, drilled as one continuous natural
conversation that keeps forcing the move — different micro-contexts inside the scene, so the
learner hears the move six to ten times instead of once. The situational frame gives a move
one appearance and moves on; this frame builds automaticity, which is what a day-76 learner
reaches for under pressure. Same authoring format, same two-voice friendly-informal register,
same TTS rules. Every sample line below is a complete communicative unit per §9.

16 scenes, roughly in social-lifecycle order — into the conversation, through it, out of it:

| # | Title | The move | Three sample lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hello Stranger** | Openings and re-openings | "Hello stranger — I haven't seen you for ages!" · "How did it go on Friday, in the end?" · "I was just thinking about you the other day." |
| 2 | **And What About You?** | Turn-returning and follow-up questions | "And what about you? How was your week?" · "And then what happened?" · "What was it like, being back?" |
| 3 | **Say That Again** | Repair — the flagship | "Sorry, I didn't catch that." · "Could you say that again, a bit slower?" · "Do you mean she's not coming at all?" |
| 4 | **You're Joking!** | Big reactions — delight, shock, awe | "You're joking!" · "No! I don't believe it." · "That's the best thing I've heard all week." |
| 5 | **Tell Me About It** | Agreeing and commiserating | "Tell me about it — mine does exactly the same." · "I know exactly what you mean." · "Same here, honestly." |
| 6 | **I See What You Mean, But** | Softened disagreement and conceding | "I see what you mean, but I'm not sure I agree." · "The food was good, I'll give you that." · "Maybe it's just me, then." |
| 7 | **Poor You** | Sympathy and offers of help | "Oh no, you poor thing." · "Is there anything I can do?" · "That sounds really hard, honestly." |
| 8 | **You Look Well** | Compliments — giving and receiving | "You look really well — have you been away?" · "I love your jacket. Where's it from?" · "That's kind of you to say, thank you." |
| 9 | **Go On, Come With Us** | Inviting and light persuading | "Go on, come with us — it'll be fun." · "You can leave early if you're tired." · "All right, you've talked me into it." |
| 10 | **I'd Love To, But** | Declining and cancelling gracefully | "I'd love to, but I can't this Saturday." · "I'm so sorry — I'm going to have to cancel tonight." · "Can we do it another week instead?" |
| 11 | **Shall We Say Half Past?** | Proposing and adjusting arrangements | "Shall we say half past one instead?" · "Where's good for you?" · "Perfect — see you there at eight." |
| 12 | **If I Were You** | Asking for and giving advice | "What do you think I should do?" · "If I were you, I'd take it." · "It's up to you, of course, but I think you'd regret saying no." |
| 13 | **To Be Honest** | Opinions with hedges | "To be honest, it wasn't really my thing." · "It's not bad — I just expected more." · "I might be wrong, but I think it's overrated." |
| 14 | **Guess What** | Telling news — good and bad — and receiving it | "Guess what — we got the house!" · "I've got some bad news, I'm afraid." · "I'm so sorry to hear that." |
| 15 | **What's the Word** | Vagueness, word-hunting, holding the floor | "What's the word — it's on the tip of my tongue." · "Hang on, it'll come to me." · "You know the place I mean — the one by the bridge." |
| 16 | **Anyway, I'd Better Go** | Changing topic and closing warmly | "Anyway, I'd better go — I'm already late." · "Right, I'll let you get on." · "Lovely to see you — let's do this properly soon." |

**How a move-scene stays a conversation, not a phrasebook.** Worked shape for scene 3, *Say
That Again*: two friends in a noisy café — one repair; a name gets misheard — second repair,
different form; a phone number — "slower, please"; an unfamiliar word — "what does that
mean?"; and the payoff line, the native saying "sorry, I'm mumbling — I'll slow down", so the
learner hears that asking is *welcome*, not shameful. One continuous, plausible fourteen-line
conversation; the move lands six times in six forms. Every scene in the frame is authored to
that recipe: a scenario that naturally *forces* its move repeatedly, never a list.

**What the frame buys.** Coverage by construction — the missing-moves table above can't
happen, because the move inventory IS the table of contents. Exposure density — six to ten
reps per move instead of one. And it doubles as the QA checklist for pods 2–3 and every
specialist pod: the same trick §6 of the ladder plays with discourse *frames*, played one
level down with *moves*.

**What it costs.** Authoring is harder per scene — a bad move-scene degenerates into a
phrasebook, and the recipe above is the only defence. And topic variety per scene shrinks:
the situational frame tours weekend–family–flat–film; the move frame tours forms of one
speech act. A learner browsing scene titles sees moves, not life — which I'd argue is the
more honest menu, but it reads less like a story.

## Head to head

| | Situational (current draft) | Move frame (rival) |
|---|---|---|
| Day-76 test | Moves appear once each; 5 missing entirely, incl. repair | Every move drilled to automaticity; coverage by construction |
| Naturalness | High — 10 of 14 already genuinely good | Achievable, but only with the forcing-scenario recipe; the failure mode is phrasebook |
| Authoring cost | Sunk — it exists, and 10 scenes keep as-is | 16 fresh scenes, harder brief per scene |
| Scales to pods 2–3 | Slate-by-slate taste each time | Move inventory becomes the standing checklist |

## Recommendation

**My read: the move frame wins the day-76 test outright, and the day-76 test is the pod's
stated purpose.** Pod-1 exists to install moves; organising it by anything else makes the
moves incidental, and the current draft proves the point — a good team authored 196 pleasant
sentences and the north-star move appears zero times. That's not an authoring lapse; it's
what topic-organisation does. If you want one decision: **adopt the rival frame as the pod-1
canon**, and mine the current draft for its best material — scenes 5, 9, 11 and 13 are
already near-perfect move-scenes for sympathy, disagreement, backchannelling and news, and
convert almost verbatim.

If instead you keep the situational frame — legitimate; it's warmer as a menu — the minimum
honest patch is: **kill 12, de-interview 4 and 10, trim 3's booking tail, and add two scenes:
*Say That Again* and *I'd Love To, But*.** That takes the set from good to defensible.

Either way, one thing is non-negotiable: **pod-1 does not ship without a repair scene.** It
is the single highest-value social move a day-76 learner can own, it is named in the pod's
own north star, and right now it is drilled exactly never.
