## 2. The pod's stated shape

A Method Pod scene is **Tom and Aran, as themselves, in Italian**, talking about their own methodology the way they actually talk — which the corpus shows is: completion, ratification, teasing, flagged stories, and arguments that survive their own digressions.

- **Six turns**, alternating, per the ruled generation discipline.
- **Each scene = one owned exchange + ONE newly admitted response position.** "Owned" means attested in live POD-1 or in the two specimen sets that passed on 2026-08-29.
- **Per scene, two admissions are recorded** (per the graph ruling): the surface forms it admits (availability for phrase baskets only — never a prompt target, heard ≠ owned) and the shape it witnesses. Scenes are walks; delivery position stays derived, not authored — I have proposed no ordering.
- **Speakability**: these sit at Layers 5–6 (sophisticated expression, register). The zero-admission scene deliberately drops its *vocabulary* to Layer 0–1 while keeping its *shape* at the summit — that separation is the point of the experiment.

## 3. The stage-content guard — where I drew the line

**Kept out entirely:** the Jeremy Vine story (the archive has it going "near the front" of the Bologna talk — it is stage content, full stop); the Bologna trial itself, its predictions, and Module 4 §4.2's trial-design argument; any numbers of the 30+30 family; anything about Italy, the event, or a room of polyglots; and — most importantly — **any scene that walks an argument in the order a talk would walk it.** No scene here builds toward a thesis; each takes one invariant, in fragment, mid-conversation, as if it were the hundredth time they'd turned it over.

**Kept in:** single invariants from the 2020 transcripts as conversational subject matter — items-and-edges, effort-in-memory, one-sentence-value, play-as-mechanism. My read: the invariants are their daily conversational water and can't be "rehearsed away"; what would rehearse the talk is sequence and argument-chain, so that is what I excluded.

## 4. The specimens

Four scenes, six turns, English with the Italian beside it. Informal throughout — *tu*, per the standing default; they are old friends.

### Scene 1 — *Il palazzo in mezzo al campo* (The building in the field)

**Owns:** backchannel ratification (POD-1, set one). **Newly admits:** joint construction — completing the partner's clause and having it ratified. **Layer 5.** Subject: invariant 1, items and edges.

| | English | Italiano |
|---|---|---|
| TOM | It's not that you know the words. You know what's *between* the words. | Non è che conosci le parole. Conosci quello che c'è *tra* le parole. |
| ARAN | The edges. Like a city — you don't know it because you know the buildings. | I collegamenti. Come una città — non la conosci perché conosci i palazzi. |
| TOM | You know it because you know the streets. So a new word without… | La conosci perché conosci le strade. Quindi una parola nuova senza… |
| ARAN | …without streets is a building in a field. | …senza strade è un palazzo in mezzo a un campo. |
| TOM | Exactly that. Nobody lives there. | Esatto, proprio così. Non ci abita nessuno. |
| ARAN | And schools keep building them. Poor things. | E le scuole continuano a costruirli. Poverini. |

**Near-neighbour selection points, deliberate:** *palazzo* ≠ sp. *palacio* (building, not palace — a false friend on the scene's key noun); *strada* has no live Spanish neighbour (pull toward *calle* → nothing); locative *ci* ("non **ci** abita nessuno") — Spanish drops it entirely, selection by absence; *quindi* vs *entonces/así que* — the connective has no cognate anchor.

---

### Scene 2 — *Il colpo basso* (The dirty trick)

**Owns:** anecdote-as-evidence with status flag (parts 1/2's "crocodile"; set two's anecdote's-door). **Newly admits:** the sceptical challenge landing *mid-story* and being survived — then the anecdote turned on the listener. **Layer 5–6.** Subject: invariant 3, effort implicated in memory.

| | English | Italiano |
|---|---|---|
| ARAN | I've got a story. It proves nothing — accept it anyway. | Ho una storia. Non dimostra niente — accettala lo stesso. |
| TOM | Go on, then. | Dai, racconta. |
| ARAN | A learner writes to me: the words she remembers are the ones that cost her effort. The easy ones — gone. | Una studentessa mi scrive: le parole che ricorda sono quelle che le sono costate fatica. Quelle facili — sparite. |
| TOM | One learner. You're building a church on one learner? | Una studentessa. Stai costruendo una chiesa su una studentessa? |
| ARAN | I said it proves nothing. But you'll remember it, won't you? | Ho detto che non dimostra niente. Ma tu te la ricorderai, no? |
| TOM | …That's a dirty trick. Yes. | …Che colpo basso. Sì. |

**Near-neighbour points:** *fatica* ≠ sp. *fatiga* (effort vs tiredness — false friend carrying the invariant itself); *niente* vs *nada* (no cognate anchor); *ricordarsi* — cognate verb, non-matching reflexive pattern ("te la ricorderai" vs "la recordarás"); *sparite* vs *desaparecidas* (selection by absence).

### Scene 3 — *Due parole* (Two words) — **ZERO-ADMISSION**

**Owns:** say-that-again / more-slowly / counting (all live POD-1); the read-back line. **Newly admits:** the teasing audit of a public self-repair — the [30:35] arithmetic move, the survivable mistake at the summit. **Layer 0–1 vocabulary carrying a Layer 5 shape.** Subject: invariants 4 and 7 — minimum viable stimulus, one sentence has real value — *enacted*, not discussed.

I verified this one against the live rows: every English content word below occurs in `pod-0`'s 231 sentences (try again, more slowly, of course, need, words, three, two, third, said, talk, day, very well, enough). It carries no new vocabulary at all; what it newly admits is purely the shape.

| | English | Italiano |
|---|---|---|
| TOM | Can we try again? More slowly. | Possiamo riprovare? Più lentamente. |
| ARAN | Of course. But you don't need all the words. | Certo. Ma non ti servono tutte le parole. |
| TOM | I need three. No — two. | Me ne servono tre. No — due. |
| ARAN | You said three. | Hai detto tre. |
| TOM | The third one — that's you. Two words, and I can talk all day. | La terza — sei tu. Due parole, e posso parlare tutto il giorno. |
| ARAN | Very well. That's enough for me. | Benissimo. A me basta. |

**Near-neighbour points:** partitive *ne* ("me **ne** servono tre") — Spanish has no *ne*; the Spanish-adjacent speaker will omit it, and this scene walks straight into that; *servire* for need ("mi serve") where Spanish reaches for *necesitar*; *aspettare/sperare* deliberately absent here but note *riprovare* vs *volver a intentar* — Italian prefixes where Spanish periphrases.

**The open question the commission asked** — can zero-admission be written well at the summit? — my answer is a qualified yes, and this is the evidence: the summit shape (self-repair, teasing audit, repair absorbed with a counter-tease) survives intact on a Layer-0 vocabulary. Whether it passes the *ear* is yours to say.

### Scene 4 — *Dov'eravamo?* (Where were we?)

**Owns:** reformulation (promoted in set two — "so what you're saying is"). **Newly admits:** the flagged digression with an explicit return marker — the deficit-list move, minted because Part 5 attests digressions but never the marker. **Layer 5–6.** Subject: invariants 6 and 9 — play as the mechanism, language as a complex adaptive system.

| | English | Italiano |
|---|---|---|
| ARAN | The moment you've got edges, you can go in lots of directions. And that's when it becomes a game. | Nel momento in cui hai i collegamenti, puoi andare in tante direzioni. Ed è lì che diventa un gioco. |
| TOM | So what you're saying is: control arrives before correctness. | Quindi quello che stai dicendo è: il controllo arriva prima della correttezza. |
| ARAN | Hang on — that makes me think of the garden. Two minutes, I promise. | Aspetta — questo mi fa pensare al giardino. Due minuti, promesso. |
| TOM | Clock's running. | Il tempo corre. |
| ARAN | Nobody designs a garden leaf by leaf. You plant, and the garden answers. Anyway — where were we? | Nessuno progetta un giardino foglia per foglia. Tu pianti, e il giardino risponde. Comunque — dov'eravamo? |
| TOM | Control before correctness. And your garden just agreed with me. | Al controllo prima della correttezza. E il tuo giardino mi ha appena dato ragione. |

**Near-neighbour points:** the staple itself is a near-neighbour walk — "quindi **quello che stai dicendo**" sits one notch off "así que **lo que estás diciendo**" (quello che/lo que, stai/estás, dicendo/diciendo) — the highest-value selection point in the set, because it's the move they'll reach for most; *aspetta* vs *espera*; *dov'**eravamo*** — essere where Spanish uses estar ("dónde **estábamos**"), the systemic ser/estar → essere/stare remap; *diventa* — no Spanish cognate (*se vuelve/se convierte*), selection by absence; *dato ragione* vs *dado la razón* — article drop.
