# CORE scene 0 — the medium contract — 2026-08-31

The first CORE content authored since the pod/frame branch train closed. N1201 was ratified and
sited as a prologue — scene 0, after the hello, before the first transaction — and the dialogue
did not exist. Here it is: six turns on the happy path, and the two attested branches attached at
position 2.

Cast: **Mira**, a named individual, and a **Neighbour**, the generic counterpart — pod-0's own
convention. No sector, no culture, no till, no clinic, no classes. The business the scene pivots
to is an ordinary arrangement, which is CORE and belongs to nobody's sector.

## Design notes

### The six turns are five positions and an answer

| Turn | Position | Move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 — declare the limitation, propose the weaker medium, name the fallback, ask | — |
| 2 | 2 — grant + reassure | F14 |
| 3 | 3 — issue the standing repair licence, mutual and pre-disarmed | — |
| 4 | 4 — accept the licence | F1 |
| 5 | 5 — pivot to business (composed into N7 position 1) | F21 |
| 6 | the pivot taken up (N7 position 4) | F1 |

The sixth turn is real work. A pivot nobody answers has not been shown to be a pivot, and the
scene's whole claim is that the contract hands the encounter over to the trunk in one move. Six
was the count the commission named and six is what the shape wanted; no seventh turn was needed.

N1's open has already run when turn 1 starts — the contract wraps the hello rather than replacing
it, which is why the first words are "Before we start", the corpus's own hinge.

### The scene is the OFFER, not the notice

Per the ruling of 2026-08-31: the notice is the primitive and carries no branch; the offer is the
notice plus a request for a grant, and the survivability belongs to the request. Scene 0 is
therefore the offer, because the branches are half of what this scene exists to teach. **A
notice-realisation scene 0 is authorable and is not authored here** — it is named as a gap in the
walk set's accounting, not silently skipped.

### The branches attach, they do not follow

All three variants are continuations of **turn 2** — the same turn, answered differently. In the
walk set each carries an explicit `attaches_to: {walk: "W1201", step: 2, node: "N1201", position:
2}`. None of them is a later scene, and the decline in particular is only worth anything at the
moment the learner is in it.

**Both attested accounts of the decline are held**, because they are scoped differently and teach
different things. The **capacity** account is scoped to *today* — "I'm half asleep" — and leaves an
implicit licence to re-offer, which turn 5 takes up in two words ("Another day, then"). The
**stakes** account is scoped to a *domain*. The corpus's domain is medical; the sector is stripped,
so the domain here is the thing that has to be exact — dates and numbers. That scope then bites at
the pivot: the business at hand *is* a date, so the pivot itself is conducted in the fallback and
the learner honours a contract they agreed to thirty seconds earlier. That is the same mechanism as
the Bologna pharmacist switching at the dosage, with no pharmacy in it.

**The recoveries came unasked**, as the health calibration read predicted. The decline runs the
four canonical moves — yield instantly with no second offer, ratify the choice as sensible,
de-personalise the refusal, re-anchor and pivot — and the Welsh offence-disclaimer form ("no
offence meant" / "none taken at all") is gone while its function stays: the account does the
de-personalising, and the neighbour's "It's me, this morning" carries it without ceremony.

**The diversion**: the grant is never spoken anywhere in that walk. Acceptance happens by conduct —
the counterpart simply carries on in the language — and the interlude shows no sign of handing the
encounter back, so Mira has to take herself back. Her turn 5 does three jobs in one breath:
acknowledge the biography warmly, self-initiate the pivot, and issue the repair licence on the way
through. Positions 3 and 4 are salvaged from a turn that was never going to arrive, which is
exactly the corpus's own recovery, twice attested.

### The Bologna acceptance test, run against this dialogue

Tom's test is that the thing instantiates in a pair carrying none of Wales's history. Run honestly,
line by line: nothing in these twenty-four turns names a country, an institution, a language, a
learning infrastructure, a civic cause, or a profession. The languages are named **deictically** —
"your language", "mine", "yours", "back to mine" — so the known text is pair-invariant with no
template slot in it; an `ita_for_eng` overlay says "Italian" and "English" and nothing else moves.
The setting is two people at a door about a parcel, which exists in Bologna, in Cardiff and in
Manila.

**Verdict: pass.** The cost it carries is the one §6 of the canonical document already named and
this scene deliberately pays: **the grant is flat**. "Go on, of course. We'll manage between us."
is friendly, not ceremonial — there is no applause economy, because a pair with no politics around
its language will not supply one. A Welsh learner meeting this scene will find position 2 thinner
than the corpus they came from. That is the culture layer leaving the room, and I have not tried to
sneak warmth back in to compensate: sector pods re-instantiate the contract in their own terms and
that is where a richer grant belongs.

Second cost, smaller and worth saying: the scene has no stakes. Health's duty-of-care raiser is
stripped by ruling, so the *reason* the medium matters is never argued — a parcel on Thursday is
not something you must get right. The stakes account in the third variant is the only place the
trunk carries any weight at all, and it carries it by scope rather than by consequence.

### Role neutrality

The shape claims no position cares who serves whom, and the scene does not smuggle a role in. Mira
and the Neighbour are not professional and client, not server and customer: neither serves the
other, and the arrangement they reach is a favour either could have asked for. The only asymmetry
is the one the offer realisation *is* — one party is learning the other's language, which is the
declaration itself and not a role. The speaker labels are swappable: nothing in any of the four
walks breaks if the Neighbour is the declarer.

### The accounting stays honest

`walks/pod-0.json` is untouched — not a step added, edited or renumbered; its 231 rows still
reconcile. Scene 0 is its own walk set at `services/shared/metagraph/walks/core-scene-0.json`,
named `core-scene-0` after the scene rather than after a pod, because it is trunk content that
every sector pod will re-instantiate rather than a pod's transcript.

Its provenance block says plainly what it is: **authored, not transcribed**. There is no `row`
anywhere in the file, no g-number is cited, `corpus_rows` is `0`, and the source block names this
job and the ratified shape. Nothing in it may be merged into a corpus accounting.

`index.cjs` auto-loads it and the selfcheck's generic walk loop validates every node, position,
move and composition edge it cites. **Before: 367 passed, 0 failed. After: 425 passed, 0 failed.**
The new file also validates against the metagraph v1 schema (checked directly with ajv; the
selfcheck itself only schema-validates pod-0).

### Two things for the store's owner, neither of them blocking

1. **A branch attached as a separate walk has no field for it.** The step-level `branches` array
   requires each branch to carry a corpus `row` (`^g[0-9]+$`), which authored content does not have
   and must not invent. So the attachment is expressed as a walk-level `attaches_to` block plus a
   `branch_point: true` marker on the step it forks from. That is a new field, taken as the
   taste-safe default rather than changing the ratified schema; if the store wants attachment to be
   first-class, that is a small deliberate edit and not mine to take.
2. **`stepRefs()` crashes on `branch: true` without a `branches` array** — `s.branches is not
   iterable`, `services/shared/metagraph/index.cjs:105`. I hit it, renamed my flag, and left the
   code alone. A one-line guard would make the next authored walk set fail loudly instead of
   throwing.
