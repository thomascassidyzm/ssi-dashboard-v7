## Measured shape (round 4, counted from the six excerpts)
- 170 transcribed speaker turns or intonation units across the six excerpts.
- Median length 4 words. About two in three are 6 words or fewer.
- The longest single stretch is about 38 words, and it is a story that others keep interrupting.
  Anything over about 35 words is longer than anything in real talk.
- Overlap is constant: in every excerpt someone starts before the last speaker has finished, two or three
  people say the same thing at once, or someone finishes another person's line.

## What the talk does (the seven points, digested)
1. Nobody discusses a topic as a topic. They talk about what is in front of them, who did what, and things
   that happened; topics drift and nobody announces or closes them.
2. Turns are tiny. A long turn means someone is telling something, and the others interrupt it with
   questions, objections and their own version.
3. People talk over each other: finishing a line, echoing the same words at once, a side question to the
   person telling the story about a detail (when, where, who).
4. Sentences get abandoned and restarted; people cut themselves off mid-word and reformulate.
5. Laughter is frequent in the recordings, but it marks a tease or the end of a story. (For SSi round 4,
   laughter is kept rare on purpose: most good lines get a reply, not a laugh.)
6. The physical scene breaks in: food, plates, a phone, something happening at the table.
7. Teasing more than opinions: the friends test and needle each other rather than announcing positions.

## How the floor moves
- The person with the story holds the floor only loosely; a friend's challenge or fact-check question
  can take it away.
- A second person often takes the floor with "I've got one too" and their own version.
- Quiet people come in with a single word or a short question, then sometimes take over.
- Chorus moments: three people say nearly the same thing in quick succession.
