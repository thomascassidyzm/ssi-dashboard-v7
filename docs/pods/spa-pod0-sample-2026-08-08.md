# Spanish pod — listen to the new scenarios

**Course:** Spanish for English speakers · **Date:** 2026-08-08

## The one question

**Does this sound like two real people having this conversation?**

That's it. You are not proofreading the Spanish, and you are not approving anything to run.
Just your ear on whether the exchanges below sound like a conversation between two people.

## What you're listening to

Aran's new pod script — 231 sentences across 22 scenes, replacing the old 142-sentence version —
has been translated into Spanish and voiced. There are two voices, one female and one male, which
is Aran's casting rule: **Maria** and **Pablo**.

**Every clip below is a line the Spanish course has never had before.** Four are brand new scenes
Aran wrote; four are lines where he reworded the English enough that the old Spanish no longer
matched. Nothing here is a line the course already had — that was the flaw in the German samples
you were sent earlier tonight.

The live Spanish course has not been touched. All of this sits on a working copy alongside it.

## Two things worth knowing before you judge

1. **Scene 3 is one voice talking to itself.** The barista and Sarah are both women, and the
   two-voice rule allows exactly one female voice, so Maria plays both sides of that exchange.
   Scenes 1 and 2 are proper two-handers because they happen to pair a man with a woman. This
   is a consequence of the rule, not a mistake — but it is the thing most likely to break the
   illusion of two real people, and it will happen in every language. Worth your ear.

2. **The script says miles and pounds.** Scene 2 measures the distance into town in miles, and
   a couple of later scenes price things in pounds. In a Spanish course those would be
   kilometres and euros. The translation followed Aran's English faithfully rather than quietly
   changing it. Say the word and it gets changed here and everywhere else it appears.
