# CORE scene 0 — restaged in the transactional trunk — 2026-08-31

Second attempt. The shape is the ratified one, untouched. The world is new: the neighbour and the
parcel are gone, and scene 0 now lives where POD 1 already lives — a café counter and a hotel desk.
The café hosts the happy path, the busy decline and the biography diversion (Sarah and the Barista,
W1's own cast). The check-in desk hosts the careful decline (the Guest and the Receptionist, W10's
own cast), because dates and numbers are literally that desk's business.

One correction runs through every branch, and it is the one that made the first version nonsense:
**someone who declines the offer falls back into the LEARNER'S language** — "let's stick to yours" —
never into their own, which is the very language the learner just offered to attempt.

## Design notes

### What changed, and why

The first attempt staged the contract between two neighbours arranging a parcel. That world cannot
motivate a decline — nothing is urgent and nothing goes wrong if it is misunderstood — so the
decline had to be a mood, and it read as one. Restaged in the transactional trunk, every turn that
was previously defended on the design is now motivated by the world:

- **The grant** has a reason: the counter is quiet.
- **The capacity decline** has a reason the learner can see over her own shoulder: a queue, and a
  barista working alone. It is "sorry, I'm rushed, let's do this in yours" almost verbatim — the
  attested account with nothing invented.
- **The stakes decline** has a consequence: a misheard check-out morning costs someone a room. The
  receptionist is being careful, not reluctant — and the read-back in the final turn *demonstrates*
  why the numbers wanted the stronger medium, without any stakes-raiser being spoken.
- **The self-pivot out of biography** has a motive the room can see: the queue is growing and nobody
  else is going to end the chat.
- **The offer itself** finally makes obvious sense: the learner is a visitor at a counter, and a
  visitor asking to do the business in the local language is a thing people actually do. Two
  neighbours who by construction share a language was the hole the whole first version fell into.

### The deixis correction

The first version's decline read "I'd rather stay in mine today" — the counterpart declining into
their *own* language, which is the very language the learner had just offered to attempt. That is
the "What???". The rule, now stated in the walk file and obeyed in every line: the learner's own
language is the shared fallback, so a decliner proposes **the learner's** language — "let's stick to
yours" — and the learner answers "mine it is". Languages stay deictic throughout ("your language",
"mine", "yours"), so the known text is pair-invariant with no template slot.

### The six turns are five positions and an answer

| Turn | Position | Move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 — declare the limitation, propose the weaker medium, name the fallback, ask | — |
| 2 | 2 — grant + reassure | F14 |
| 3 | 3 — issue the standing repair licence, mutual and pre-disarmed | — |
| 4 | 4 — accept the licence | F1 |
| 5 | 5 — pivot to business (composed into N2 position 2 — at a counter the pivot IS the order) | F21 |
| 6 | the pivot taken up as a read-back (N4 position 3, the move W10 attests) | F1 |

N1's open has already run when turn 1 starts — the contract wraps the hello rather than replacing
it, which is why the first words are "Before I order" / "Before we do the booking".

Everything settled by the first attempt's rulings is carried forward unchanged: the scene is the
**offer**, not the notice (the notice is the primitive, has no branch, and is named as a gap in the
walk set's accounting rather than silently skipped); the branches **attach at position 2** rather
than following; role-neutrality holds (nothing breaks if the counterpart is the declarer); and in
the diversion **the grant is never spoken** — acceptance happens by conduct and the learner takes
herself back to business, salvaging positions 3 and 4 on the way through. All four canonical
recovery moves arrive unasked in each decline: yield instantly, ratify the choice as sensible,
de-personalise, re-anchor and pivot.

### Two scenarios, one attachment point

The busy decline and the biography diversion are literal continuations of the happy path's turn 2 —
same counter, same ask, answered differently. The careful decline attaches at the same *position*
but restages it at the check-in desk, because the stakes account needs a business made of dates and
numbers and no café order is one. The café branches are the same ask on different afternoons — a
grant needs a quiet moment, a decline needs a queue; a listening learner meets one walk at a time,
and the answers, not the weather, are the teaching.

### The acceptance test, run line by line

Nothing in these twenty-four turns names a country, an institution, a language, a profession or a
civic cause. A café counter and a hotel desk exist in Bologna, in Cardiff and in Manila; a till is
not a sector. The stripped list stays stripped: no institutional fallback, no applause economy, no
duty-of-care stakes-raiser, no offence-disclaimer tail (the queue and the dates do the
de-personalising), no native's stake in the learner's project. And the flatness the first attempt
had to apologise for is gone without smuggling culture back in: the weight is carried by **the
business at hand** — a queue, a booking date — which any pair anywhere supplies for free.

**Verdict: pass**, and this time without the two costs the first version had to confess.
