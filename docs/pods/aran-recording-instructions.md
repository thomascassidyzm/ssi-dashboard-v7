## 2. How to record — it's all in the browser, no files to handle

You don't need to record anything separately and send it over — everything happens live in
the recording tool and uploads itself as you go.

1. Go to the recording room for the course and voice you're covering — you'll get a link
   like `/record/cym_n_for_eng?podVoice=<your voice>`. If you don't have the link, ask and
   we'll send it (it's tied to your email, so once you're cast it's yours).
2. Allow microphone access when the browser asks.
3. The screen shows you the next line to read, in Welsh, with the English underneath so you
   always know what it means. Just **read it naturally and pause when you're done** — the
   tool listens for the gap in your speech and automatically moves on to the next line. You
   don't need to press anything between lines.
4. **If the Welsh text on screen doesn't sound right to you** — wrong word, wrong register,
   whatever — you can **edit it right there before you record it**. That's expected and
   welcome; this is your call as the native speaker. If you edit a line *after* you've
   already recorded it, just re-record it — the new take automatically replaces the old one,
   nothing needs deleting.
5. Work through your whole list in one sitting if you can — it's much easier to stay in a
   consistent pace and tone that way, but if you need to stop, just pick up later exactly
   where you left off; nothing is lost.

That's it — no exporting, no file naming, no manual upload. The moment you finish a line,
it's saved.

---

## 3. Pacing — read at a natural, easy conversational pace

This is transactional, everyday dialogue — a barista, a receptionist, a pharmacist, someone
giving directions. Read it the way you'd actually say it in that situation: **clear and a
little careful, not rushed, not performed.** Not slow-motion "teaching voice" Welsh — just
unhurried natural speech, the way you'd talk to someone whose Welsh is still coming along.

If a character is a different person in a different scene (the barista shows up more than
once, a customer shows up more than once), you don't need to voice them differently each
time — same voice, same natural pace, throughout.

---

## 4. One thing to know about `'…'` if you see it

You probably won't see this yet in the current pod-0 script, but it may start appearing in
future updates, so it's worth knowing: if a line has **`…`** in the middle of it (a proper
ellipsis, not just a comma), that's a marked **breathing point** — it tells you where a
natural pause sits inside a longer sentence, not a place to trail off or change tone. Just
**take a natural breath there and carry on** in the same sentence, the way you would if you
were speaking it for real and needed a beat to keep going. It's not a stage direction to act
out — it's literally just "breathe here."

---

## 5. What happens to your takes

Every line you record is stored immediately under your voice, mastered automatically, and
becomes part of that course's audio. You'll be able to see your progress (how many lines
recorded vs still open) on the same screen — if that counter doesn't seem to be updating,
don't worry, it's a known small display glitch, not a sign anything failed. Your takes are
being saved regardless.

If anything looks or sounds wrong once it's in, or you're not sure whether a line landed,
just ask — we can check the database directly and confirm.
