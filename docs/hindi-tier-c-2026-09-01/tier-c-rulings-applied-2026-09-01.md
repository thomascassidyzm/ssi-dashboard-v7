## Ruling 2 — the fused future

**It cleared 2 seeds, and there was nothing else in the course for it to clear.** That second half is the finding, so it is worth being exact about it.

Kai's shape is now on the page at seed 5: the verb alone is taught as one chunk, *to practise speaking*, and the fused form sits on top of it as an overlapping chunk, *I'm going to practise speaking*. Seed 25 does the same with the question — *to help me*, then *are you going to help me*.

I then went looking for the later instances to mark not-new, and they are not there. The course has eleven LEGOs that teach an English "going to" future. Only **three** of them actually lost their Hindi to the proofreader's fusion — seeds 5, 25 and 82. The other eight still carry the periphrastic वाला forms in her new Hindi, untouched and working.

And the eight survivors are not repetitions of a bare marker. Each one is a whole predicate carrying its own new verb — *he's going to ask you*, *where he's going to meet*, *that man is going to tell*, *going to be okay*. On Kai's own shape those **are** the overlapping fused chunks he wants to exist, so marking them not-new would delete the very thing the ruling asks for. Four of them (seeds 82, 223, 293, 493) are also the **only** LEGO in their seed — a not-new flag there takes the whole seed off the learner path, silently, because not-new LEGOs are real in the data and never delivered.

So I set no flags. The course never repeats a bare "I'm going to" chunk, which is what the ruling was written to stop.

Two things to note. The earlier count of "73 seeds course-wide carrying a synthetic future" does not reproduce against the live database — I measure **52** seeds carrying a Hindi synthetic-future ending, and most of those are conditionals like चाहूँगा *("I'd like")*, not futures at all. That count was measuring Hindi morphology, and Ruling 1 says Hindi morphology is not what decides this. Second, **seed 82 is the one genuine third case and I did not touch it** — it sits in the 251 mechanical seeds another job owns, and its fix is exactly that job's re-gloss. Flagging it here so it does not fall between us.

## Ruling 3 — split and reordered known sides

**It cleared 27 seeds.** Nine of them needed no change at all: the chunk's Hindi is intact and simply sits in a different place in the sentence now, which the ruling says is not a defect. Seeds 255, 323, 381, 392, 117, 138, 113, 91 and 470 are all in that group — for example *"you'll be ready to leave"* is still जाने के लिए तैयार होंगे, just with कब dropped into the middle of it. Nothing to do.

The other eighteen needed the known side moved to its new contiguous citation form, because the proofreader changed the words as well as the order. Kai's own example is one of them: seed 49's *"you know"* was आप समझते हैं and is now stored as **आप समझ रहे हैं** — contiguous, no dots, still meaning "you know". Others in the same shape: *"it's like this"* → बात ऐसी है, *"what you said"* → आपने जो कहा, *"I feel as if"* → मुझे लग रहा है कि, *"it changes"* → इससे बदलाव आता है, *"to drive"* → ड्राइव करना, *"I'd have been happy"* → मुझे ख़ुशी होती.

One of the eighteen deserves a flag rather than silence: seed 384's *"couldn't agree with"* is now stored as उससे सहमत नहीं था, which reads as *"didn't agree with"* — the ability modal softened when she rewrote the sentence. The triage read this seed as an ordering problem, and on Ruling 3 it is one, so I kept the chunk. But the modal is genuinely weaker than the English and it is Kai's call whether that matters.

## The drill-coverage gaps

Kai's ruling says the work these seeds need is drill, not surgery. Here is what the drill actually looks like, measured across the 60 chunks in the Ruling-3 seeds.

**Every single chunk gets exactly the same amount: five practice sentences, five distinct sentence frames, no verbatim repeats.** That uniformity is the gap. The course gives a chunk whose Hindi now runs backwards against the English precisely as much practice as a chunk that was never disturbed — five sentences either way. On Kai's ruling these are the chunks that need *more* than the default, and not one of them has it.

Second, the sentences are short. The median chunk gets four extra words of English around it. Twenty-five of the sixty get three or fewer.

Five chunks are thin enough to name, where the richest drill sentence in the set adds three words or less — the learner never produces them inside a real sentence at all:

- *"we need to"* (seed 104) — five sentences, two or three extra words each
- *"more important"* (seed 137) — same
- *"he'd like to know"* (seed 581) — five sentences, but three of them add only one word
- *"I'm happy so far"* (seed 187) — only three of its five sentences even contain the chunk
- *"why not"* (seed 113) — only **one** of five sentences contains the chunk at all

Then seventeen chunks are only ever practised at the end of the sentence — zero or one of their five sentences puts any English after them. Among them: *"enough words"*, *"last time"*, *"more important"*, *"woke earlier than"*, *"opposite the post office"* and *"if you'd told me"* all sit sentence-finally in five out of five. A learner drilled that way never has to place the chunk mid-sentence, which is the exact skill the reordered Hindi now demands.

I wrote no new phrases — that was outside this pass.

**One drill set is not thin but broken.** Seeds 5 and 25 are the two Ruling-2 seeds, and their twenty practice sentences all drill "I'm going to" / "are you going to" as a *detachable* marker glued onto the end of a Hindi verb — करने वाला हूँ appended English-style, as in बोलना करने वाला हूँ. The new Hindi is a suffix on the verb and cannot be appended to anything. Those twenty sentences cannot survive the ruling; they need rebuilding as a phrase-writing pass.

## The 43 that no ruling covers

These are not order problems and they are not fusion. In each one a meaning-bearing English word lost its Hindi outright, or the construction changed so the chunk has nothing left to sit on. **I fixed none of them** — they need your call on where the boundary now falls.

**Seed 26 — “I like feeling as if I'm nearly ready to go.”**  
The Hindi now says: मुझे यह अहसास अच्छा लग रहा है जैसे मैं जाने के लिए लगभग तैयार हूँ।  
The chunk "feeling as if" is now carried by अहसास at the front and जैसे four words later, and no contiguous run of those words is legitimate Hindi meaning "feeling as if" — so there is nothing to store as a citation form.

**Seed 35 — “She doesn't want to read anything this afternoon.”**  
The Hindi now says: वह आज दोपहर बाद कुछ नहीं पढ़ना चाहती।  
The negation moved into the object (कुछ नहीं पढ़ना), so "she doesn't want" has no नहीं of its own and "read anything" has flipped polarity to "read nothing" — a meaning change, not an order change.

**Seed 55 — “I don't enjoy waking up when I didn't sleep very well.”**  
The Hindi now says: जब अच्छी नींद नहीं आती तो मुझे जागना अच्छा नहीं लगता।  
The "didn't sleep very well" clause became present habitual (अच्छी नींद नहीं आती) while the English stays past — the chunk no longer answers the tense the English teaches.

**Seed 71 — “We didn't want to let anyone hear the truth.”**  
The Hindi now says: हम नहीं चाहते थे कि कोई सच सुने।  
The causative was rewritten away: देना is gone entirely, so "we didn't want to let anyone" has no Hindi for the "let" at all.

**Seed 100 — “You shouldn't worry about doing something similar.”**  
The Hindi now says: आप ऐसा ही कुछ करने की चिंता न करें।  
चाहिए is replaced by a subjunctive न करें, leaving "you shouldn't" with only a bare न — the modal has no Hindi word left.

**Seed 116 — “This isn't the best choice I could make.”**  
The Hindi now says: मैं इससे बेहतर विकल्प चुन सकता था।  
Recast as a positive comparative (मैं इससे बेहतर विकल्प चुन सकता था), so neither "this isn't" nor "the best choice" survives in any form.

**Seed 118 — “I feel better than I felt when we were in the pub.”**  
The Hindi now says: मुझे उससे बेहतर लग रहा है जब हम पब में थे।  
The new sentence has no verb of feeling at all (महसूस is gone), so the chunk glossed "felt" has nothing to attach to.

**Seed 133 — “You get to know someone very well when you work together.”**  
The Hindi now says: जब आप किसी के साथ काम करते हैं तो उसे अच्छी तरह जान जाते हैं।  
बहुत is gone so "very well" is now just "well", and साथ has migrated to किसी के साथ so "together" has no Hindi — two meaning-bearing English words lost their counterparts.

**Seed 134 — “It's not a problem when you work at something difficult with them.”**  
The Hindi now says: उनके साथ किसी मुश्किल चीज़ पर काम करने में समस्या नहीं आती है।  
The "when you…" clause became a nominalised "in working" (काम करने में), so "when you work with them" has no clause left to map onto.

**Seed 135 — “I don't know why you think that it's so good.”**  
The Hindi now says: पता नहीं यह आपको इतना अच्छा क्यों लगता है।  
"I don't know" would now have to be stored as पता नहीं — which is already the Hindi for "I wonder if" in S289, so taking it would create a second valid answer for the same prompt.

**Seed 143 — “It's the same thing as we were talking about earlier.”**  
The Hindi now says: हम पहले इसी बारे में हम बात कर रहे थे।  
वही चीज़ is replaced by a bare इसी, so "the same thing" has no Hindi. Separately, her new sentence repeats हम twice — a typo worth fixing whatever is ruled here.

**Seed 147 — “She was very kind when she saw me feeling nervous.”**  
The Hindi now says: मुझे घबराते देखकर वह बहुत उदार हो गई थी।  
"when she saw me" became a participle (देखकर) welded onto the first clause — there is no जब clause left, so the chunk has no boundary to sit on.

**Seed 150 — “Can you tell me what your name is?”**  
The Hindi now says: क्या आप मुझे अपना नाम बता सकते हैं?  
The embedded question is gone: "what your name is" shrinks to अपना नाम, which means "your name" and not "what your name is".

**Seed 151 — “That wasn't what I was hoping would happen.”**  
The Hindi now says: मुझे उम्मीद नहीं थी कि ऐसा होगा।  
The negation moved onto "hoping", so "that wasn't" has no Hindi at all and "I was hoping that would happen" has become negative against a positive English gloss.

**Seed 158 — “Let's talk about something else.”**  
The Hindi now says: चलिए कुछ और बात करते हैं।  
के बारे में is gone, so the chunk glossed "about something else" has no Hindi for "about" — it shrank to a bare कुछ और.

**Seed 197 — “My son works as a teacher.”**  
The Hindi now says: मेरा बेटा एक शिक्षक है।  
The sentence became a plain equative (मेरा बेटा एक शिक्षक है), so both "works" and "as a teacher" have vanished.

**Seed 214 — “Did you have a good time at the weekend?”**  
The Hindi now says: क्या आपने अच्छा सप्ताहांत बिताया?  
समय is gone so "a good time" has no Hindi, and में is gone so "at the weekend" has lost its postposition — the weekend became the object of the verb.

**Seed 225 — “He would give you an answer if he could.”**  
The Hindi now says: अगर वह आपको जवाब दे सकता, तो दे देता।  
The generic कर सकता is replaced by the lexical दे सकता, so nothing contiguous in the new sentence means "if he could" on its own.

**Seed 229 — “That woman would help you if she could.”**  
The Hindi now says: अगर वह औरत आपकी मदद कर सकती, तो कर देती।  
The whole conditional is rebuilt as अगर … कर सकती, तो कर देती — a different construction, so the single chunk "would help you if she could" has no counterpart to be reordered into.

**Seed 289 — “I wonder if she's going to be there this afternoon.”**  
The Hindi now says: पता नहीं वह आज दोपहर वहाँ होगी या नहीं।  
"I wonder if" is now पता नहीं at the front plus या नहीं at the end, and the only contiguous candidate — पता नहीं — already means "I don't know" elsewhere in the course.

**Seed 297 — “I don't know many people who speak English.”**  
The Hindi now says: मैं अंग्रेज़ी बोलने वाले ज़्यादा लोगों को नहीं जानता।  
"not many people" now depends on the verb's नहीं (ज़्यादा लोगों को नहीं जानता), so the chunk cannot stand on its own as a noun phrase.

**Seed 315 — “I think that he couldn't afford the car that he wanted.”**  
The Hindi now says: मुझे लगता है कि वह उस कार को नहीं ख़रीद सकता जो उसे पसंद थी।  
The tense shifted to present (नहीं ख़रीद सकता) while the English stays past, and चाहता था became पसंद थी — the two chunks now compete for the same words.

**Seed 339 — “No I think he's hurt himself quite badly.”**  
The Hindi now says: नहीं, मुझे लगता है उसे बहुत बुरी तरह चोट लगी है।  
The verb went intransitive (चोट लगी है), so "himself" has no Hindi word left at all — खुद को is simply gone.

**Seed 346 — “I wanted her to know that I liked her book.”**  
The Hindi now says: मैं उसे बताना चाहता था कि मुझे उसकी किताब पसंद आई।  
"know" was replaced by "tell" (बताना), which lands inside the "I wanted her to" chunk, so the original chunk has nothing to map to.

**Seed 370 — “I didn't see anyone that I knew.”**  
The Hindi now says: मुझे अपना कोई जानने वाला नहीं दिखा।  
The relative clause collapsed into a participial phrase (अपना कोई जानने वाला), merging "didn't see anyone" and "that I knew" into one inseparable run.

**Seed 379 — “Yes I was lucky enough to travel to Africa.”**  
The Hindi now says: हाँ, मैं भाग्यशाली था कि अफ़्रीका जा सका।  
इतना is gone so "lucky enough" has lost its "enough", and "to travel to" is now a finite जा सका inside a कि-clause rather than a chunk.

**Seed 396 — “We don't need to stand until everybody else is ready.”**  
The Hindi now says: हमें बाक़ी सबके तैयार होने तक खड़े होने की ज़रूरत नहीं है।  
जब तक is gone — "until" is now a तक suffixed to the whole clause, so the chunk has no word of its own.

**Seed 407 — “Shouldn't we try to set a good example?”**  
The Hindi now says: क्या हमें अच्छा उदाहरण नहीं रखना चाहिए?  
कोशिश disappears altogether, so "shouldn't we try" has no Hindi verb of trying to point at.

**Seed 414 — “Could we have a bottle of red wine please?”**  
The Hindi now says: क्या हमें एक बोतल रेड वाइन देंगे?  
Both मिल सकती है and कृपया are gone, so "could we have" and "please" have no counterparts in the new sentence.

**Seed 448 — “Yes they'll be happy to help.”**  
The Hindi now says: हाँ, उन्हें मदद करके ख़ुशी होगी।  
The construction went dative (उन्हें ख़ुशी होगी), so "they'll be" is split around a noun and there is no copula to carry it.

**Seed 474 — “I don't even want it as a free offer.”**  
The Hindi now says: मुझे यह मुफ़्त ऑफ़र में भी नहीं चाहिए।  
के रूप में is gone, so the chunk glossed "as" has no Hindi left.

**Seed 478 — “She has such a kind heart.”**  
The Hindi now says: वह बहुत दयालु है।  
दिल disappears — "a kind heart" has nothing to map to in वह बहुत दयालु है.

**Seed 479 — “It's the least I could do.”**  
The Hindi now says: मैं इतना तो कर ही सकता था।  
कम से कम is gone; "the least" is now carried by an idiom (इतना तो … ही) that no chunk boundary matches.

**Seed 485 — “Nothing would make me happier than to get away.”**  
The Hindi now says: यहाँ से दूर जाने से ज़्यादा ख़ुशी मुझे और किसी बात से नहीं मिलेगी।  
"would make me" is now मुझे … नहीं मिलेगी with the whole comparison between, and the adjective "happier" became the noun ख़ुशी.

**Seed 558 — “It's so late at night.”**  
The Hindi now says: बहुत रात हो चुकी है।  
इतनी देर is gone — "so late" is now carried by बहुत रात हो चुकी है, where no run corresponds to the chunk.

**Seed 561 — “I don't care if it's a fast car.”**  
The Hindi now says: मुझे परवाह नहीं कि कार तेज़ है या नहीं।  
The adjective went predicative (कार तेज़ है), so "a fast car" is no longer a noun phrase at all.

**Seed 573 — “It's the kind of thing that makes the holidays very special.”**  
The Hindi now says: इस तरह की चीज़ें छुट्टियों को बहुत ख़ास बना देती हैं।  
जो disappears and the sentence is pluralised, so "that makes" has no relative clause and "the kind of thing" has changed number.

**Seed 593 — “However much I argued I still had to share.”**  
The Hindi now says: इतनी बहस करने के बावजूद, मुझे बाँटना ही पड़ा।  
"however much I argued" became a concessive nominal (के बावजूद), so चाहे … कितना भी has no counterpart.

**Seed 602 — “How it started in the first place.”**  
The Hindi now says: इस सबकी शुरुआत कैसे हुई।  
The verb was nominalised to शुरुआत and "in the first place" vanished entirely.

**Seed 604 — “She offered to let us stay with her.”**  
The Hindi now says: उसने पेशकश की कि हम उसके साथ रह सकते हैं।  
The single chunk "offered to let us stay" splits into पेशकश की plus a separate कि-clause, and the causative देने is gone.

**Seed 608 — “It would have been the sensible thing to do.”**  
The Hindi now says: ऐसा करना समझदारी होती।  
"the sensible thing" shrinks to a bare समझदारी and "it would have been" has no verb of its own left.

**Seed 615 — “I thought you were very brave to say that.”**  
The Hindi now says: मेरा ख़्याल है कि आपका ऐसा कहना बहादुरी थी।  
"very brave" became the noun बहादुरी and बहुत disappeared, so the chunk has no adjective to map to.

**Seed 616 — “You were very brave to say you thought that.”**  
The Hindi now says: ऐसा कहना आपकी बहादुरी थी कि आप ऐसा सोचते हैं।  
The relative clause became a complement clause with a tense shift to present, so "you thought" no longer corresponds to जो आपने सोचा.

## Two things found on the way

**A word she changed that nobody asked about.** Seed 143's new Hindi reads हम पहले इसी बारे में हम बात कर रहे थे — हम appears twice. That is a typo in her edit, not a judgement, and it should be fixed whatever is ruled about the seed's boundaries.

**A clash the ruling would have created.** Seed 135's *"I don't know"* and seed 289's *"I wonder if"* both want to become पता नहीं under their new Hindi. Two different English answers behind one Hindi prompt is exactly what the zero-uncertainty rule forbids, so I refused the rewrite on both and filed them as residue. They need one decision between them, not two separate ones.

Rewriting the known side unlinked the audio for all 28 chunks — 28 Hindi prompt clips and 28 presentation clips, 56 in total, currently silent. Nothing old was deleted.

## What I did not touch

The 251 mechanical seeds, and the practice phrases. Where a chunk's Hindi changed, the sentences drilling it still carry the old spelling — a course-wide re-gloss that belongs to the mechanical pass, not here. The nukta corrections alone (ख़ुश, काफ़ी, क़मीज़, दिमाग़, परफ़ेक्ट) reach more than two hundred practice phrases, most of them in seeds that pass owns.
