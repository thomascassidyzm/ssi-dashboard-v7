## What was actually wrong

Three separate defects, not one.

1. **A filler the voice read aloud.** The generator emitted `ඒ ගෙ` into slots it couldn't fill, 3–9 times per clip, and the voice spoke every one. Learners heard *"ay ge ay ge ay ge"*. Removing it cut **41.2 seconds** of nonsense from 27 clips alone.
2. **`මමා` for `මම`** — "mamaa" instead of "I". Voiced on 73 clips. Proved spoken by reading the provider's own per-token log, then by measuring the loudness of that exact window in the bytes a learner downloads: louder than the clip average. Speech, not a glitch.
3. **Stale clips.** Cards were repaired months ago; their audio was never rebuilt. On 24 of the first 27, the learner *read* one word and *heard* a different one — one card taught "works as a teacher" while the clip said **"son"**.

Underneath, a quieter one: **~18 seed sentences and 42 practice phrases carried Sinhala the course never teaches** — including one seed that said *"walked"* where its own English said *"woke"*.

## Where we were wrong, and caught it

This is the part worth your attention, because it is why I'd trust the numbers above.

- **A worker refused its own instructions.** Told to apply a prepared fix to seed 181, it found the fix reused a word the course doesn't teach until **seed 469** — 288 seeds later. It substituted the word taught at seed 19 instead. That one catch exposed the same fault in **7 other seeds**, all now fixed.
- **A "fix" that would have made things worse.** I proposed replacing the stray `ගෙ` with `ගැන` ("about"). Tested against all 24 rows, it failed on most: it would have turned *"to a restaurant"* into *"about a restaurant"* and produced `ගැන ගැන` in one row. Not applied.
- **A test that was measuring the wrong thing.** A duration check kept failing one clip. It turned out the course's `...` convention is voiced as a real pause the check didn't model — it was failing **92% of good clips** in that class. Fixed the check, not the clip.
- **Six words wrongly condemned.** `මමතා`, `අරගෙන`, `හැරෙනවා`, `ළිහිල්`, `දිකිනකොට`, `කියන්නද` were all called corrupt because they were rare. They are real Sinhala. Rarity is not corruption, and we stopped using it as evidence.
- **One repair broke something.** Fixing card text silently removed the audio from 9 cards. Caught by checking rather than by being told, and all 9 restored.

**Every change to a seed or card was checked by a second, independent agent** whose job was to disprove it. On the highest-risk item that second agent declined to confirm one claim and said so.

## What is still open

**Nothing is broken and hidden.** These are known and measured.

1. **Seed 226 — 7 phrases with English words in the Sinhala prompt** (`the මිනිහා`). Held deliberately: the card teaches a distinction English marks and Sinhala doesn't, so no wording satisfies the one-prompt-one-answer rule. **This is a design decision on that seed, not a repair.**
2. **`S0382L04` — a card glossed "did you hear" whose own seed says "ask".** Sinhala `අහනවා` means both. Three fixes exist, each with a cost. My recommendation: move seed 382 to `විමසන්න`, the distinct "ask" verb the course already teaches at seed 99.
3. **137 places where a word is used before it is formally taught.** Measured, none confirmed harmful — most are ordinary word-endings. Pre-existing, not created today: of ~230 edits today, exactly **one** introduced such a breach, and it was found and fixed.
4. **The presentation template is itself malformed** — it renders an illegal Sinhala vowel sequence across **all ~1,300** presentation clips. Cosmetic in the data, worth a proper look.
5. **600 of 1,170 healthy clips no longer match their card text** — stale, not corrupt. The next thing I'd fix.
6. **59 cards have no presentation audio.** Pre-existing, unrelated to today.
