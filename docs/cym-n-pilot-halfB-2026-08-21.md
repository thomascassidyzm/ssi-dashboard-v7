## 2. Known-side controlled-language cost

This is the thing word-coverage is structurally blind to, and the reason the pilot exists. Seven of my 20 seeds need an English construction the kept range never debuted.

**Substantial (needs a carrier debut before the seed is usable):**

1. **Canon 482 — "The only X is that Y."** A noun-phrase subject taking a that-clause complement through the copula. The kept range attaches that-clauses only to verbs of saying/thinking/needing (S0080L02 "she said that", S0024L02 + S0031L04 "I think it's…", S0174L02 "we need"). The identificatory frame is new English. **+2 legos** (a carrier such as "the problem is that…" plus its copular hinge).
2. **Canon 582 — "What's it like to X?"** Dummy-it + *like* + infinitive. The kept range has *like* only as a comparative preposition (S0140L02 `fel`) and as the verb *to like* (S0090L01). **+1 to +2 legos.**
3. **Canon 522 — hortative "Let's …" plus extraposed "it was ADJ to VERB."** Half-debuted: "let me" (S0261L01) and "shall we" (S0229L01/S0233L01) are close cousins; "it's important" (S0127L01) and "it's possible" (S0131L01) give the extraposition frame in the present. **+1 lego.**

**Mild (a new English word arriving with its own lego, no extra carrier):** 542 "whenever"; 362 "rather" as a degree modifier; 662 "you all"; 642 the vocative "madam". **+0 to +1 each, ≈ +3 total.**

**Estimated extra cost for half B: +7 to +9 legos** on top of the 54 — about **+13%**.

**Separately — target-side construction debt, which is not a known-side gap but is a real build cost.** Three constructions appear in the canon tail that the kept range never teaches, even though their English is perfectly ordinary:
- **Negative bod-clause** (`bod hi ddim isio` 302, `bod nhw ddim` 482). Kept range has affirmative embedded clauses only (`bo' ti'n`, `bo' ni angen`, `bo' hi'n`). **+1 shared carrier lego.**
- **Infixed object pronoun** (`isio'i roi o` 382, `wedi'i dorri o` 622). Kept range uses the postposed form (`rhoi fo iddo fo`, S0224L01). **+1 shared carrier lego.**
- **Response-form yes/no** (`nac oes` 282, `nag oedd` 362, `ydan` 402). See §3.2 — this is a policy problem, not a lego problem.

## 3. ZUT findings

### 3.1 Hard collision — "there"
Canon 562 maps **there → `yna`**. Kept **S0123L02** maps **there → `yno`**. Same known prompt, two target forms, production direction. This must be resolved course-wide before either form is atomised. Recommendation: STANDARDISE on the kept form `yno` (the kept range is the fixed point; canon is the newcomer) — but note the canon Welsh here is 10/10 agreed across the ten `*_for_cym` copies, so it is not a typo, it is a genuine northern variant. **Tom's call, flagged, not actioned.**

### 3.2 Hard collision class — bare "yes" / "no"
Within my 20 seeds alone: **no → `nac oes`** (282) and **no → `nag oedd`** (362); **yes → `ydan`** (402). Welsh response forms agree with the question's verb, so a bare English "yes"/"no" gloss can never be ZUT-clean. The kept range already handles this correctly and instinctively — it never glosses a bare yes/no, only bound forms with their carrier: "yes, I enjoyed it" (S0092L01), "yes, I'm ready" (S0125L01), "no, we don't have" (S0148L01), "yes, we've got" (S0149L01).

**Ruling this pilot recommends:** the response particle is a construction-feature, never a unit of intention. It must never be atomised. Absorb it into an M-lego with its carrier and gloss the whole thought. Applies to a large slice of the canon tail — every seed opening with a bare Yes/No.

### 3.3 Register near-collision, resolved by the vocative — canon 642
"how do you feel?" is already taught in the *ti* form (S0103L01 `sut wyt ti'n teimlo?`). Canon 642 wants the *chi* form. The vocative `madam` is what makes both ZUT-legal, and it is doctrinally the *only* permitted disambiguator (no parentheses, no register annotation). This is a worked confirmation that the tu-first + vocative-marking rules combine correctly on real canon material. **Requirement:** the vocative must reach the learner-facing gloss; a gloss of "you feel" alone would be a hard collision with both S0103L01 and S0193L01.

### 3.4 Form variance — same chunk, two target spellings
Not ZUT collisions in the strict sense (the knowns differ), but ZUT-fatal the moment either side is atomised. Five instances in 20 seeds:

| Canon | Canon form | Kept form | lego_id |
|---|---|---|---|
| 302 | `ddeudodd` | `ddudodd` | S0080L02 |
| 442 | `oedden` | `oeddan` | S0186L01 |
| 522 | `ei bod hi'n` | `bo' hi'n` | S0031L04 |
| 602 | `nath` | `wnaeth` | S0078L03, S0259L03 |
| 622 | `gwbod` | `gwybod` | S0052L01 |

Plus 662 `ei siarad hi` vs kept `siarad hi` (S0033L01).

At 5–6 instances per 20 seeds, extrapolating across 401 canon seeds gives roughly **100–120 variance points**. This is a single mechanical normalisation pass against the kept range's orthography, not per-seed authoring work — and it should be run *before* decomposition, not after, or every one of them becomes a ZUT rejection at submission time.

### 3.5 Reverse convergence — noted, no action
Harmless (many knowns → one target is reception, not production): "that's not" → `dydy hynna ddim yn` alongside kept "that doesn't" (S0262L02); "to put" → `rhoi` alongside kept "to give" (S0224L01); "rather" → `eitha` alongside kept "fairly" (S0111L01, S0238L01).

## 4. Fragment seeds — a build cost the coverage measure also missed

Three of my 20 seeds (**422** "A question."; **542** "Whenever you feel angry."; **602** "How it started in the first place.") are **not complete sentences**. Two of them arrive lowercase and unpunctuated in canon, which is corroborating evidence they were authored as clause fragments rather than sentences.

Under the floors, every lego needs **≥5 USE phrases and USE must be complete natural sentences, never fragments**. A fragment seed cannot generate its own USE phrases; the USE has to be built by extending the fragment with other legos. That is not fatal — it is exactly the BUILD/USE distinction — but it means these seeds cost *more* phrase-authoring effort per lego, not less, despite looking trivially cheap on a word count. **15% of my sample.**

## 5. Verdict on the crude word-coverage approximation

The crude measure predicted **85 of 401 canon seeds fully covered ≈ 21%**. I measured **2 of 20 = 10%**.

**The crude measure over-counted coverage by roughly 2×.** Why, in order of size:

1. **It cannot see paradigm gaps.** A taught `oeddan nhw isio` does not give you `oedd o isio` (382), `hi angen` (322), `hi ddim isio` (302), `roedd o'n` (362) or `nath o` (602). The words are all "covered"; the forms are not taught. This alone accounts for five of my 20 seeds.
2. **It cannot see constructions.** Infixed object pronouns, negative bod-clauses, response-form yes/no, identificatory copula + that-clause. Every word present, the sentence still unteachable without a carrier debut.
3. **It is structurally blind to the known side.** 7 of 20 seeds (35%) carry an English construction the kept course never debuted. A word-coverage check on the *Welsh* cannot detect an *English* gap by construction — this is precisely the blind spot the pilot was built to measure, and it is worth **+13%** on top of the lego count.
4. **A word bound inside a taught M-lego is not an available lego.** `go iawn` sits inside S0140L03, `tyfu i fyny` inside S0154L02/S0161L01, `cwestiwn` inside S0252L03. Coverage says taught; usability says it depends. These were my hardest calls (§6).

**It under-counts in exactly one direction**, and this is the honest counterweight: Welsh mutation and orthographic drift make already-taught items look new. `broblem`/`problem`, `rywbeth`/`rhywbeth`, `nes i`/`wnes i`, `gwbod`/`gwybod`, `nath`/`wnaeth` — I found 5–6 in 20 seeds. A naive string match would have called every one of those NEW. **This is why the pilot's instruction to match on meaning and form rather than string equality was the whole exercise: a string-match count would have landed near 3.0 new/seed by *double-counting mutations as new* while simultaneously *missing* the paradigm and construction gaps — right answer, wrong reasons, and no way to tell which.**

Net: **overcount of coverage dominates comfortably.** Plan on roughly **half** the fully-covered seeds the crude estimate promised.

## 7. What was NOT done (explicit gaps)

- **No decomposition was submitted anywhere.** This is measurement only. Nothing written to `cym_n_for_eng`, to `cym_n_for_eng_new`, or to any other course, in any database.
- **No phrases were authored.** The phrase counts above are floor-derived arithmetic (4 BUILD + 5 USE per lego), not measured. Actual authoring effort per lego was not sampled.
- **I did not verify the consensus Welsh against the source `*_for_cym` courses myself.** I took `sampleB.tsv` as given; all 20 rows carried 10/10 agreement. The canon Welsh remains a machine batch of 2026-07-16 never proofread by a human, and this pilot does not change that.
- **Half A (the other 20 seeds) is not covered here.** The two halves are directly comparable by construction; a combined 40-seed number needs both.
- **Syllable counts were reasoned, not mechanically measured.** No proposed lego looked close to the 8-syllable ceiling, but I did not run a counter.
