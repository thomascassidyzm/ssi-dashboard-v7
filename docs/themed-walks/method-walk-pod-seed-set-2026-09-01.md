# The canonical Method walk pod-seed set — the discussant's floor

**Authored 2026-09-01.** This is the canonical **English known-side** pod-seed set for the
Method walk — the first selected themed walk, the Bologna consumable, and the artefact every
language pair's Method walk production layer is built from. There is one canonical set, so it
is identical for every pair by definition. **It contains no target language of any kind.** The
Italian in `canonical_pod_scenarios.target_text` was read as read-only evidence that the walk
is real and rendered; not one Italian word below is output. The target side is a pair overlay
and a separate downstream mapping job, exactly as seed translation always was.

This is the third run of the act that produced the canonical health seed set
(`docs/sector-pods/health-general-seed-set-2026-08-31.md`) and the canonical CORE pod set /
every-optional-walk floor (`docs/pods/core-pod-seed-set-2026-08-31.md`). Those two are the
format and the standard; where this corpus forced the pattern to bend, the bend is stated in
place and collected in the companion pattern-deltas report for the four walks that follow.

**Why this walk exists as a production layer.** The Method Pod is a listening walk — its own
design doc says "heard is not owned." These pod-seeds are what make the Italian trial a
PRODUCTION trial rather than a listening one: under Tom's ruling, every pod line delivered as
a known/target pair is an established mapping, so what the learner is missing is not the
material but the handle — and a handle is a cut.

**The rulings this set is authored under** (Tom, 2026-08-31/09-01, none re-openable):

- **Seeds are immutable.** The 668 canonical seeds are untouched; nothing is interleaved.
- **If it's been in a dialogue, they own it.** ZUT is determinism of the mapping, not drill
  count. Pod material needs a CUT, not a new admission event.
- **A pod sentence, intact, is a legitimate basket phrase** wherever it contains the LEGO
  being taught. Intact reuse is legal anywhere in its own seed; NOVEL composition reaching a
  later sibling is a forward reference and is not.
- **Never cut below a phrase.** Single meaningful units of intention are phrases; the
  forbidden atoms are grammar fragments that are not moves.
- **Canonical layer only:** English known side. No target language, no pair, no course code,
  no dialect. Divergence in the known side is caused by cutting; pods do not cut, so this set
  is pair-invariant by construction.
- **The selector is interest, not occupation.** This is a themed walk; nothing below calls it
  a sector.

## 1. The projection — two mouths, roles derived over acts, not speakers

**What roles does this corpus even offer?** Two mouths: ARAN and TOM, named peers, in every
row of both arms. There are no drill rows, no narrator codas, no service counter, no
patient — the three role-anchors the health and CORE projections hung on simply do not exist
here. And the mouths do not partition the work: Aran probes ("But?", "Was it?", "Tell me")
and holds forth (the cliff, the transcripts); Tom holds forth (the city, the atoms) and
probes ("How do we know that?", "Since when?"). In health, role could be read off the
speaker tag. **Here role must be derived over ACTS**, because both chairs do both jobs —
this corpus's own design doc calls the underlying material "the zero-asymmetry pole."

Two roles, derived:

1. **discussant** — the person holding up their end of an ideas conversation: the probe, the
   ratification, the reformulation and its verdicts, the repair, the digression and its
   policing, the anecdote contract, the disagreement register (pause vs standing), the debt
   and its recovery, the callback, and the learner's own testimony about learning. Every
   discussant act below is attested in BOTH mouths, which is the demonstration that it is
   role-symmetric: whichever chair the learner occupies, these are their turns.
2. **explainer** — everything in discussant, PLUS the method's claims as sustained
   first-person exposition: the advocate who re-teaches the method (the map of Rome, the
   transcript ladder, the group-dynamic story at full length). **Discussant is a strict 100%
   subset of explainer** — the same relation health ruled between general and nurse.

**This set extracts the discussant projection.** The explainer's long holds stay receptive —
heard, owned as mappings, and available to a future explainer pass exactly as the nurse
segment waits behind general. One deliberate reach into explainer territory is made and
bounded: where the corpus attests a method claim in short worn form — "You don't need all
the words", "There is no such thing as a word", "No form. Press play", "It's a hundred
hours" — the claim mints, because a learner who chose this walk by interest will want to SAY
the method's one-liners, and the corpus itself delivers them at discussant length. The test
applied per line: *would the learner, holding up their end of a conversation about how
languages are learned, need to produce this turn essentially as written?* Long expository
holds, speaker-specific biography (Aran's Manx film, the negative-woman story, the garden
neighbour, the banking exposition, the IP email) and studio business (the recording, the
hands) fail it and stay receptive; their reusable frames mint from their shortest attested
carriers instead.

**Reconciliation.** The ledger (§6) accounts every row of both arms: minted-or-split,
consolidated (same line, or same act in the other arm), deferred to the floor or to scene 0,
or receptive. The counts are stated there and verified mechanically (§7).

## 2. The anchor — chosen

**Decision: the Method walk anchors at the end of CORE scene 0 — the earliest position the
design allows — and the anchor's binding contract is the assumed-ownership inventory in
Appendix A.** Same functional form as the health set's anchor, and for the same reason: the
anchor gates when the thread can open, and pushing it early bounds nothing this set needs.

What is different here, stated rather than hidden: **this walk's Appendix A is heavier than
health's.** The discussant register leans on opinion and epistemic frames ("I think",
"I don't know", "I want"), on the full number/day/clock coda inventory (the hundred-hours
ladder, the scheduling scene), and on comparison glue ("like", "than"). All of it is
early-core in any base course — no item in Appendix A is later than the coda class the base
courses cut in their opening rounds — so the anchor does not move: scene 0 complete, plus
Appendix A owned, bound by each pair overlay to the earliest lego id at or after
scene-0-complete by which Appendix A is owned. Anything a particular base course does not own
there, that pair's overlay authors in-pair as `is_new = true`; the canonical set does not
move. The every-optional-walk floor (CP001–CP195) is not a prerequisite: it TRAVELS with
this walk, so floor chunks enter every ownership statement below as owned by construction.

## 4. The spine — the ordered seed set

**Reading the entries.** Each seed carries: its verbatim source line with row id — `cNN` is
`global_order` in `method-pod-chapters`, `sNN` in `method-pod-43-scene`, `= sNN` marks a
verbatim-shared line; the canonical English seed; the phrase-minimum cut — `chunk` **(new)**
admits a LEGO, `chunk` *(MWnnn)* is owned by an earlier seed in this set, `chunk` *(CPnnn)*
is every-optional-walk floor property, `chunk` *(core)* is an assumed course-core string
(Appendix A); the derived job — what it admits that no earlier seed did, over new LEGOs and
new frames; and the ownership statement, implicit in the cut's citations and stated where it
needs a word. Where the canonical seed differs from its source line, the edit and its reason
are stated. **Sides**: a chunk cited as a *side* of an owned chunk (person-flip, tense-flip,
elision) is an owned mapping's other face, never a new admission — the pair overlay
re-verifies target tiling and mints an in-pair cut where target morphology resists (CORE
decision 8, carried here). Sequencing is by **admission economy, not walk order**: the pod
has delivered everything already, so each seed admits as little as possible, and
zero-admission seeds — marked **ZERO** — are manufactured wherever the corpus allows.

Glue — bare "and", "but", "so", "or", "well", "just", "too", "very", "now", "also", "again",
"though", "then", "still", "about", "like", "actually", "after that", "each", "mm", "oh",
personal names, place names and language names as slot content — is course-core and never
minted (Appendix A). A chunk marked **(new)** always includes its own glue when the worn
form carries it.
