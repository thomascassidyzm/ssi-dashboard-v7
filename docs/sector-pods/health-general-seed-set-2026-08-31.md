# The canonical health seed set — general role (the ward floor)

**Authored 2026-08-31.** This is the canonical **English known-side** seed set for the health
sector's general-role segment — the artefact every language pair's health segment is built from.
There is one canonical set, so it is identical for every pair by definition. **It contains no
target language of any kind.** The target side is a pair overlay and a separate downstream
mapping job, exactly as seed translation always was. The corpus's own Welsh interjections
appear below only inside verbatim quotations of Aran's source turns.

**Source (read-only, unedited):** `docs/sector-pods/source/health-sector-conversations-v3.md`
— Aran's hand-authored dialogue, 438 turns, 219 healthcare-worker (HW) turns. Turn numbering is
deterministic: turn N is the Nth `**HW:**`/`**P:**` bullet in file order, verified mechanically
against the role map's attested quotes.

**Scope (ruled, not re-litigated):** general role only — the first-person projection of the
turns a general health worker / HCA / ward assistant produces. Patient turns are context, not
seeds. General is a strict 100% subset of nurse, so nothing here is ever re-authored when nurse
follows.

**What this feeds:** the pair-overlay mapping job for each pair; the registration of the health
segment against the cross-course union-ZUT gate. The ownership statements in §4 are what that
gate will check.

## 1. The projection — 85 turns, reconciled exactly

The role map (job #488) composed general as 44 three-way-core turns + 41 ward-floor turns = 85,
but noted its section recipe was an approximation: the five ward-floor sections hold 45 HW
turns, so four were excluded by hand and unrecorded. I re-derived the projection per turn with
the role map's own question — *would a learner working as a general health worker need to
produce this turn, essentially as written, allowing only the swap of a self-identifying role
word?* — and landed on **85 exactly**, with the four exclusions identified:

| Block | Turns | Count |
|---|---|---|
| §1.0 linguistic opener, nurse sequence | #1–#35 odd | 18 |
| §1.1 names and first meeting | #37–#53 odd | 9 |
| §1.2 settling in | #55, #57, #59, #61, #63, #65, #67, #69 | 8 |
| §1.3 observations | #73–#89 odd | 9 |
| §1.4 pain assessment | #91, #97, #99, #103, #107 | 5 |
| §1.7 meals and drinking | #145, #147, #149, #151, #153, #155, #157 | 7 |
| §1.8 mobility | #163, #165, #167, #169, #173, #175, #177, #179 | 8 |
| §1.9 comfort round | #181–#197 odd | 9 |
| §2.0 linguistic opener, doctor sequence | #235–#257 odd | 12 |
| **Total** | | **85** |

**The four exclusions, and why they are a class, not a scatter:**

- **#71** — "You can, of course. I'll ask the doctor to write something up, just in case you
  need it." Requesting a prescription.
- **#159** — "…When did the sickness start? I can get you something for it, and maybe we try a
  bit of dry toast." Offering an anti-emetic.
- **#161** — "I'll get the anti-sickness sorted first, then the toast. And I'll let the doctor
  know…" Administering anti-sickness medication.
- **#171** — "…Dizziness when you stand can be the tablets, or just lying down too long - I'll
  check your blood pressure sitting and standing." Clinical interpretation of a medication
  effect, and an orthostatic-obs decision.

Every excluded turn touches medicines. **The general worker's boundary is the medicine
cabinet:** they ask about pain, take observations, serve meals, walk patients and run the
comfort round, but they never offer, promise, request or interpret medication. The five §1.4
inclusions respect the same line — #91/#97/#103 ask and relay ("Would you like something for
it?" is asking a preference, not administering), #99 reframes the scale, #107 reassures;
#93/#95/#101/#105 all promise or fetch medication and stay with the nurse.

This reconciles the role map's 44 + 41 = 85 to the turn. The 44: all of §1.0 (18) and §2.0
(12), all of §1.1 (9), and five from §1.4. The 41: the five ward-floor sections minus the four
medicine-touching turns.

## 2. The core anchor — chosen

**Decision: the segment anchors at the end of CORE scene 0 — the earliest position the helix
design allows — and the anchor's binding contract is the assumed-ownership inventory in
Appendix A.**

The sector helix makes the anchor the core position a segment is authored against: every core
chunk owned up to the anchor enters the segment as `is_new = false` — tiling and vocabulary, no
round, no learning event. The canonical set is pair-unbound, so it cannot name a lego id in any
base course. What it can do — and what this document does — is fix the anchor *functionally*:

1. **Scene 0 complete.** The learner has walked W1201–W1204 (the medium contract: the offer,
   the repair licence, the decline survived, the biography diversion recovered). Every seed
   below treats scene 0's acts and chunks as owned.
2. **Appendix A owned.** Every seed's ownership statement names the exact everyday-English
   chunks it assumes from core; Appendix A is their union. A pair overlay binds this anchor to
   the earliest lego id in its base course, at or after scene-0-complete, by which Appendix A
   is owned. Anything in Appendix A that a particular base course does not own by then, that
   pair's overlay authors in-segment as `is_new = true` — the canonical set does not move.

**Why the earliest anchor:** the helix's own rule is to push anchors as early as authoring
allows, because the anchor gates when the health thread opens and bounds overdrill for learners
past it. This segment is arrival-and-ward-floor language; its only hard prerequisite is the
medium contract itself, which scene 0 teaches. A later anchor buys nothing this set needs — the
everyday inventory it leans on is early-core in any pair — and delays every learner's entry to
the segment.

## 3. Scene 0 is the prologue — and the 30 opener turns resolved against it

unconditionally. The learner arrives at the health segment already having walked: the **offer**

diversion** answered and recovered ("On my own, mostly — a bit every day for about a year…"

register note strips as sector business: the duty-of-care stakes-raiser ("it's really important

## 4. The spine — the ordered seed set

**Reading the entries.** Each seed carries: its source turn, quoted verbatim with number and
section; the canonical English seed; the derived job — what it admits that no earlier seed did;
and the ownership statement — the exact chunks it assumes already owned, as strings. "Owned
(core)" strings are the Appendix A inventory; "owned (scene 0)" cites the walk step; "owned
(HGnn)" cites an earlier seed. Where a canonical seed differs from its source turn, the edit
and its reason are stated in the entry — otherwise the seed is the turn verbatim. The sequence
is ordered so each seed admits as little as possible; near-duplicate turns consolidate to one
canonical seed per act, because ZUT demands one production form per intention, and every
covered turn is named.

Phrase-minimum cuts and practice baskets are in §5, in the same order, as far as quality
allowed — the depth-before-breadth ruling.

## 5. Cuts and baskets — in order, as deep as quality allowed

**The rules these baskets follow.** The cut never goes below a phrase — atomising destroys the
worn whole chunk that is the pod's whole value — and the cut must tile its seed exactly. Only
**new** LEGOs get baskets: chunks owned from core or scene 0 enter as `is_new = false` — tiling
and vocabulary, no round, no learning event — so basketing them would be authoring overdrill.
Floors are the standing ones: **at least 4 BUILD and 5 USE per new LEGO** — fewer phrases is a
fail, and variety is a bonus on top of volume, never a substitute. BUILD phrases may be
fragments but must extend into natural full English by appending or prepending owned chunks;
USE phrases are complete natural sentences, tier-1 or they die. Every phrase composes only from
this seed's LEGO, chunks owned at this point in the sequence, and core glue — no forward
references, so a chunk taught by a later seed never appears in an earlier basket.
