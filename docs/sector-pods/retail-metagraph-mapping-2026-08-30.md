# Retail mapped onto the shape metagraph — the scripted-sector test

**2026-08-30.** The fourth sector, mapped in production, not as a pilot: the port question is
answered (trades, the far port, cost one node and two moves and the curve bent DOWN) and Tom has
licensed the fan-out. Retail was commissioned for a different question: **what does a HIGHLY
SCRIPTED sector do to the trunk?** Returns and refunds, price disputes, out-of-stock, escalation
to a manager, theft suspicion — high-frequency, low-stakes, corporately scripted encounters. The
interest is whether scriptedness means the sector reaches FEWER store nodes than an unscripted one,
and whether refusal-with-reason and challenge-the-premise still attest under a script. Three
sectors' mints concentrate in one place — where duty overrides consent — so the expected result
here is **zero or one mints**, and a sector that mints nothing is the trunk working.

**There is no corpus, and nothing below pretends otherwise.** Nobody has written down the
recurring spoken encounters of a shop worker. The inventory in §3 is generated by this mapping —
every entry labelled **derived-confident** (any competent retail worker has this conversation
weekly; no research needed to assert it exists) or **constructed** (reasoned into existence; may
not survive contact with a real Saturday girl). This is a derivation test, not an attestation
test: it can show what the trunk can and cannot EXPRESS for the sector; it cannot show
frequencies, and no percentage below pretends to be one.

*(Port verdict paragraph is written at §10 after the mapping; the prediction in §0 was committed
before any mapping was done — see the commit history of this file.)*

## 0. The prediction, written down and committed before any mapping was done

Stated now, frozen by commit, scored in §8. Priors: health minted 10 nodes; Ireland 2 nodes + 1
move; trades 1 node + 2 moves, the curve bending down; all mints concentrated where duty
overrides consent.

**P1 — mint count: ZERO nodes, zero moves — and I name the two refusals in advance.** Retail's
duty-override strand exists (the age-gate refusal, the licensing refusal) but it is REFUSAL, not
action against refused consent: no one at a till disconnects anything. The two tempting mints,
predicted refused: **(a) the innocent-out** — the theft challenge delivered as its innocent
counterpart ("did you forget to pay for the items in the bottom of the trolley?"), predicted to
fail the recorded position test (the repair position exists under a bare accusation too; the
frame only re-prices it) and to compose as F102's normalise + an elicit; **(b) the script-break**
— the confidential aside against the corporate voice ("I'm not supposed to say, but the sale
starts Thursday — wait"), predicted refused under Ireland's recorded rule that a licence is not a
position: it is F2's volunteered material wearing a licence flag. If either survives mapping as a
genuine mint, it is a move, not a node, and I will say the prediction was wrong.

**P2 — Family A re-attests, and N103 REVIVES.** Trades lost exactly N103 because it keeps files
on installations, not people. Retail keeps files on transactions and orders — the click-and-collect
pickup and the refund-verification are known-answer checks against a record. If N103 attests here,
the possession-driven dropout theory (each sector deletes precisely the Family A shape whose
defining possession it lacks) gains its positive case: give the sector the file back and the
shape returns. Conversely I predict **N102 fails or attests only at branch** — general retail has
no undergoing party (the fitting room's services are the branch case). N101, N104–N110 all
predicted to attest.

**P3 — the accumulated mints re-attest, which graduates them.** N401 predicted to be the DENSEST
it has been in any sector — the refused return, the age refusal, the script-bound "no" are the
sector's daily bread — with the wrinkle that the contest path often points at a person ON THE
PREMISES (the manager: N105 composed at N401.5, C403's exact pattern). N402 at the
come-back-with-your-card refund and the collect-with-ID. F401 at the faulty-goods and
service complaints. From trades: **F501 the exhibit re-attests** (the shelf-edge label walked to
and shown; the worn sole exhibited at the returns desk) and **N501 thinly and only at one
constructed site** (the retained fake ID — flagged as the least confident attestation in this
document); **F502 predicted NOT to attest** — retail tells, but does not make the told sign.

**P4 — the ladder.** Retail is the first sector mapped AT the store's home rung: POD-1's rung 1
(the worker knows the stock) is retail in a different mask. Per-stretch, as trades established:
rung 0 with regulars and staff, rung 1 on the shop floor, rungs 2–3 at the returns desk and the
disputes, the duty apex only at the age gate and the security stop. **K1 shallow and
self-collapsing** — the evidence is on open display and the exhibit costs nothing, unlike the
fault behind the wall. **K2 rotating on the happy path** (either side can walk), **pinned in
trust at the regulated points**, with the sector's distinctive wrinkle: the principal's deputy is
physically present (the manager), making the contest path near-free. **K3 pinned to the shop AND
SPLIT** — the system on one side, the receipt in the customer's pocket on the other; encounters
are settled by whichever record is produced. **K4 low by definition** — it is the low-stakes
sector — with one spike: the security stop, where the customer's dignity and the shop's liability
land on the table at once, and where I predict the register (the innocent-out) does all the work
that in other sectors a shape would do.

**P5 — the scripted-sector question, answered in advance.** Scripts are institutionally owned
SURFACE — the store already has the field for them (`surface_variants`), and "Do you have a
loyalty card?" is a surface variant whose owner is a corporation. So I predict: the happy path is
shape-POOR (N1, N2, N3, P1, P2, F21 and little else, over and over), and the trunk is reached on
the FAILURE branches — the return, the dispute, the escalation, the stop. A scripted sector does
not reach fewer shapes because it is scripted; it reaches them at lower frequency, which a
derived inventory cannot measure and this document will not pretend to. Where retail genuinely
reaches less of the store than trades did is the Method-Pod end — and one deletion is predicted
outright: **N16 the precision haggle is positionally DELETED by the fixed price.** The price tag
removes the counterbid positions entirely — not dearer, ABSENT — which would be the position test
firing against a store node for the first time, and an institutional fact (fixed pricing)
deleting a shape from a sector the way a missing possession deleted N103 from trades.

**P6 — the deaths.** F18 dies at K3 inside the business (the scanner decides what normal is);
F11 dies mid-business (the till's questions are not returnable); **F15 dies with ZERO
time-index** — trades' "couldn't tell you till I've looked" stood because K1 was genuinely
unassigned pre-inspection; in retail the system always already knows, so the bare "no idea"
never stands and converts immediately to the check ("give me two seconds, I'll look it up").
All three revive at rung 0 — the regular at the till, the staff room. The one-rule form (a family
dies exactly where the kind that kills it is pinned, and revives the moment it unpins) predicted
to hold, fifth corpus.

**P7 — invariants.** O7 holds all five positions — the unbidden disclosure at the till, with the
retail violation-by-fixing being the discount, the freebie, or worst, the upsell — and F302
attests richly, because the worker's authority over their own record is partial: "the system says
two in the back, but the system lies." Fifth corpus for both.

**P8 — the second axis, and the hole.** Supported, shape-level: retail's costliest failures
mostly SIT ON encounters (the underage sale — the age check is the encounter; the wrongful
accusation — the stop is the encounter; the allergen answer at the food counter — the enquiry is
the encounter, and the shape discipline that saves lives is exactly F15-converts-to-the-record).
The Ireland-shaped hole predicted present in one place: **the recall that never reaches the
buyer** — the unsafe product sold in a conversation that had no reason to mention it, the danger
never discovered in speech. And retail's fill is predicted to be a THIRD pattern: Ireland guessed
outreach, trades mandates inspection-encounters, retail BROADCASTS (the point-of-sale notice, the
loyalty-data letter) — the hole filled without conversation at all.

**The epistemic caveat, recorded up front as both priors recorded it:** this prediction and this
mapping are one mind in one sitting, and the prediction was written after reading all three prior
mappings in full — including trades' scoring section. The commit order is real; the independence
is partial; a near-perfect score under these conditions is weak evidence, and the misses will be
the informative lines.

## 3. The encounter inventory — written down for the first time

The recurring structured spoken encounters of a retail worker, in two families: **shop floor,
with the public** (E1–E12) and **staff-side** (E13–E16). Each is a position sequence with its
failure branch, because survivability is defined on the failure branch. Labels per the standard
set by Ireland: *derived-confident* or *constructed*.

**E1 — The sale.** *(derived-confident.)* Greet → the ask or the basket presented → scan/serve →
the scripted solicits ("any cashback? do you have a loyalty card? would you like a bag?") →
reckoning → payment → receipt → close. The happy path of the whole sector, run hundreds of times
a shift, almost entirely in corporate surface. **Failure branch:** card declined → the
face-saving re-try ("sometimes the machine's fussy — try it again"), then the fallback ("is
there another way you'd like to pay? I can hold it all here").

**E2 — Where-do-I-find-it.** *(derived-confident.)* The ask ("where's the rice vinegar?") → the
direction, anchored to the walkable world ("aisle seven, halfway down on the left, above the soy
sauce") → sometimes walked there in person. **Failure branch:** not where it should be → the
stock check (E3).

**E3 — Availability and out-of-stock.** *(derived-confident.)* "Have you got any more in the
back?" → the check, against the system or the stockroom → yes and fetched, or no → the
substitute ("no 500g till Thursday — the 250s are two-for on offer, if that helps") → or the
order-in / other-branch check ("town has four — want me to ring and get one put aside?") → the
bounded promise ("delivery's Tuesday, but I can't promise it'll be on it"). **Failure branch:**
the system and the shelf disagree → the flag before the verdict ("the system says two, but the
system lies — let me walk and look").

**E4 — The advice counter.** *(derived-confident; strongest at DIY, electronics, pharmacy-adjacent
and food counters.)* The trouble declared ("my tap drips", "I need something for a damp wall",
"does this contain nuts?") → elicited → the recommendation with grounds → options with what each
costs and forgives → uptake. The allergen form is the sector's life-or-death sentence and its
discipline is routing to the record: never the bare guess, always the label or the folder.
**Failure branch:** the worker does not know → "I don't know" held with status and converted —
to the label, the folder, the person who knows ("I'll get Priya, she does the counter").

**E5 — The age-restricted sale.** *(derived-confident.)* The gate: the sale halts ("I have to
ask for ID for this") → the document produced and checked against the threshold → pass and the
sale resumes, or no ID → the refusal, disowned to the law ("I can't — it's not me, I'd lose us
the licence — bring your passport in and it's yours") → the goods stay, the task travels with
the customer. **Failure branch:** the press ("I'm clearly thirty") → drop the self, hold the
duty, name the scheme ("it's Challenge 25 — if you look under 25 I have to ask, and you should
be flattered"). **Sub-branch, constructed and thin:** the ID is fake and retained under policy —
the one act-against-consent in the sector, flagged in §7 as this document's least confident
attestation.

**E6 — The return, receipt in hand.** *(derived-confident.)* The item and the story → the
receipt checked against the system → the condition checked against the policy ("tags on,
unworn — that's fine") → the refund to the original card → receipt → close. **Failure branch:**
the transaction cannot be found → verify harder (card number, order lookup) or convert to E7.

**E7 — The return refused, or bent.** *(derived-confident.)* No receipt, out of window, worn,
non-returnable class → the refusal, disowned to the policy ("I can't put it back on the card
without the receipt — that's not me, the till literally won't do it") → the substitute offered
("what I CAN do is a credit note at today's price") → taken, or the contest → the path named:
the manager, head office, the card statement as proof. **Failure branch:** the customer contests
the person → drop the self, hold the policy, fetch the tier that can bend it (E10).

**E8 — The price dispute.** *(derived-confident.)* "It said £3 on the shelf" → the check,
against the record — and often the walk: worker and customer to the aisle, the shelf-edge label
read together ("see, that ticket's for the 250g one — it's underneath the wrong stock") → the
verdict: honoured ("it's our mess, I'll put it through at three"), or corrected with the
evidence shown. **Failure branch:** the label really was wrong and the customer is right → own
it at once, fix it at the till, thank the telling ("you were right to flag it — I'll get that
ticket changed").

**E9 — The faulty-goods complaint.** *(derived-confident.)* The item back, the fault shown →
sorry on behalf ("that shouldn't have happened — I'm sorry you've had the trip back in") → the
fault verdict, referenced to a norm ("that's a seam gone, that's manufacturing — that's not
wear") → the remedy by the rule: refund, replace, or send-off ("it has to go to the repair
centre — everything's noted, we'll text you, ten working days") → banked. **Failure branch:**
the verdict is contested ("I've worn them twice") → the evidence rules, and above it the
statute: the rights the shop cannot refuse even when its policy would.

**E10 — Escalation to the manager.** *(derived-confident.)* The contested refusal → "I'll get
the manager for you" → the handover WITH the state ("this gentleman's got the 4-series without a
receipt, I've offered the credit note") → the manager re-runs the verdict holding what the
assistant does not: discretion ("go on — card refund, this once. Keep the receipt next time,
that's all I ask"). The sector's signature: the principal's deputy is IN THE BUILDING, so the
contest path costs a shout, not a form. **Failure branch:** the manager refuses too → the path
points out of the building at last (head office, the card issuer, trading standards).

**E11 — The security stop.** *(derived-confident as a type; ordering constructed.)* The
suspicion → the stop at the line, framed as its innocent counterpart ("sorry — I don't think the
scanner got the things in the bottom of the trolley, did it?") → the check, against the record
(receipt versus bag) → three exits: the innocent repair, face preserved ("oh Lord — no, it
didn't — I'll pay for those now"); the offence, and the process takes over ("I have to ask you
to come to the office — it's on the cameras, and it's not my call from here"); or the stop was
WRONG → own it whole, at once ("my mistake — I'm sorry, that was my error, not yours").
**Failure branch:** the wrongful stop is not survivable by script — it is the sector's dearest
sentence and the recovery (immediate personal ownership, no policy voice) is drilled in §6.

**E12 — The regular, and the rung-0 stretch.** *(derived-confident.)* "Usual, love?" — names
known, states exchanged, the football, the weather, the shared lines that build up over years
("no scratchcard today, I'm reformed" — "course you are"). Interleaved with service: the worker
mid-natter serves the queue and banks the thread ("hold that thought — yes love, just the milk?
— go on, so what did he say?"). **Failure branch:** none worth the name; this is where every
family lives.

**E13 — Shift handover.** *(derived-confident.)* What is done, what is not, what is coming
("delivery's worked to cage three, cage four's chill so it's priority, code-check's done to
aisle five, and the card machine on two is playing up — it's logged") → read-backs on the
load-bearing numbers → the record updated so the absent (tomorrow's shift) inherit it.
**Failure branch:** the handover was not banked → the next shift discovers it the hard way;
the record is the recovery.

**E14 — Task allocation and the new starter.** *(derived-confident.)* The instruction with its
dimensions ("face up seven and eight, dairy first, blues in front") → read-back → shown once,
watched once, corrected in flight → continue. Show-then-do, exactly as trades E14; health's
ruling (witnessed compliance is a P3 variant) followed deliberately. **Failure branch:** the
read-back is wrong → the cheap correction now versus the dear one at close.

**E15 — The scripted no.** *(derived-confident.)* The ask the script forbids — the discount
plea, "just sell me the display one", the staff-discount favour, the restricted quantity →
refusal, disowned ("I can't — it's not a me thing, the till won't let me and they check") →
sometimes the licit substitute ("what I can do is price-match it if you find it cheaper").
**Failure branch:** "go on, no one's looking" → hold the rule, keep the warmth, close ritually
("you're terrible. NO.").

**E16 — The recall at the till.** *(constructed.)* The flagged barcode → the sale stopped
mid-scan ("it won't let me sell you this one — it's been recalled, there's a safety notice") →
what the customer should do with the one at home ("bring yours back, full refund, no receipt
needed") → substitute or refund. The sector's mandated-encounter analogue, and it fires only
when the till catches it. **Failure branch:** none in the room — the failure is the buyer this
conversation never reaches (§6).
