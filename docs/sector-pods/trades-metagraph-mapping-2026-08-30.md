# Trades mapped onto the shape metagraph — the distance test

**2026-08-30.** The third sector, and the first FAR port. Health was built on the trunk; Ireland is
health's near neighbour — an official at a counter is a nurse at a desk with the body removed. Trades
was chosen deliberately because it inherits nothing clinical: a different asymmetry profile, a
genuine safety strand of its own, money on the table, and a second whole family of encounters
(site colleagues) that neither prior sector has. If the trunk holds HERE, the fan-out is licensed.
If trades mints heavily on shape rather than on vocabulary, the trunk does not port that far and
that is the finding.

**There is no corpus, and nothing below pretends otherwise.** Nobody has written down the recurring
spoken encounters of a tradesperson. The inventory in §3 is generated by this mapping — every entry
labelled **derived-confident** (any competent tradesperson has this conversation weekly; no research
needed to assert it exists) or **constructed** (reasoned into existence; may not survive contact
with a real plumber). This is a derivation test, not an attestation test, exactly as Ireland's was:
it can show what the trunk can and cannot EXPRESS for the sector and where the mints concentrate;
it cannot show frequencies, and no percentage below pretends to be one.

## 3. The encounter inventory — written down for the first time

The recurring structured spoken encounters of a tradesperson, in two families: **with the public**
(E1–E11, E18) and **with the site** (E12–E17). Each is a position sequence with its failure branch,
because survivability is defined on the failure branch. Labels per the standard set by Ireland:
*derived-confident* or *constructed*.

**E1 — The call-out.** *(derived-confident; ordering constructed.)* The symptom described by
someone who does not own the vocabulary ("there's a banging when the heating comes on") → elicit,
in chains, with imprecise answers taken up rather than rejected — anchored ("does it do it in the
morning or all day?"), routed to an artefact ("is there a red light on the boiler?") → the visit
arranged: when, access, who will be home, the dog, the parking → banked. **Failure branch:** the
symptom cannot be usefully described at all → triage to a visit anyway, with the caller's task
named ("video it on your phone when it does it").

**E2 — The diagnosis delivered.** *(derived-confident — the seed set's "explaining what is
actually wrong".)* The looking done → the verdict, about a thing the customer cannot see and cannot
verify, referenced to a norm ("that stack's back-falling — it should drop away from the house") →
often the exhibit: the customer summoned to the evidence ("come and look at this") → the meaning
("that's why it smells after a bath") → the fix recommended with grounds → uptake or challenge.
**Failure branch:** the customer cannot follow or does not believe it → reformulate downward,
re-exhibit, or route to the record (photos, the meter reading).

**E3 — The quote.** *(derived-confident — seed.)* Scope stated back → options with grounds ("patch
it and it'll see you two winters; do it properly and it's done") → the option chosen → the number,
with what it includes and what it does NOT ("if the joists are gone under there, that's extra —
I'll know when I'm in") → sometimes haggled → settlement, and what happens next. **Failure
branch:** the number is contested → grounds, not movement ("that's two days and the access
equipment") — or movement by SCOPE, never by rate ("we can get it under that if you do your own
making-good").

**E4 — Bad news mid-job.** *(derived-confident — seed: cost or time moves.)* Work suspended → the
discovery reported with its evidence, usually exhibited ("see how it's all powder behind here?") →
what it does to the price and the clock → the decision handed to the customer with the state
preserved ("I'll leave it opened up and safe; have a think and ring me in the morning — if it's
the full replacement I need to order the steel Tuesday") → the customer decides or banks the task.
**Failure branch:** the customer presses for the original price → the scope-or-price hold ("I can
do the original job at the original price, but I'm writing down what I found").

**E5 — Refusing unsafe work.** *(derived-confident — seed; the safety strand.)* The ask ("just cap
it off", "leave the old board in, it's fine") → the refusal, disowned to the regs ("it's not me,
I'm not allowed — and I wouldn't anyway") → three escalating forms: **(a)** refuse-and-walk, with
the alternative named ("get a second opinion if you like — any registered fitter will tell you the
same"); **(b)** warn-and-waiver — proceed on the customer's head, spoken and SIGNED ("I'll need
your signature to say I advised replacement and you declined"); **(c)** the **compelled
make-safe** — where duty removes the choice entirely: the danger declared, the act announced and
done against the customer's wishes ("I can't leave it connected — I'm disconnecting it now and
tagging it"), the record affixed, the reinstatement condition named ("nobody reconnects that until
it's been re-tested"). **Failure branch of (c):** the customer contests the person → drop the
self, hold the duty, name the forum ("ring Gas Safe if you want it checked — they'll stand by it").

**E6 — The narrated intervention.** *(derived-confident.)* Acting on the customer's house in their
presence: the act announced before it is done ("I'm going to turn your water off now — you'll hear
the pipes knock"), consent taken or assumed-with-licence, the act narrated as it goes, the
undergoing acknowledged ("sorry about the noise — ten more minutes"). **Failure branch:** the
customer halts the act mid-flight ("wait — that's the wall with the wiring in it") → stop, ratify,
verify, resume or re-plan.

**E7 — Handover and sign-off.** *(derived-confident — seed.)* What was done, shown → the operating
instructions, with read-backs ("bleed that one first, always") → the conditional instructions ("if
it drops below one bar, top it up — if it keeps dropping, call me BEFORE you keep topping") → the
certificate and where it lives → the warranty and its bounds → sign-off → payment (E8). **Failure
branch:** snagging (E17).

**E8 — The reckoning.** *(derived-confident.)* Invoice → explanation of the lines → pay-request →
payment → receipt. Money changes hands: P2 territory, dark in health, recovered in Ireland,
recovered here again. **Failure branch:** contest of a line (grounds supplied, E3's logic), or
non-payment (E18).

**E9 — The callback.** *(derived-confident.)* "It's leaking again" → the telling ratified ("you
were right to ring") → triage against the record: own work, or not ("that's the original pipework
— look, different solder — mine's the run on the left") → if own fault, owned and priced at zero
("that's on me — I'll be out in the morning, no charge"); if not, the boundary held and a new job
quoted. **Failure branch:** the attribution is contested → the evidence, the photos, the
certificate — the record decides.

**E10 — The customer's own diagnosis.** *(derived-confident — every tradesperson meets it
weekly.)* "I've had a look on YouTube — it's just the washer" → conceded and held ("could be, and
I'll check it first — but a washer wouldn't give you that knocking") → the premise re-weighted
with grounds → the professional's read substituted or the customer's confirmed ("you were right,
as it goes — it WAS the washer"). **Failure branch:** the customer insists on the cheap theory →
do the cheap check first, on the meter, and let the evidence rule.

**E11 — Living with the job.** *(derived-confident.)* The interim instructions between visits:
what still works, what must not be used, thresholds and escalations ("you can flush it, but
nothing down the sink till Thursday; if it backs up again, ring me the same day"). **Failure
branch:** the instruction was not followed → no blame theatre; re-instruct, re-anchor, record.

**E12 — The merchant's counter.** *(derived-confident.)* The trade counter: order → availability →
the substitute ("no 22mm till Thursday — I've got it in 15 if you want to twin it") → reckoning on
account → banter. **This is POD-1 in a different mask and it is listed to say exactly that.**

**E13 — The site briefing.** *(derived-confident — seed; ordering constructed.)* The induction or
toolbox talk: today's hazards named → the controls and the rules ("nobody past the tape without a
harness — scaffold's signed off to the second lift only") → constraints elicited and recorded
("anyone not worked at height before? — right, you're with Dai") → questions → the sheet signed by
each hearer. Delivered one-to-many, but the load-bearing exchanges — the constraint elicit, the
read-back, the countersign — run dyadically with each hearer in turn. **Failure branch:** a hearer
did not follow it → it is re-run dyadically, and the record shows who was told.

**E14 — Task allocation and the apprentice.** *(derived-confident.)* The instruction with its
dimensions → read-back of the numbers ("690 centres, yeah?") → confirm-and-extend ("690, and keep
your fixings clear of the run at the top") → show-then-do: the first one demonstrated, the second
done under watch, corrected in flight → continue. **Failure branch:** the read-back is wrong → the
correction, cheap here and dear later — which is the whole reason the read-back is drilled.

**E15 — Cross-trade coordination.** *(derived-confident.)* The sequencing negotiation: "I can't
second-fix till your lot are plastered out" → the dependency named, the dates offered and declined
with accounts and counters → agreement, or the disagreement parked upward ("take it to the site
agent — we'll both live with what he says"). **Failure branch:** the other trade's overrun eats
your slot → re-arrange, or escalate with the record ("it's in the programme — week eight, look").

**E16 — The stop call.** *(derived-confident as a type — the safety strand; ordering
constructed.)* Danger spotted live on another's act → the halt, immediate and unsoftened ("STOP —
don't step back") → the danger named → ratified without offence taken → made safe → resumed or
re-planned. Runs in BOTH directions across the learner's seat: the learner stops, and is stopped.
**Failure branch:** the stop is resented ("I know what I'm doing") → the rank-free hold: on site
the stop call outranks the CV, and the shape must survive the resentment.

**E17 — Snagging.** *(derived-confident in commercial work; ordering constructed.)* The walkthrough
against the list → each item: accepted ("fair enough, I'll sort that Friday"), or disputed with
evidence ("that mark was under the other trade's board — it's in my photos from Tuesday") → the
redo tasks banked WITH dates → re-inspection named → sign-off and release of payment. **Failure
branch:** the list keeps growing → the scope boundary held against the record ("that's not
snagging, that's a variation — happy to quote it").

**E18 — Chasing the money.** *(derived-confident.)* The unpaid invoice raised → the excuse, with
its counter-offer ("cash-flow's tight — I can do half Friday") → accepted-and-bounded ("half
Friday, the rest by the end of the month — and I'm pausing the second fix till then") → or
escalated to the process ("after that it goes to small claims — I'd rather not"). Listed
deliberately: it is the money-hold at its most pinned, and it is this document's test bench for
whether money is a kind (§5).

## 5. The ladder — placement ruled, and the money argued both ways

Using the recorded test throughout: **a position appears, disappears or changes hands — not merely
gets harder or dearer.**

**K1 — pinned to the tradesperson, TIME-INDEXED.** Before the inspection, the fault behind the wall
is assigned to neither party — which is why the K1-holder's bare F15 stands pre-inspection (§4.1)
and why F302's epistemic flag is this sector's native register. After the boards are up, K1 pins
hard: the customer cannot verify the diagnosis, which is what E2's exhibit move exists to repair.
The customer holds their share (the house's history — "has it always done that?"), elicited in E1:
split, like Ireland, but shallower — the customer's share informs, it does not decide.

**K2 — ROTATING, which the ladder has not seen on a pinned-kind rung before.** Either party can
refuse and end the encounter at almost every point: the customer declines the quote, takes the
patch option, dismisses the firm; the tradesperson declines the job, down-tools, walks. Run the
test: no refusal position is missing from either seat — N201's discretionary settle is available to
BOTH. That is rung-0 rotation of K2 coexisting with pinned K1 and K3 — evidence that the kinds
rotate or pin INDEPENDENTLY, which the single-number rung was already straining to say (Ireland
needed per-encounter rungs; trades needs per-kind rotation). **The one place K2 stops rotating is
the compelled make-safe: duty held in trust overrides both parties, and the customer's refusal
position DISAPPEARS** — the test fires, and that is exactly where the sector's one node mints.

**K3 — pinned to the tradesperson: the meter, the manometer, the certificate, the photos.** F18
dies to it; E9's callback triage is decided by it; E17's disputed snag is settled by it. Unlike
Ireland, the record is of the INSTALLATION, never of the person — which is why N103 fails to
attest (§8, the prediction's one miss).

**K4 — present, and it CHANGES SEATS between the two encounter families.** Customer-facing, the
stake is the customer's: their house undergoes the act (N102's undergoing position — the property
reading confirmed), their money is on the table. Site-facing, the stake is the LEARNER'S OWN BODY
and their mates' — the first sector where the safety strand protects the speaker's side of the
encounter. The briefing (E13) and the stop call (E16) are K4-of-the-learner shapes; health's
Family B ports to them with the beneficiary flipped, and role annotation absorbs the flip exactly
as the rubric says it should.

**The money, argued both ways as instructed, then ruled.**

*The case FOR money as a kind (or K2 in different clothes):* the customer's hold on payment looks
load-bearing everywhere. The quote encounter exists at all because money must change hands before
work does; the deposit ("half up front") looks like a position money creates; the retention at
