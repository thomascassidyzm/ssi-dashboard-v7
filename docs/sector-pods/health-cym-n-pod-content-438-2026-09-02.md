**Authored 2026-09-02. This is the real thing.** All 438 Welsh turns are live in
`canonical_pod_scenarios`, `target_lang = cym_n`, visible and editable in the Script Lab's
three-column view at `popty.app/canonical/scripts/health`. Nothing is held back and no line waits
on this document. Aran is the language authority and the correction loop: he edits in place.

**This document was rebuilt on 2026-09-02 after Tom corrected the frame it was first written
under.** What changed, and why it matters more than the document:

- **North and south Welsh are two distinct courses with their own pods.** Not one course with a
  variant column. Two courses that happen to share material are still two courses, and the sharing
  is an optimisation discovered afterwards, never the structure decided in advance. The first
  version of this document carried 832 per-row southern annotations. That was the wrong object.
  It has been removed from the record and replaced by §4, a scoping note for the south course as
  separate work. **No southern text was ever written to a database row.**
- **Pod content does not cut.** Cuts exist to make *production* deterministic, and a listening
  walk does not produce. The first version carried 1,278 chunk mappings classified
  deterministic/split/inversion/erasure, and a corpus-wide ZUT sweep over them. All of that was
  the wrong object for this artefact. It is gone from this document; nothing was deleted from the
  repository, and §6 says exactly where it still sits.
- **The grain rule underneath both, which is derived and never looked up:** everything is cut to
  the smallest grain at which known→target stays deterministic, and no smaller. Main seeds reach
  word and phrase because determinism still holds there. Pod-seeds — a separate artefact, not this
  one — stop at the sentence, because below the sentence conversational material stops being
  deterministic and ZUT fails. Pod content does not cut at all, because it does not produce.

**What survived the correction unchanged: the 438 Welsh turns themselves.** They were always whole
target-language lines, they were always north, and they are what a learner hears. The correction
removed scaffolding, not content.

**The English canon was not touched.** Not in the markdown, not in the database, not by
re-cutting, not by fixing anything that looked like a typo. Verified mechanically after every
write: all 438 `english_text` values are byte-identical to
`docs/sector-pods/source/health-sector-conversations-v3.md`.

**Authoring only.** No voice was cast, no audio was generated, and nothing entered a recording

## 0. How this Welsh was written

`cym_n` — northern colloquial Welsh, Aran's own, binding to the base course `cym_n_for_eng` and
consistent with the 57-row general-role cut already live under `health-general-welsh`.

**Register: `chi` throughout.** The standing law is tu-first *unless context insists*, and a
clinician addressing a patient is the context that insists — every one of these 438 turns is
duty-of-care speech between a professional and a stranger, mostly an elderly one. The patients'
own speech is `chi` too: they call the nurse *bach*, *del* and *love*, which is warmth, not
intimacy of address. No turn in the corpus signalled a genuine `ti`.

**The deixis lands where the fiction puts it.** The 57-seed cut had generic deixis ("this isn't my
first language") and resolved it to *Cymraeg*. This corpus is specific: the Filipino nurse and
doctor say "English isn't my first language", so the Welsh says *dim Saesneg ydy fy iaith gynta i*.

**The same English move gets the same Welsh answer, every time.** Not a cut and not a rule the
learner ever sees — just the consistency a single authoring pass can hold and a fan-out cannot.
*shall we* → *wnawn ni*; *let's* → *dewch i ni*; *let me* → *gadwch i mi*; *would you like* →
*fasach chi'n licio*; the *X amdani* ratifier for "X it is"; *dyna i be mae X* for "that's what X
is for"; the need-family on *angen*; obligation on *bydd raid i*; epistemic *ella*; "keep -ing" and
"still" on *dal i*; *deudwch wrtha i* for "tell me" kept apart from *rhowch wybod i mi* for "let me
know"; *dod i ben* for "manage" kept apart from *ymdopi* for "cope"; *dod â / mynd â* for bring and
take; *galw ar* for summoning a person kept apart from *galw heibio* for calling round; and *canu'r
gloch* against *ffonio*, because Welsh will not let one verb ring both a bell and a phone.

**Four idioms for four paces, because they are four different things:** *yn ara bach* (slowly),
*yn ara deg* (nice and slow), *yn ara deg iawn* (extra slow), *mynd gan bwyll* (take it steady).
"Nice and clear" is deliberately not in that family — it takes *yn braf a chlir*.

**Numbers are modern decimal.** Blood pressure digit-group-wise (*135 dros 80*), years as whole
numbers (*mil naw pedwar deg un*), and the blood-sugar figures in 2.9 spelled out (*pedwar deg
wyth*) so they read aloud rather than needing conversion mid-sentence.

**Borrowings are register, not laziness.** *tsiecio, sortio, jobsys, sbwnj, cwstard, jwg, plîs,
jyst, lot, saff, neis, reit, sori, niwsans, ypsetio, tecstio, ffonio, bwcio* — how the ward
actually talks in the north, and the corpus's own patients say "bach" and "Righto".

**Gender rulings the pronouns forced:** *poen* feminine (*y boen*, *mae hi'n waeth*); *cloch*,
*braich*, *brest*, *tabled*, *potel*, *taflen*, *rhestr*, *canmoliaeth*, *her*, *calon*, *anadl*
feminine; the blood-pressure reading and the cough masculine. The ambient "it" of weather and noise
is **feminine** *hi* (*ydy hi'n swnllyd?*), which English's neuter gives no clue about. And the
numeral-gender calls that a single English word cannot signal: *efo chi'ch **dwy*** at SC01-F06-S05
where the pair is the patient and her daughter, *chi'ch **dau*** at SC12-F03-S05 where it is the
patient and her husband and Welsh takes masculine for a mixed pair, *ni'n **tair*** at SC13-F04-S05.

**Mutation is absorbed, never taught.** *fy nallt i*, *y ddwy wen*, *tri pheth*, *y frest*, *dwy
botel fach*. A learner meets them as worn phrases.

**79 of the 438 turns sit in a flow the corpus tags safety-critical** — 14 carry the ⚠ on the line
itself — and they take the plainest register in the walk: no idiom a learner could misread, the
identity check on the walk's standard frame, and Welsh negative concord written out rather than
compressed. Reconciled exactly against the corpus's own flag: none missed, none added.

## 2. Where the risk sits — start here, Aran

A pointer for a corrector, not a queue for an approver. Every one of these is already live and
already editable; nothing is held back. It is here so the first ten minutes of editing land where
they pay most.

**The lines I am least sure of:**

- **SC09-F03-S02** — *"Mi wnewch chi ddal gafael yna i, gnewch?"* for "You will hold onto me, won't
  you?". The reduced northern conjugated *yn* + *fi* is written *yna i*; *ynof fi* is the standard.
  If the reduced form is written differently in your ear, this is the one to fix.
- **SC11-F02-S04** — *"Mi fasa'i gael o wedi'i sgwennu i lawr yn hyfryd"* for "Written down would
  be lovely". English can make a bare participle a subject and Welsh cannot, so I built a
  *having-it-written* clause under it. Correct, and heavy. A lighter line may exist.
- **SC22-F01-S05** — *"dyna'r fuddugoliaeth fwya un"* for "that's the biggest single win". The
  postposed *un* is right but rare; *dyna'r un peth mwya effeithiol* would be plainer.
- **SC16-F01-S06** — *"Ar y gwely archwilio, ia?"* for "On the couch, is it?". *soffa* would be
  wrong and an untranslated *couch* would be wrong; *gwely archwilio* is clinically right and may
  be stiffer than a real patient's word.

**Four idiom relocations that could each go another way**, all defensible: "stiff as a board" →
*stiff fel procer* (Welsh is stiff as a *poker*, SC09-F01-S02); "Fat chance of me running off" →
*Does 'na fawr o beryg…* (SC04-F02-S06); "Don't keep me in suspense" → *ar bigau'r drain*
(SC17-F01-S06); "scout's honour" → *ar fy ngwir* (SC20-F03-S06).

**Two clinical-register choices:** *llaeth* for "dairy", taken from the 57-row cut for consistency
though *cynnyrch llaeth* is more precise on a ⚠ allergy line (SC08-F02-S02); and *chwilen* for "a
bug, basically" (SC18-F02-S03), where the doctor is deliberately dropping register and *haint*
would undo the point of the turn.

**The register call that runs through everything is `chi`.** One look, and if it goes the other way
it is one mechanical pass over 438 rows, not a re-authoring.

**Where this walk differs from the 57-row `health-general-welsh` cut** — information for your
editing pass, not anything to decide. "A treat" takes *yn gampus* rather than spreading *del*
across three jobs, so *campus* stays the one approval-lexeme. "You'll both" takes masculine
*chi'ch dau* at SC12-F03-S05 where the cut pinned feminine course-wide — the cut's own hazard note
said a different cast would be a different known, and this corpus contains one. And *clên* runs
throughout rather than alternating with *ffeind*.

## 3. What Welsh requires that the English never shows

Not a curriculum and not a cut — a description of what this walk's Welsh is actually leaning on,
so that whoever builds the sector's pod-seeds later knows what they are walking into. Most
load-bearing first.

1. **Conjugated prepositions, in nearly every turn** — *wrtha i, wrthach chi, wrthan ni, wrtho fo,
   arna i, arnoch chi, arni hi, amdano fo, amdani, amdanyn nhw, ato, atoch chi, iddo fo, iddi hi,
   iddyn nhw, ohonan ni, ohonoch chi, gen i, gynnoch chi, gynnon ni, drwyddo fo, ynoch chi*.
   English strands its prepositions; Welsh cannot.
2. **The *gen i* family — everything English predicates that Welsh possesses.** *mae gen i* (have),
   *mae'n well gen i* (prefer), *mae'n ddrwg gen i* (be sorry), *mae'n dda gen i* (be pleased),
   *mae gen i ofn* (be frightened), *wrth ei bodd* (be delighted), *dw i ar lwgu* (be starving).
   **This is also the whole of the north/south structural difference — see §4.**
3. **Verb-echo answers and tags** in place of English's pronoun-plus-auxiliary: *Ydy · Oes · Cewch
   · Naddo · Ia · Medra · Gnewch · Does · Wnewch chi*.
4. **The *-wch* imperative — the walk's whole production register** — and *peidiwch â* for its
   negative.
5. **Present + *ers*** for have-been-Xing.
6. **Clefts and focus** — the *sy'*-focus, the *mai*-cleft, identificatory fronting (*Siân dw i*),
   the appositive *sef*. Welsh's answer to English's stress-for-emphasis.
7. **Clause-frames** *cyn i / tra / fel bod / nes / rhag ofn*, and the *bod* family for
   that-clauses.
8. **The *wedi*- and *cael*-passives** — English's be-passive and get-passive land on two different
   Welsh machines.
9. **The pre-posed object before a verbal noun** — *i'w gofio, i'w fwyta, eich cyfarfod chi*.
10. **The *â*-family** — carrying (*dod â, mynd â*), propulsion (*ffwrdd â ni*), comparison.
11. **Preposition semantics English does not distinguish** — remedial *at*, motion-toward *at*,
    blocking *rhag*, summons *ar*, governing *wrth*, and illness's *ar* (*roedd o ar fy mam i*).
12. **Gender on nouns, numerals and adjectives**, and the ambient feminine *hi*.
13. **Negative concord** — *Ddeudodd neb ddim byd*. English allows one negative per clause; Welsh
    stacks them and requires it.
14. **Bare embedded yes/no questions** — *gawn ni weld fedrwch chi ei orffen o* — where English
    needs "if" or "whether" and Welsh needs nothing.
15. **The alternative-question *'ta***, load-bearing in 2.4 where the rebound-tenderness question
    depends on its two limbs being unmistakable.
16. **The colloquial reductions that ARE the northern register** — *dechra, gynta, hynna, gola,
    petha, cry, gan fwya, stafell molchi*. Writing them out in full would put the whole walk in a
    register no Welsh nurse uses.

## 4. The south course — a scoping note, not authored here

**South Welsh is a separate course with its own pods and its own 438 turns.** It is not authored in
this job and nothing southern was written to any row. This section says what it would take.

**The evidence, and what it does and does not mean.** Job #913 measured the north/south divergence
across the 57-seed general-role set: 37 distinct items, 157 occurrences, 51 of 57 seeds. My own
authoring of these 438 turns reproduced that proportion on eight times the corpus — 385 of 438
turns (88%) would carry at least one divergence, against #913's 89%. **That means the two courses
will share a great deal of text. It does not mean there is one course.** Overlap is an efficiency
to exploit at authoring and recording time; it is never a reason to merge two courses into one
artefact.

**What the south authoring job actually is.** All 438 turns, authored as south Welsh, into their
own pod. Not a find-and-replace over the north lines — #913's own caveat rules that out and my
authoring confirmed it. Concretely, from the north corpus:

- **Roughly 44 lemma-level items diverge**, surfacing as about 205 distinct inflected forms across
  832 places. The heavy ones are the pronoun/copula set (*dach chi* → *dych chi*, *mae o* → *mae
  e*), the *mi* preverbal particle, *efo* → *gyda*, *rŵan* → *nawr*, *medru* → *gallu*,
  *deud/dallt* → *dweud/deall*, *gneud* → *gwneud*, *isio* → *eisiau/moyn*, *bob dim* → *popeth*.
- **About seven items are new against #913's 37**, and they are new because conversation surfaces
  what a seed set does not: *ella* → *falle* (the epistemic adverb — seeds rarely hedge), *medda* →
  *meddai* (the quotative — you need reported speech before it appears), *gadwch* → *gadewch*,
  *sgwennu* → *sgrifennu*, *ia* → *ie*, *y rheiny* → *rheina*, *hogan* → *merch*.
- **Sixteen items I could not resolve** and would not guess. A southern authority answers them in
  one pass: *'ta* in both its roles, *reit 'ta / rŵan 'ta*, the pace family, *sownd*, *clustog*,
  *tŷ bach*, *ylwch*, *toc*, *del*, *call*, *nôl*, *disgwyl* = expect, *saff*, and the several
  "likely shared" cases.
- **About 21 turns need a genuinely different sentence shape, not a swap** — and they are all one
  fork: the *gen i* possessive family moving to *gyda fi*, which re-orders the possessed noun and
  the possessor phrase relative to each other. #913 found this in two seeds; 438 turns of
  conversation show it reaching much further, into *mae'n ddrwg gen i*, *mae'n dda gen i*, *mae
  gynnoch chi*, *sy gynnon ni*, *basa'n well gynnoch chi*, and *mae gen i ofn* — which in the south
  does not swap a token at all but moves to a different preposition, *mae ofn arna i*.

**The head start exists and is real.** The material I wrote under the superseded frame — 832
annotations and 21 whole southern lines — is retained in
`docs/sector-pods/health-welsh-438/ctx*.json` under the `south` and `southLine` keys, labelled as
superseded in that directory's README. It is not the structure of the south course, but it is a
usable map of where the south course will differ, and it means the south authoring job starts with
its hardest 21 sentences already drafted rather than blank.

**What it does not tell you** is where the south course should live in the store. That is §5.

## 5. The schema question, answered plainly

**Asked: does the schema support two distinct Welsh courses each with their own pods, or does it
assume one Welsh with variants?**

**Answer: the estate has two pod layers and they disagree. One already models two Welsh courses
correctly. The other has no concept of a course at all — and that is the layer these 438 rows are
in.**

### 5.1 `listening_pods` — models it correctly, and already has both

129 rows. Columns include **`course_code`**, and the primary key is `<course_code>:<slug>`. Both
Welsh courses are already there as separate rows with separate pods:

```
cym_n_for_eng:pod-0                     course_code = cym_n_for_eng
cym_s_for_eng:pod-0                     course_code = cym_s_for_eng
cym_n_for_eng:pod-0-gated-2026-08-06    course_code = cym_n_for_eng
cym_s_for_eng:pod-0-gated-2026-08-06    course_code = cym_s_for_eng
```

`cym_n_for_eng` and `cym_s_for_eng` sit alongside 66 other course codes as ordinary peers. At this
layer Tom's correction is not a change at all — it is what the schema already says.

### 5.2 `canonical_pod_scenarios` — has no course concept, and this is the finding

The table holding all 438 of these rows has **no `course_code` column and no reference to one**.
Its columns are `id, pod_slug, scene_number, scene_label, scene_title, scene_subtitle, difficulty,
sentence_number, global_order, speaker, english_text, author_notes, created_at, updated_at,
variant_key, target_text, target_lang`.

So: a row is keyed by a free-text `pod_slug` and carries **exactly one `target_text` and one
`target_lang`**. Two courses therefore require two `pod_slug` values with two full sets of rows —
which is *mechanically possible*, and it is what the estate has in fact been doing:

```
   438  health                 target_lang = cym_n     ← these rows
    57  health-general-welsh   target_lang = cym_n
   306  care-work              target_lang = (null)
   330  hospitality            target_lang = (null)
   367  learning-flagship      target_lang = (null)
   330  retail                 target_lang = (null)
   414  trades                 target_lang = (null)
   231  pod-1                  target_lang = (null)
   276  method-pod-43-scene    target_lang = ita
   309  method-pod-chapters    target_lang = ita
```

**But it is convention, not structure.** Nothing in the schema ties a `pod_slug` to a `course_code`.
Nothing declares that `health` is the language-neutral English canon while `health-general-welsh`
is a pair realisation of it — the only thing carrying that distinction is a naming habit and a
`format` field in `tools/pods/pod-corpora.json`, where `targetLang` is likewise a single scalar per
walk. **There is no constraint, no foreign key and no check that would have stopped what I did.**

### 5.3 What I did, and why it is the finding rather than a mistake to quietly fix

I wrote north Welsh into `pod_slug = 'health'` — the slug carrying the shared, language-neutral
English canon, the one `retail`, `trades`, `hospitality` and `care-work` are siblings of, all with
`target_lang = null`. In doing so I consumed the only target column that shared canon has.

Under the corrected frame the right shape is almost certainly three slugs, not one:

```
health            the English canon           target_lang null, untouched, shared by every pair
health-cym-n      the NORTH Welsh pod         438 rows   ← where these lines belong
health-cym-s      the SOUTH Welsh pod         438 rows   ← separate work
```

…or, better and matching what `listening_pods` already does, `cym_n_for_eng:health` and
`cym_s_for_eng:health`, so the two layers key the same way instead of two different ways.

**I have not moved them, and I am not going to on my own initiative.** Three reasons. The naming
convention is a structural decision with estate-wide consequences — every sector walk and every
future pair inherits whichever shape is picked, and `listening_pods` and `canonical_pod_scenarios`
keying differently is itself a defect worth fixing deliberately rather than by my guess. The
Script Lab reads `pod_slug` directly, so a re-slug moves the URL Aran is editing at. And the 438
English rows under `health` must survive any such move untouched, which is a migration with a
gate, not a rename.

`tools/pods/reslug-pod-rows.cjs` already exists and is the tool for it. It is a short job once the
convention is chosen.

**The honest summary: the rows are right, the Welsh is right, and the slug is a structural question
that outranks them.** Nothing is lost either way — the 438 lines are reproducible from
`docs/sector-pods/health-welsh-438/` and land wherever they are pointed.

## 6. Explicit gaps

- **The superseded material is retained, not deleted.** 1,278 chunk mappings and 832 southern
  annotations live in `docs/sector-pods/health-welsh-438/ctx*.json` under the `chunks`, `south` and
  `southLine` keys, and in git history at commit `9c1aa76fd`, which is the first version of this
  document. That directory's `README.md` labels both as superseded and says what they may not be
  used for. Nothing downstream may read a `chunks` entry as a LEGO or a cut.
- **The English-version flows are authored in Welsh, and that is a decision worth naming.** Six
  flows — the Filipino nurse and doctor sequences at 1.0 and 2.0 — are scenes in which the
  healthcare worker is explicitly speaking *English*. All 60 of their turns carry a Welsh line
  anyway, because the target column is the pair realisation of the canonical English and not a
  claim about which language the fiction happens in. **If that is wrong it is one edit each**, and
  it is the single most likely thing anyone would do differently.
- **Welsh already embedded in the English is carried through verbatim, never re-rendered** —
  *Chwarae teg i chi*, *Diolch*, *bach*. One exception: "Fair play for saying so" became *Chwarae
  teg i chi am ddeud*, because Welsh cannot leave *chwarae teg* without its addressee.
- **I did not census `cym_n_for_eng` against this walk's vocabulary.** No such measurement exists
  and I did not make one. Everything here leans on high-frequency early-course Welsh by design, but
  that is an intention, not a measurement.
- **Clinical accuracy is not mine and I did not touch it.** The corpus's own design note says the
  nil-by-mouth handling, safety-netting thresholds and blood-sugar figures want a practising
  clinician. I mapped them faithfully; if a figure is wrong in English it is now wrong in Welsh too.
- **Two English oddities noticed and deliberately not edited**, per the standing rule: SC02-F02-S01's
  "And you are?" is a fragment Welsh has to complete (*A chithau?*), and SC04-F02-S04's "Is it come
  down?" is dialectal rather than standard. Both are almost certainly authored voice.
- **Pod-seeds for the health sector do not exist and were not generated.** They are a separate
  artefact that cuts at the sentence, and they are not derivable from this walk.
