# Trades conversations v1
## The trades themed walk — canonical English dialogue: the quote, the job, and the return visit

**Authored 2026-09-01, from nothing.** There was no trades corpus — the mapping
(`docs/sector-pods/trades-metagraph-mapping-2026-08-30.md`, proposal
`services/shared/metagraph/proposed/trades-2026-08-30.json`) records that plainly and derives the
encounter inventory E1–E18 without a line of dialogue in it. This file is that dialogue: the
mapping used as the spec, every encounter it derives covered by scenes written for it. **AUTHORED,
NOT TRANSCRIBED.** No line here is attested speech; the standard of work it is written against is
Aran's hand-authored health corpus (`health-sector-conversations-v3.md`, same directory).

**Canonical layer only.** This is the English known side and it is pair-invariant: no target
language, no pair, no course code, no dialect choice. Languages are named deictically — "your
language" / "mine" — per scene 0's ratified convention, so every scene instantiates in any pair.

**The learner seat is the TRADESPERSON**, in both encounter families — facing the public and
facing the site — matching the mapping's ruling and both prior sectors. Roles are derived, not
authored: the dialogue has both ends, and §Accounting reports every mouth this walk yields with
its turn count.

**Scene 0 sits at the front of this walk unconditionally and is not re-authored here.** The
medium contract is ratified and on main: `services/shared/metagraph/walks/core-scene-0.json`
(W1201–W1204, the offer with its decline and diversion branches) and `core-scene-0-notice.json`
(W1401, the notice). The learner arrives at scene 1 having walked all five. Where a flow below
needs the contract's shadow, a turn *uses* the already-granted licence; nothing re-issues it.

**Reading the labels.** Each scene opens with a shape header: *Walks* — the graph shapes the
scene traverses (store ids bare; proposed ids tagged); *Admits* — the ONE new response position
this scene adds beyond what the learner owns from CORE, scene 0 and earlier scenes
(zero-admission scenes say so — they are deliberate, and the highest-yield kind); *Branch set* —
the attested response families the flows cover, which is what makes each scene a branch point
with its continuations attached. **T** = tradesperson (the learner seat), **C** = customer,
**M** = merchant, **A** = apprentice, **O** = another trade, **TEN** = tenant. **⚠** marks the
safety-critical lines a learner must catch first time. Every flow is a six-turn arc.

# Part 1: The quote — call-out to number

## Scene 1 — The call-out

*Walks:* N12 trouble-and-advice + P5 elicit chains + N7 arrangement; N108 [health, proposed] —
the gate, the dog, constraints recorded for a visit not yet arrived.
*Admits:* the elicit routed to an artefact — the caller's imprecise answer taken up, never
rejected, and the next question aimed at a thing they can go and look at.
*Branch set:* symptom describable / symptom not describable (the caller's task named) / the
access negotiation.

### Flow 1 *(happy path — the banging heating)*
- **C:** "Hello — is that the plumber's? There's a banging noise when the heating comes on. It's been getting worse all week."
- **T:** "You've come to the right place. Is it banging all day, or just when it first fires up?"
- **C:** "Mostly first thing, I think. Then it settles down, sort of."
- **T:** "That narrows it — that's useful. Do one thing for me: look at the front of the boiler. There's a little dial. What number is the needle on?"
- **C:** "Hold on… right. It's just under the one."
- **T:** "That tells me a lot. Nothing to worry about tonight — I can be with you Thursday, first thing, and I'll want ten minutes with the boiler and a look at the radiators upstairs."

### Flow 2 *(problem — the noise nobody can describe)*
- **C:** "It's making a noise. I don't know how to describe it — a sort of… noise. My husband says it's the pipes."
- **T:** "Don't worry — noises are hard over the phone. Is it a knocking, like someone tapping? Or more of a whoosh, like wind?"
- **C:** "Neither, really. It comes and goes. I'm not much good at this, am I?"
- **T:** "You're doing fine — half my job is noises nobody can describe. Let's not guess: next time it does it, video it on your phone, sound and all, and send it to this number."
- **C:** "Oh, I can do that. It usually starts in the evening, when the water's running."
- **T:** "That's a clue on its own, that is. Send me the video tonight, and I'll ring you in the morning and tell you what we're looking at."

### Flow 3 *(the access negotiation)*
- **T:** "Thursday morning I can do — I'd be with you for eight, before the traffic."
- **C:** "Eight's no good, I'm on the school run. There's nobody home till half nine."
- **T:** "Half nine works if I take the next job first. Is there parking on your road, or am I carrying tools half a mile?"
- **C:** "There's a drive — I'll leave it clear. Oh, and the side gate sticks. And we've got a dog, but he's soft."
- **T:** "Soft dogs still eat sandwiches — shut him in the kitchen while I'm in and out, if you would. Anything else I should know before I turn up?"
- **C:** "Just the gate — lift it as you push and it opens fine. See you Thursday, half nine."

## Scene 3 — The look, and not knowing yet

## Scene 4 — The diagnosis, and the exhibit

*Walks:* N104 [health, proposed] result delivery + **F501 [trades, proposed] the exhibit** — the
K1-holder collapses their own asymmetry, relocates the encounter to the evidence, and only then
asks for the decision. Flow 2 is S502's recovery: re-exhibit and route to the record, never
re-assert. Flow 3 is E10, F16 + the cheap check on the meter.
*Admits:* the exhibit — "come and look at this," before the verdict and before the ask.
*Branch set:* believed / not believed / the customer's own theory.

### Flow 1 *(happy path — shown, told, chosen)*
- **T:** "Come and look at this. See that white crust all along the joint? Run your eye down the pipe — dry till there, then crusty."
- **C:** "I see it — like scale, sort of. Is that what's been banging?"
- **T:** "That's a weep — a slow leak that dries as it goes. The bang is the pipe moving where it's come loose; the weep's rotted the clip out. That pipe should sit as still as a rail."
- **C:** "So it's been leaking all this time? Into the wall?"
- **T:** "A cupful a week, maybe — the crust tells you it's slow. The fix is a new fitting and two clips, an hour's work — and I'd do the clip on the far run while I'm in there, because it's going the same way."
- **C:** "Do it — both clips. I'd rather not meet you again over the same pipe."

### Flow 2 *(problem — not believed, and the record decides)*
- **C:** "A leak? That pipe's bone dry — I've just watched you wipe it. I think you're seeing jobs where there aren't any."
- **T:** "Fair challenge — it's your money. Look again at the crust, though: water leaves that behind when it dries. A dry pipe with crust on it has been wet a hundred times."
- **C:** "Or it's twenty years of dust. No offence, but I've been quoted for ghosts before."
- **T:** "None taken — you're right to push. So let's not use my eyes: here's a dry bit of kitchen roll, wrapped round the joint. We'll have our tea, and the paper gives you the verdict, not me."
- **C:** "…Well. There's your spot of damp, right on the fold. I'll be honest — I didn't expect that."
- **T:** "Good instinct to test it, all the same — never feel bad for checking. So: new fitting, two clips, an hour. Same answer, but now it's the paper's answer."

### Flow 3 *(the customer's own diagnosis — the washer gets its chance)*
- **C:** "Before you start — I've had a look on YouTube, and I reckon it's just the washer. Ten-minute job, they said."
- **T:** "Could be, and I'll check the washer first — it's the cheapest thing on the list. But a worn washer gives you a drip; it doesn't give you that knocking you rang about."
- **C:** "The fella on the video had knocking. Sounded just like ours."
- **T:** "Then let's give the washer its chance — two minutes, and no charge if that's all it is. If the knock's still there with a new washer in, we go looking further. Deal?"
- **C:** "Deal. …That's it changed, then? Give the tap a go."
- **T:** "Tap's on… and there's your knock, same as before. Washer's ruled out, ten pounds saved — now come and look at where the noise is really coming from."

## Scene 5 — The quote

*Walks:* N8 + P1 options with grounds; N16 where haggled — settlement by SCOPE, never by rate
(S504's recovery); N109 [health, proposed] — the bound on the unknown; the exclusions are
N107-form. Flow 3 is K2 rotating: the customer's no ends the encounter and costs nothing.
*Admits:* the bounded number — what it includes, what it excludes, and "I'll know when I'm in"
spoken so the unknown has a name before it has a price.
*Branch set:* chosen / haggled / declined.

### Flow 1 *(happy path — two ways to go)*
- **T:** "Right — two ways to go. Patch the run: I replace the bad fitting and the clips, and it'll see you two or three winters. Or do it properly: new run along that wall, and it's done for twenty years."
- **C:** "What's the difference in money?"
- **T:** "The patch is about one-eighty, parts and all, done today. The proper job's about six hundred — a day's work, and the wall boxing has to come off, which is most of the extra."
- **C:** "Six hundred. And if the boxing comes off and it's ugly under there?"
- **T:** "Then that's extra, and I tell you before I touch it — if the joists are gone under that run, that's a different job and a different number. I'll know when I'm in, and nothing happens past the quote without your say-so."
- **C:** "Do the proper job. When can you start, and what do you want up front?"

### Flow 2 *(the haggle — grounds first, then scope, never rate)*
- **C:** "Six hundred's steep. The fella down the road said his cousin would do it for four."
- **T:** "Then his cousin's either quicker than me or he's not lifting the boxing. Mine's six because it's two days' work and the making-good after — the number's built out of the job, not out of the air."
- **C:** "Four and a half, cash, and I'll say nice things about you to the neighbours."
- **T:** "The rate doesn't move — that's how I'm still in business to answer the phone. But the scope can: do your own making-good, paint and boxing back yourself, and I can get you under five."
- **C:** "So I do the decorating and you do the pipes. Four-eighty?"
- **T:** "Four-eighty with the making-good yours — and we both write that down, so there's no arguing in a fortnight. Deal?"

### Flow 3 *(the decline — and it costs nothing)*
- **C:** "I'll be straight with you — six hundred's more than we can do this side of Christmas. We'll have to leave it."
- **T:** "That's an honest answer and I'll take it. The patch at one-eighty still stands if you want the noise gone — but hear the important bit: nothing I found today is dangerous. Leaving it costs you noise, not safety."
- **C:** "That helps, actually. If it were your house, what would you do?"
- **T:** "If it were mine? Patch it now, save up, and do the proper job in spring when the heating's off anyway — it's a cheaper job with the system cold."
- **C:** "Then that's what we'll do. Patch today, and pencil us in for April."
- **T:** "Patch today, April in the book. And if that noise changes — gets faster, or you ever see water — ring me. Don't wait for April."

## Scene 9 — Refusing unsafe work

*Walks:* N401 [ireland, proposed] bound refusal, in trades clothes — the rule cited, authorship
