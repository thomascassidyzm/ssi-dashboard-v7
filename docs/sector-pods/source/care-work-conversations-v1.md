**AUTHORED, NOT TRANSCRIBED.** Every line in this file was written for this walk on 2026-09-01,
against the care-work mapping (`docs/sector-pods/care-work-metagraph-mapping-2026-08-30.md`,

dialogue, like its exemplar (`health-sector-conversations-v3.md`).

**The register, in one line:** care work is domestic, repeated and relational — the same two
people meet daily, there is no discharge, and companionship is not a garnish on the service; it
is half the service.

**Canonical layer:** English known side only. No target language, no pair, no course code.
Languages are named deictically per scene 0's ratified convention — the learner says "yours" /
"mine", the native the reverse. CORE scene 0 (`walks/core-scene-0.json`, W1201–W1204, and
`core-scene-0-notice.json`, W1401) sits at the front of this walk unconditionally and is not
re-authored here; context 1.0 below is the STANDING form of that contract — what a daily
relationship adds to it — and nothing scene 0 teaches is re-taught.

**Reading the labels:** each flow is a six-turn arc with a short tag naming the path it threads
(*happy path*, *question*, *problem*, *safety-critical ⚠*, *human moment*). **CW** = the care
worker (the learner seat, in every flow). The other voices are named per scene: **P** = the
person receiving care, **F** = a family member, **C** = a colleague or professional. **⚠**
marks the safety-critical lines a learner must catch first time. Within a context, later flows
attach at the trunk flow's branch point — same opening, different world — scene 0's attachment
discipline.

**The cast, who recur because recurrence is the register:**
- **Part 1 (domiciliary):** Anna, the care worker. Eileen, eighty-four, lives alone with her
  cat Smokey; sharp as a pin, slower on her feet than she'll admit. Cath, her daughter. Donna,
  at the office. The district nurse.
- **Part 2 (residential):** Anna again, on shifts at the home. Arthur, who was a docker for
  forty years and is living with dementia; his wife Nancy died nine years ago. Michael, his
  son. Sam, the colleague on nights. Joan, a new client, at her first assessment.

# Part 1: The call — Eileen

## 1.0 The standing arrangement (the medium contract in a standing relationship)

*Walks:* N1201

### Flow 1 *(happy path — the one-line re-invocation)*
- **CW:** "Morning, Eileen — it's only me. Are we in yours today, then?"
- **P:** "Course we are. We always are — you'll not get out of it that easily."
- **CW:** "Good. Stop me if I make a mess of anything — same as ever."
- **P:** "Same as ever. You hardly need me to these days, mind."
- **CW:** "I need you to more than you know. Right — kettle first, or curtains first?"
- **P:** "Kettle. The curtains can wait for daylight; the tea can't wait at all."

### Flow 2 *(problem — the daily decline, at no cost)*
- **CW:** "Morning, Eileen — it's only me. Are we in yours today?"
- **P:** "Not today, love. My head's all cotton wool this morning — let's have it in yours."
- **CW:** "Yours can have the day off, then. It'll keep — it's not going anywhere, and neither am I."
- **P:** "You're not offended? You do try so hard with it."
- **CW:** "Not a bit. It's a rest day, not a losing day. Now — kettle on, and you can tell me about this head of yours."
- **P:** "Go on, then. It's the not sleeping, I think — but the tea will help."

### Flow 3 *(human moment — the substitute worker)*
- **CW:** "Morning — I'm Anna. Karen's poorly today, so you've got me, I'm afraid."
- **P:** "Oh! Well, come in out of the rain, Anna-instead-of-Karen. Do you know how I like things done?"
- **CW:** "Not yet — so you'll have to be my teacher this morning. Tell me as we go, and I'll have it right by the end."
- **P:** "First lesson: the tea goes in before the milk, and Smokey has his breakfast before I have mine."
- **CW:** "Tea before milk, cat before you. What's next on the list — your list, not mine?"
- **P:** "The wash, then the tablets. Karen writes it all in the folder by the bread bin, if you get lost."

## 1.1 The door and the day

### Flow 1 *(happy path — the day referenced to its baseline)*
- **CW:** "Now then. How's today treating you — better than yesterday, or more of the same?"
- **P:** "Better, I'd say. The knee's loosened up. I got to the gate and back before you came."
- **CW:** "The gate and back on your own steam — that's further than Tuesday, and Tuesday was further than Monday."
- **P:** "You keep score better than the bowls club."
- **CW:** "It's worth keeping score of. I'll put the gate in the book — the nurse likes to see it written down."
- **P:** "Put it in big letters, then. I earned it."

### Flow 2 *(human moment — the noticing)*
- **CW:** "Right, wash first and then — Eileen, you're quiet today. Usually I can't get a word in while the kettle's on."
- **P:** "Am I? Take no notice. I didn't sleep much, that's all."
- **CW:** "That's the second bad night this week, then. Was it the knee keeping you up, or the head going round?"
- **P:** "The head, mostly. I was thinking about Ron. It was our anniversary, yesterday — would have been."
- **CW:** "Oh, Eileen. That'll keep anyone's light on. Do you want the wash first, or the tea and a sit-down first?"
- **P:** "The tea, I think. And you can tell me something cheerful while it brews."

### Flow 3 *(problem — the help she'd rather not need)*
- **CW:** "Shall I do the bed while you're finishing your toast?"
- **P:** "I can do my own bed. I ran this house for sixty years, you know — I don't need looking after."
- **CW:** "Nobody said looking after. You're the one who knows where everything lives — I'm just the extra pair of hands."
- **P:** "Hm. Sixty years, and now I get an extra pair of hands whether I want them or not."
- **CW:** "Then put them to work on something you'd rather not waste your knee on. Your list, not mine — what's the worst job in this house?"
- **P:** "The bottom sheet. It fights me. Go on then — but I'm doing the pillows."

## 1.2 The wash

*Walks:* N102, N110, N5, F11, F18, O101

### Flow 1 *(happy path — the act announced and narrated)*
- **CW:** "Ready when you are. I'll do your back and your feet, and you do the rest — same as always. Alright?"
- **P:** "Go on, then. No dawdling — the water goes cold in this bathroom."
- **CW:** "Water's coming now — it's a bit warm, tell me if it's too much. That's your back done. Feet next."
- **P:** "The left foot's ticklish, I'm warning you now."
- **CW:** "Duly warned. There — all done, and no laughing fits. Towel's on the rail, warm from the radiator."
- **P:** "A warm towel. You'd pay good money for this in a hotel."

### Flow 2 *(human moment — the chat is half the service)*
- **CW:** "Right, back and feet. So — did your granddaughter ring last night, in the end?"
- **P:** "She did! An hour, we talked. She's got an interview Friday — the big school, the one she wanted."
- **CW:** "The one she wanted! You'll be lighting candles all week, then. Lean forward a touch for me — there."
- **P:** "I will. She's clever, that one. Cleverer than her mother, only don't tell Cath I said so."
- **CW:** "Your secret's safe in this bathroom. Feet now — ticklish one last. And you'll tell me Friday how she got on?"
- **P:** "You'll be the first to know after me. Ooh — mind that foot!"

### Flow 3 *(safety-critical ⚠ — the flinch, and the halt)*
- **CW:** "Water's coming now — tell me if it's too much. Starting with your back."
- **P:** ⚠ "Ah — no, stop. Stop a minute. That shoulder — don't touch the shoulder."
- **CW:** "Stopped. Thank you for telling me straight away. Let me look, don't move it — Eileen, there's a bruise there like a plum. When did that come?"
- **P:** "I came down on it in the night, reaching for the lamp. It's nothing — don't fuss."
- **CW:** "It's not a fuss, but it is going in the book, and the nurse should look at it today. We'll finish the wash a different way — you hold the cloth and set the pace, I'll only do what you ask me to."
- **P:** "Alright. But you're making the tea after, for the trouble."

## 1.3 "I'm not having it today" — the refusal

*Walks:* F202, F103, N201

### Flow 1 *(happy path — refused, accepted, recorded)*
- **CW:** "Shall we get your wash done before the news comes on?"
- **P:** "I'm not having a wash today. I'm cold, and I'm comfortable, and you can't make me."
- **CW:** "You're quite right, I can't — and I wouldn't want to. Fair enough — we'll leave it."
- **P:** "Well. That was easier than I expected."
- **CW:** "It's your wash, Eileen, not mine. I'll just pop in the book that you weren't feeling like it, so the story's straight — and I'll warm the bathroom up before I go, in case you change your mind after the tea."
- **P:** "You can do that. And I might. And it's still my choice if I don't."

### Flow 2 *(problem — the return, and the second no)*
- **CW:** "Bathroom's warm now, and the news is finished. Shall we have a go at that wash?"
- **P:** "No. I said no this morning and it's still no. You're like a dog with a bone."
- **CW:** "Last time of asking, I promise — a no's a no, twice over. Flannel and a bowl here by the fire instead, just hands and face? Or nothing at all — that's allowed too."
- **P:** "…The bowl by the fire I'll take. But only because you warmed the towel."
- **CW:** "Done. And Eileen — that's two days without the full wash now. If tomorrow's a no as well, I'll mention it to the office, only so the nurse can check there's nothing making it hard that we could fix."
- **P:** "Three days is when you lot start worrying, is it? Go on then. Tomorrow we'll see."

### Flow 3 *(safety-critical ⚠ — the refusals cross the threshold)*
- **CW:** *(on the phone)* "Donna, it's Anna, at Eileen's. I need to flag something — it's the water tablets."
- **C:** "Go on — I've got her file open."
- **CW:** ⚠ "That's three mornings running she's refused them. Each one's written in the book with the time. One no is her right, but three is a pattern — and her ankles are puffier than Friday."
- **C:** "Three days and swelling — you were right to ring. I'll get the district nurse out today. Anything else changed?"
- **CW:** "She's off her food a bit too — half the toast, no egg. It's all in the notes. And Donna — she doesn't want a fuss made, so whoever comes, gently does it."
- **C:** "Noted, all of it. The nurse will ring you before she goes in. Thanks, Anna — good catch."

## 1.4 The tablets

*Walks:* N102, N103, F103, N106, O101

### Flow 1 *(happy path — the check, even after years)*
- **CW:** "Tablet time. You know it's not me being funny — I have to look at the sheet every time, even after two years of your tea."
- **P:** "Go on, do your checking. Eileen Prosser, fourth of May, thirty-nine — I'll say it before you ask."
- **CW:** "And the sheet agrees with you, as usual. Two white ones this morning, and the little yellow one — the round white one's not till teatime."
- **P:** "The yellow one sticks. Pass the big glass, not the little one."
- **CW:** "Big glass. There — all three down. I'll sign the sheet, and that's us square till teatime."
- **P:** "Square till teatime. Now sit down and drink your tea before it stews."

### Flow 2 *(question — the new one, audited)*
- **CW:** "Tablets now. Two white ones, the yellow one — and there's a new one this morning, a pink one."
- **P:** "A pink one? What's that when it's at home? Nobody told me about a pink one."
- **CW:** "Quite right to stop me. The sheet says the doctor started it Tuesday, after your blood test — it's for your blood pressure, instead of the old blue one, not as well as."
- **P:** "Instead of, not as well as. And the blue one's finished with, then?"
- **CW:** "Finished with and going back to the chemist with me today, so it can't muddle anybody. And Eileen — you keep asking about every strange tablet, always. You're the best safety check either of us has got."
- **P:** "I'll ask, don't you worry. It's my insides they're going into."

### Flow 3 *(safety-critical ⚠ — the box is wrong: stop and verify)*
- **CW:** "Tablet time. Chemist's delivered the new box — let me just… hold on."
- **P:** "What's the matter? You've gone all still."
- **CW:** ⚠ "These aren't right. The sheet says two white ones in the morning, and this pack's got three in the morning slot — and the yellow one's missing altogether. So we stop right there: nothing gets taken till I'm certain."
- **P:** "Well, I'm not taking a stranger's tablets, and that's flat."
- **CW:** "Nor will you. I'm ringing the office now, and they'll get the chemist on it this morning — the old box does today, there's enough left, and I'll watch for the new one tomorrow. You'll not go without and you'll not take a wrong one; that's the whole job."
- **P:** "Good. You can put that in your book in big letters, too."

## 1.5 The kitchen and the chart

### Flow 1 *(happy path — dinner, and the chart that follows it)*
- **CW:** "Right — dinner. There's the chops Cath brought, or I can do you the cheese and potato thing you liked last week."
- **P:** "The cheese and potato. The chops can be tomorrow, when there's more of me to chew them."
- **CW:** "Cheese and potato it is, and I'll cut up an apple for after. You had the full bowl of porridge this morning — that's going on the chart, it's the best breakfast this week."
- **P:** "That chart of yours knows more about my stomach than I do."
- **CW:** "That's the point of it — the evening carer reads it, and the nurse reads it, so nobody has to guess and nobody keeps asking you. Eat well today and tomorrow the chart says so."
- **P:** "Then put the apple on it as well. Every bite counts, you said."

### Flow 2 *(problem — the fridge, with dignity)*
- **CW:** "While the potatoes are on — that ham in the fridge has gone past its date, Eileen. Friday, it says."
- **P:** "There's nothing wrong with that ham. Waste not, want not — we didn't throw food away in my day."
- **CW:** "And your day fed a family on half of what mine wastes, I know. But past-date ham can put you in bed for a week, and then who'd boss me about? It's not worth the gamble."
- **P:** "Hm. You do the gambling face very well."
- **CW:** "Learned it from you. How's this — it goes out, and I put ham on Thursday's shopping list, fresh, and you're not a penny worse off by the weekend."
- **P:** "Go on then, bin it. But the cheese stays — the date on cheese is a suggestion."

## 1.6 "You won't tell anyone" — the disclosure

*Walks:* N204, O7, F204, N109

### Flow 1 *(human moment — heard, not fixed)*
- **CW:** "There's your tea. I'll do the sink while you drink it."
- **P:** "Leave the sink a minute. Sit down by me. It gets very quiet in this house, Anna. After you go, there's nobody till the morning."
- **CW:** "That's a long stretch of quiet, teatime to breakfast."
- **P:** "It is. I talk to Smokey, and I talk to Ron's photograph, and I'm not sure which of them listens better. You'll think I'm soft."
- **CW:** "I think you're honest, and I'm glad you said it to me and not just to the cat. Tell me about the quiet — is it worse in winter?"
- **P:** "Worse when the clocks change. There — I've said it now. Go and do your sink, and we'll say no more today."

### Flow 2 *(question — the promise asked for, and bounded)*
- **CW:** "You've been circling something all morning, Eileen. Out with it, if you want to."
- **P:** "If I tell you a thing — you won't tell anyone, will you? Promise me first."
- **CW:** "I can't promise that, sweetheart — I won't lie to you. If it's something that worries me, I have to pass it on; that's the one rule I can't bend, even for you. But you can still tell me, and nothing gets passed on without you knowing I've done it."
- **P:** "That's not much of a promise, is it?"
- **CW:** "It's the only honest one I've got — the rest of me is all yours: I'll listen as long as you like, and nothing you say will change how I am with you."
- **P:** "…Leave it today, then. Maybe Thursday. Don't you go anywhere, Thursday."

### Flow 3 *(safety-critical ⚠ — the threshold crossed)*
- **CW:** "It's Thursday, and I'm going nowhere. Is it about the money, Eileen? There's been no cash in your purse for the shopping twice now."
- **P:** ⚠ "It's Gary. My nephew. He borrows my card, for his petrol, he says — and it's more than petrol, Anna, it's a lot more. But he's family. I don't like to say no to him, and you mustn't say anything."
- **CW:** "I'm really glad you've told me — that took some doing. And I have to be straight with you: this is one of the things I can't keep, even though you've asked me. It goes to the office today."
- **P:** "No — no, you'll set them all on him, and he'll say I'm losing my marbles—"
- **CW:** "Nobody's setting anyone on anyone, and nobody thinks that of you — being robbed isn't losing your marbles. What happens next is somebody kind comes and talks to YOU, about what YOU want — and I'll be here Tuesday same as always, and Thursday after that. You won't be on your own for a single step of it."
- **P:** "…You'll really be here Tuesday? Whatever happens?"

## 1.7 The pass-on

*Walks:* N501, F101, F103, N105, F16

### Flow 1 *(safety-critical ⚠ — announced, and done)*
- **CW:** "Before I go, I'm saying it plainly so there's no surprises: what you told me about Gary and the card, I'm reporting to the safeguarding team this afternoon."
- **P:** "And if I say don't?"
- **CW:** ⚠ "Then I do it anyway, and I hate that answer, but it's the true one — this one isn't a choice I get to make, or you get to make for me. Money being taken from you is the kind of harm I'm not allowed to sit on."
- **P:** "It sounds so hard, when you say it like that. 'Harm.' He's my sister's boy."
- **CW:** "I know he is, and nobody's asking you to stop loving him. The team's job is to make the taking stop — not to punish you, and what happens about Gary is their call and yours, never mine. I've written down only what you told me, in your own words — do you want to hear it back before I send it?"
- **P:** "…Yes. Read it to me. If it's going, it's going in my words."

### Flow 2 *(problem — the press, from someone you'll see Tuesday)*
- **CW:** "Morning, Eileen — it's only me."
- **P:** "Don't you 'only me' me. The safeguarding people rang Cath. I trusted you, Anna. You've ruined everything."
- **CW:** "You did trust me, and I did report it, and I'd report it again tomorrow — so be as cross with me as you like, and I'll still be here Tuesday, and Thursday, and every day after that. That part never changes."
- **P:** "Everyone will know my business now. Sixty years I've kept my own business."
- **CW:** "Four people know, and every one of them is on your side — and what happens next is decided with you at the table, not about you. The team don't move an inch without asking you first; that's their own rule, not mine."
- **P:** "…You'd best put the kettle on, then. And you're still not forgiven till at least Thursday."

## 1.8 "She's not herself"

*Walks:* N12, F302, F101, N105, N107, F103

### Flow 1 *(safety-critical ⚠ — the gut reading, priced and escalated)*
- **CW:** *(on the phone)* "Is that the district nurse? It's Anna — I'm with Eileen Prosser, and I want eyes on her today."
- **C:** "Go on — what are you seeing?"
- **CW:** "I can't put my finger on it, and I'll say that straight out — no fever, no fall. She's just not right. She didn't want her toast, and she always wants her toast. She's muddled her granddaughter's name twice, and that's not her — she's sharp as you like, normally."
- **C:** ⚠ "Off her food and new confusion, in an older lady — that's worth listening to, and I'm coming this morning. And hear me on this before I go: if she goes drowsy, or you can't rouse her properly, don't wait for me — ring for an ambulance straight away. Say that back to me."
- **CW:** "Drowsy, or I can't rouse her properly: ambulance straight away, you second, no waiting. And it's going in the book with the time you said it."
- **C:** "That's it exactly. I'll be there within the hour — and keep writing what you're seeing, times and all. It's the times that tell the story."

### Flow 2 *(problem — the report pressed until it's taken up)*
- **CW:** *(on the phone)* "Donna, it's Anna again — about Eileen. I rang yesterday, and nothing's been out yet."
- **C:** "It's on the list, Anna — we've two off sick and the list's long. She's eating, isn't she? It might settle."
- **CW:** "It might, and I'm not a nurse — but I'm the one who sees her every day, and every day this week has been a step down. It's in the notes three days running now: Monday the toast, Tuesday the names, today she didn't want to get out of the chair. That's three flags, not one."
- **C:** "…Three days running. Alright, you've made your case — read me the Monday note, I'll take it down while you're on."
- **CW:** "'Didn't finish breakfast, first time in months. Quieter than usual. Knee no worse — this is new.' That's Monday. Want Tuesday's as well?"
- **C:** "No — that's enough. I'm moving her up the list; the nurse will be there by two. Keep writing it the way you're writing it, Anna — it's the notes that got her seen."

## 1.9 The daughter

*Walks:* N104, N109, F401, F501, N401

### Flow 1 *(happy path — the update, referenced to her own baseline)*
- **F:** "How's she doing, Anna? Honestly, now."
- **CW:** "Honestly: she's eating less than she was — and for your mum, that's a change worth watching. Anyone else, half a plate is a diet; for Eileen it's news."
- **F:** "She's always been a good eater. Is she poorly, then?"
- **CW:** "The nurse checked her Tuesday and there's nothing sinister — it may just be a low patch. But she's alright in herself: she bossed me through the crossword this morning and won."
- **F:** "But you'd tell me? If it got worse — you'd ring me, not wait for the review?"
- **CW:** "I won't promise what I can't be sure of, so I'll promise what I can: anything that worries me goes in the book the same day, the office the same day, and your phone the same evening. That's the deal I can keep."

### Flow 2 *(question — the story, challenged with the chart)*
- **F:** "Don't bother with fish for her, by the way. She won't touch it — never has."
- **CW:** "Won't she, now?"
- **F:** "Never, our whole lives. My father used to bring it home Fridays and she'd cook it for us and have an egg herself, every single week. She's never liked it — you can take that as gospel."
- **CW:** "I'd have taken it as gospel — except she cleared a plate of it a fortnight Friday. Poached, with parsley sauce. I wrote it on the chart because I couldn't believe it myself — look, there it is, her best dinner that week."
- **F:** "She never did! Fish? My mother?"
- **CW:** "Every scrap, and asked was there more. People surprise you at eighty-four — or maybe she spent fifty years giving the fish to you lot. Shall I keep it on the menu?"

### Flow 3 *(problem — the family instruction against her wishes)*
- **F:** "One more thing — the sherry. I've told her and I'll tell you: no more evening sherry. Her balance is bad enough. I mean it, Anna."
- **CW:** "I hear you, and I know it comes from a good place — and I can't do it. It's her sherry and her choice: while she understands what it is and what it risks, nobody can take it off her. That's the law protecting her, not me being awkward."
- **F:** "Her choice? She's eighty-four and she fell in the night not three weeks ago!"
- **CW:** "She did — and she's still allowed to weigh that up herself, the same as you and me. What I CAN do: I've told her plainly what the nurse said about the balance, it's written that I've told her, and she has it earlier now, with her tea instead of at bedtime — that was her own idea, when we talked it through."
- **F:** "So I just have to live with it, do I?"
- **CW:** "You can do better than live with it — bring it to the review on the twelfth. If the falls risk has changed, that's the room where it gets looked at properly, with her in it. She listens to you more than you think, Cath — but at the table, not through the keyhole."

## 1.10 "Not allowed, my love"

*Walks:* N401, P101

- **P:** "Anna — sit down a minute. I want you to witness my will. You're the only one I trust since all this Gary business."

## 1.11 The shopping, and the doorway

*Walks:* P2, F103, N104, N105, F6

### Flow 1 *(happy path — the purse held in trust)*
- **CW:** "Shopping's in. Here's the receipt, and here's your change — count it with me? I make it four items, and the receipt agrees."
- **P:** "Bread, milk, ham — fresh ham, see, like you promised — and the biscuits. And the change is right."
- **CW:** "Then you sign under my name in the money book — two names, one story. That way if anyone ever asks, the book answers and neither of us has to remember."
- **P:** "Hold on — I make the change two short, now I look. You've been diddled at the till."
- **CW:** "Have I? Let's let the receipt decide — that's what we keep it for. Look — the milk's gone up since last week. There's your two."
- **P:** "The price of milk! Daylight robbery, and all legal. Alright — where do I sign?"

### Flow 2 *(safety-critical ⚠ — the doorway, with capacity)*
- **P:** "Fetch my coat, would you — I'm walking to the post office for my pension. I've done it every Thursday for fifty years."
