## Why gender is 0, honestly

Not an oversight. Dutch and German do not inflect predicate adjectives for gender — *ik ben moe* / *ich bin müde* is the same in any mouth — and no draft line carries a gendered profession noun or participle. The cast is also explicitly gendered in all three courses (no stale `'n'` to resolve): the Learner is female in each — Noor, Lena, Sonja. So rule 2 has no surface to act on here.

## Left alone, deliberately

- **Peer scenes 1, 4, 5, 6, 22** — `je`/`du` is correct there under rule 3's default.
- **General solo lines** ("if you can speak slowly", "when you talk quickly it makes me feel stupid", "it sounds as though you want us not to do that") — no service cue in the line, so default T stands.
- **Two genuine judgment calls, logged not changed:** *Vind je het goed als ik hier ga zitten?* and its sibling *…als we dit hier neerzetten?* — asking to sit is *often* said to a stranger, but the line does not clearly say so, and rule 3's default wins ties. One word from you flips both to `u`.
- **The scene-20 thanking run** (six Dutch lines: "thank you for all your work", "you're very kind", "thank you for being so friendly") — left T. If you read scene 20 as still standing at the ice-cream counter, those six flip to `u`/`uw` together.
- **`Ga rechtdoor over deze weg`** — the Dutch bare imperative carries no T/V marking at all, so there was nothing to fix.

## Two things you should know

**1. The Dutch scenes are now mixed register, and only you can close it.** My scope was `target_text_draft = true`. **24 NON-draft Dutch lines in the same service scenes still say `je`/`alsjeblieft`** — the barista's *Wil je normaal of groot?*, Sarah's *Dank je wel*, five customers' *alsjeblieft*, the shop assistant's *Ben je hier op vakantie?*. Scene 7 in particular now has the customer saying `u` and the barista answering `je`. Those lines are marked human-proofread, so I did not touch them. Say the word and I will sweep them in the same shape.

**2. Austrian German is written in heavy Viennese dialect spelling** — *i mechat an Kaffee*, *hobn S' wos zum Essen*, *Kennan S' ma sogn*. Its register was already right (that is why only one line moved). Whether a learner course should teach that orthography is a taste call, not an A-108 defect, so I changed nothing.

## Confidence

Dutch and German: confident to native standard on T/V. Austrian: confident on the *register* judgment (the `S'`/`du` boundary is the same as standard German); I am **not** claiming native authority on the dialect spelling, which is why I left it alone rather than normalising it.
