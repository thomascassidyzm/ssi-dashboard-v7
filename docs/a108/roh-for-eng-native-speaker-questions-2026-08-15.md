# roh_for_eng — questions for a Romansh speaker

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Raised by:** the decomposition of seeds 1–10 of `roh_for_eng`

Nobody on this build speaks Romansh. Everything below was decided from evidence inside
the 668 existing translations alone — never from a guess about the language. These are
the points where that evidence ran out and a speaker has to rule.

**How to answer:** you need to know nothing about our system. Each question is a plain
question about Romansh, with the sentences it affects written out. Answer in a sentence.

Every question is written for **Rumantsch Grischun**, which is what all 668 sentences
are written in.

---

## BLOCKING — the course currently teaches a guess here

### Q1. Are `empruvar` and `ma stentar` the same "try", or different?

Two sentences in the course use different verbs for the English word *try*:

| | English | Romansh |
|---|---|---|
| Seed 7 | I want to **try** as hard as I can today. | jau vi **ma stentar** uschè fitg sco che jau poss oz |
| Seed 8 | I'm going to **try** to explain what I mean. | jau vegn ad **empruvar** da declerar tge che jau manegel |

Everywhere else in the 668 sentences (about 18 of them), *try/trying* is `empruvar`.
Seed 7 is the only place `ma stentar` appears at all.

**Question:** does `ma stentar` mean something narrower than `empruvar` — something like
*make an effort / try hard / push yourself* — or is it just another way of saying *try*?

**Why it matters:** we currently teach `ma stentar` on a card labelled **"to try hard"**
and `empruvar` on a card labelled **"I'm going to try"**, so the learner is never given
two different Romansh words for one English intention. If they really are
interchangeable, that label is wrong and we should use `empruvar` in both places. If
`ma stentar` genuinely means *try hard*, our labelling is right and nothing changes.

### Q2. Can you say `jau vi emprender` — "I want to learn"?

Seed 2 is *I'm trying to learn* → `jau emprov d'emprender`. We need the bare form of
*to learn* so the learner can build other sentences with it, and we have taught it as
`emprender` on the strength of these existing sentences:

- Seed 20: ti vuls **emprender** ses num svelt
- Seed 64: **emprender** rumantsch n'è betg simpel
- Seed 109: nus stuain lavurar fitg per **emprender** blers pleds novs

**Question:** are these correct, exactly as written?
- **jau vi emprender rumantsch** — I want to learn Romansh
- **jau vi emprender rumantsch cun tai ussa** — I want to learn Romansh with you now

---

### Q3. Does `ma regurdar` need `da` before what is remembered?

Seed 6 is *I'm trying to remember a word* → `jau emprov da ma regurdar **d'in pled**`.
So "a word" appears as `d'in pled`, not `in pled`. Seed 113 does the same thing:
`ma regurdar **da** quai che ti has ditg`.

**Question:** is it always `ma regurdar da …` — i.e. is **`ma regurdar in pled`** (with
no `d'`) simply wrong?

We have assumed yes and never written `ma regurdar` with a bare object. If we are wrong,
we have made the language harder than it is.

---

### Q4. Is `co` + verb natural in these sentences?

Existing seed 57 has `ma regurdar **co dir** quai` and seed 60 has `jau na sai anc betg
**co dir** avunda pleds`. We have built new practice sentences on that pattern:

- **jau vi emprender co discurrer rumantsch** — I want to learn how to speak Romansh
- **jau vi ma regurdar co dir in pled** — I want to remember how to say a word
- **jau vegn a ma regurdar co discurrer rumantsch** — I'm going to remember how to speak Romansh

**Question:** do these sound right, or does Romansh need something between the verb and
`co` here?

---

### Q5. Is `per rumantsch` the way to say "in Romansh"?

Seed 4 is *how to say something in Romansh* → `co dir insatge **per rumantsch**`. This
is the only place in all 668 sentences where "in Romansh" appears in that form, so we
have one example and no way to check it.

**Question:** is `per rumantsch` correct for *in Romansh* (as in "say it in Romansh"),
and is it right in all of these?

- jau vi dir insatge per rumantsch — I want to say something in Romansh
- jau vegn a declerar insatge per rumantsch — I'm going to explain something in Romansh
- jau vi emprender in pled per rumantsch — I want to learn a word in Romansh

## NON-BLOCKING — please rule when convenient

### Q6. Word order: where does `in pau` ("a little") go?

Seed 9 is `jau discur **in pau** rumantsch ussa`. We have written:

- **jau poss discurrer in pau rumantsch** — I can speak a little Romansh
- **jau vi discurrer in pau rumantsch cun tai oz** — I want to speak a little Romansh with you today

**Question:** is `in pau` in the right place in both?

### Q7. Word order: where does the time word go?

We have put `oz` (today) and `ussa` (now) at the end throughout, following seeds 1, 7
and 9. Is that right in longer sentences such as:

- **jau vegn a discurrer rumantsch cun insatgi auter ussa** — I'm going to speak Romansh with someone else now
- **jau emprov da declerar insatge per rumantsch oz** — I'm trying to explain something in Romansh today

### Q8. Is `jau vi declerar` natural, or does `declerar` want an object?

We teach `declerar` = *to explain* and have written **jau vi declerar insatge** (I want
to explain something). Seed 211 has `els na vulevan betg declerar` with no object at all,
so both seem possible — but please confirm `declerar insatge` is idiomatic.

### Q9. The comparison frame — can it take any verb?

The existing sentences use `uschè … sco …` for *as … as*: `uschè savens sco pussaivel`
(as often as possible, seed 3) and `uschè fitg sco che jau poss` (as hard as I can,
seed 7). We have reused the second one with other verbs:

- **jau vi trenar uschè fitg sco che jau poss oz** — I want to practise as hard as I can today
- **jau vi discurrer rumantsch uschè fitg sco che jau poss** — I want to speak Romansh as hard as I can

**Question:** does `uschè fitg` work with these verbs, or is it tied to effort verbs only?

### Q10. `trenar da discurrer` — is that the ordinary way to say "practise speaking"?

Seed 5 has `trenar da discurrer`, and seeds 206 and 228 use the same phrase. We have
treated it as fixed. Is `trenar` the everyday word, or is it more like *train*?

## FOR AN EDITOR RATHER THAN A SPEAKER — defects already in the 668 translations

These are not decomposition questions. They are faults we found **in the existing
translations** while reading them. None of them is in seeds 1–10, so none affects what
was built today, but they will bite whoever continues.

### E1. Five sentences say "Yoruba" where the English says "Romansh"

| Seed | English | Romansh (as stored) |
|---|---|---|
| 160 | How do you say this word in Romansh? | co di ins quest pled per **joruba**? |
| 283 | Which of your friends speak Romansh? | tgenins da tes amis discurran **joruba**? |
| 285 | She speaks Romansh. | ella discurra **joruba** |
| 286 | People who like speaking Romansh. | glieud che discurra gugent **joruba** |
| 297 | I don't know many people who speak Romansh. | jau n'enconusch betg blera glieud che discurra **joruba** |

`joruba` is Yoruba. This is copy-paste contamination from a different course's corpus.
All five should read `rumantsch`. **They must be fixed before those seeds are
decomposed** — otherwise the course will teach learners to say they speak Yoruba.

### E2. "soon" is translated four different ways

| Romansh | Seeds |
|---|---|
| bainbaud | 23, 149, 397 |
| prest | 291, 431 |
| baud (inside "as soon as") | 28, 29 |
| spert (inside "as soon as") | 97 |

Our rule is one English intention → one Romansh form, course-wide. A speaker should pick
which one *soon* is, and which one goes inside *as soon as*, before seeds past 22 are
built. (`baud` and `spert` may be a genuine distinction — *as soon as you can* vs *as
quickly as you want* — so this may be two decisions, not one.)
