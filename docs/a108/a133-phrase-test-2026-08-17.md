# A-133 — the phrase test: five different lines per voice, before and after

Every clip below is a **fresh TTS call through the wired chain** — nothing here is a re-processed archive take. 55 renders across 11 voices, five different lines each, chosen so the five probe five different ways the end-of-speech detector could be wrong rather than one line five times.

**AFTER** is `phase8.masterAudio()` — the real chain, one call, the trim under test inside it. **BEFORE** is `normalizeAudioClean()`, byte-identical to what that chain did before this branch. Play them in that order.

## What the numbers say

- **Guard refusals: 0 of 55.**
- **Words lost to the trim: 0 of 55.** Checked by reading the before clip and the after clip with local whisper and comparing them *to each other* — see the note on register below.
- **Post-speech impulses: 14 before → 4 after.** These are the clicks.
- **Loudness moved at most 0.2 LUFS** on any clip.

### The 4 impulses the chain did NOT remove — and why that is right

The trim keeps 250ms past the detected end of speech, so by construction it cannot remove anything closer in than that. Every survivor in this batch is inside that window and sits on a word-final plosive:

| clip | last word | impulse lands | level over room floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thijs p3 | *alsjeblieft* | 40ms past end of speech | 19.2dB |
| Tom — your own clone p2 | *stop* | 45ms past end of speech | 27dB |
| Olivia p2 | *stop* | 25ms past end of speech | 5.8dB |
| Olivia p5 | *stopped* | 31ms past end of speech | 59dB |

These are release bursts — the /p/ of *stop*, the /t/ of *stopped* and *alsjeblieft* — not clicks. The detector's one documented weakness is that a plosive burst can be shorter than its 40ms sustained-speech rule and so read as an impulse, which is exactly what has happened, and it is why those five lines were in the batch. Cutting them would clip the last consonant off the word. The chain leaves them, and ASR confirms the final word survives on all 4. The impulses it *did* remove all sat 380–500ms out, which is where the A-133 tail click actually lives.

### Why the word check compares clip to clip, not clip to script

The first smoke render had Ruben saying *alsjeblieft* where the script says *alstublieft* — the informal form. Whisper is already documented to flip that exact Dutch pair between model sizes on the same clip, so an absolute check against the script calls a register choice a missing word. What a trim can actually do wrong is REMOVE something, so the check asks whether the after clip still ends the way the before clip did. Where the two differ from the script in the same way, that is the voice, not the chain, and it is noted rather than scored.
