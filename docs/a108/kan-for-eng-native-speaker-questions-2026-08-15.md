# Questions for a Kannada speaker — kan_for_eng

**2026-08-15.** Nobody who built this course speaks Kannada. Everything below is a place
where the build had to make a choice it could not verify. Each question is written so a
Kannada speaker can answer it **without knowing anything about how our system works**.

You do **not** need to read any code, and you do **not** need to know what a "LEGO" is.
Where we say *card*, we mean one small piece of Kannada we teach the learner on its own.

**BLOCKING** means the course currently teaches a guess.

---

## A0. BLOCKING — "to do something": which ending do we teach?

This is the biggest question by a long way, and it affects Kannada already written into
the course.

Kannada seems to have several ways of writing "to ___" at the end of a verb, and the 668
existing translations use **four different ones**, sometimes for the very same verb.

Counting word-endings across all 668 sentences:

| Ending | How many words end this way |
|---|---|
| **-ೋಕೆ** | **121** |
| -ೋದು | 46 |
| -ಕ್ಕೆ | 29 |
| -ಲು | 25 |

And for a single verb, "to remember", the sentences disagree with each other:

- Sentence 6 says **ನೆನಪಿಸಿಕೊಳ್ಳಲು** ("I'm trying to remember a word.")
- Sentences 10, 24 and 57 say **ನೆನಪಿಸಿಕೊಳ್ಳಕ್ಕೆ** (e.g. "I'm not sure if I can remember
  the whole sentence.")

"To speak" is split three ways too: **ಮಾತಾಡಲು** (sentences 23, 28), **ಮಾತಾಡೋಕೆ**
(88, 291), **ಮಾತಾಡೋದು** (3, 137, 286).

**The questions:**

1. Are these endings genuinely interchangeable — would a Kannada speaker use any of them
   in the same sentence and sound equally natural?
2. If they are **not** interchangeable, what decides which one you use? (Does it depend on
   the verb that follows, e.g. "trying to ___" vs "starting to ___"?)
3. If we had to teach a learner **one** of them as their default, which should it be? We
   would guess **-ೋಕೆ** simply because it is the most common in the sentences we have, but
   that is arithmetic, not knowledge.

**Why it is blocking:** the course currently teaches ಕಲಿಯಲು ("to learn") and
ನೆನಪಿಸಿಕೊಳ್ಳಲು ("to remember"), both with **-ಲು**, because those are the endings the
individual seed sentences happened to use. If the answer is that -ೋಕೆ is the right default,
then **37 practice sentences already in the database** need rewriting (25 containing
ಕಲಿಯಲು, 12 containing ನೆನಪಿಸಿಕೊಳ್ಳಲು), along with the two cards that introduce them.

## A1. BLOCKING — does ಅಂದುಕೊಂಡಿದ್ದೀನಿ mean "I mean"?

Sentence 8 is *"I'm going to try to explain what I mean."* and reads
**ನಾನು ಏನು ಹೇಳಬೇಕು ಅಂದುಕೊಂಡಿದ್ದೀನಿ ಅನ್ನೋದನ್ನ ವಿವರಿಸಲು ಪ್ರಯತ್ನಿಸಲಿದ್ದೀನಿ.**

We could not work out whether **ಅಂದುಕೊಂಡಿದ್ದೀನಿ** on its own is something a person would
say, or whether it only works locked into that longer phrase.

1. Can you say **ಅಂದುಕೊಂಡಿದ್ದೀನಿ** by itself? If so, what does it mean in plain English?
2. Is it about the present ("I mean", "I think") or about the past ("I thought",
   "I decided")?
3. Is **ಏನು … ಅನ್ನೋದನ್ನ** a fixed frame that wraps a whole clause, the way English wraps one
   in "what … is"? Or are those two words independent?

**Why it is blocking:** because we could not answer this, **sentence 8 was deliberately
left undecomposed**. It is the one gap in the block we built (1–7 and 9 are done). We would
rather leave a hole than teach a card whose meaning we invented.

## A2. Informal or formal "you" — and a drift in the later sentences

The sentences overwhelmingly use the **informal** you (ನೀನು / ನಿನ್ನ). Measured across all
668 by looking for those words: **158 sentences informal, 21 formal** (ನೀವು / ನಿಮ್ಮ).

The 21 formal ones are **not scattered** — they are almost all bunched at the very end:
sentences 501, 529, and then a solid run from 639 to 667.

1. Is informal ನೀನು the right register for a friendly language course where the learner is
   talking to a practice partner?
2. Is that run at 639–667 a mistake that should be made informal like the rest?

*(Not blocking for what has been built — every sentence from 1 to 9 is informal and
consistent.)*

Related: the **released** English-for-Kannada-speakers course goes the other way — it
mostly uses formal ನಿಮ್ಮ, and writes "I am trying" as ಪ್ರಯತ್ನಿಸ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ where this course
writes ಪ್ರಯತ್ನಿಸ್ತಿದ್ದೀನಿ. **Should the two courses match each other, or is it fine for them
to differ?**

## A3. Is ಮಾತಾಡಬೇಕು really "want to speak"?

We teach **ಮಾತಾಡಬೇಕು** as *"want to speak"*, and we teach it as **person-neutral** — the
same word for "I want to speak" and "you want to speak", with ನಾನು / ನೀನು in front doing
the work of saying who.

We concluded that because sentence 1 has ನಾನು … ಮಾತಾಡಬೇಕು and sentence 15 has
ನೀನು … ಮಾತಾಡಬೇಕು — the same verb form with different subjects.

1. Is that right — is ಮಾತಾಡಬೇಕು the same regardless of who is speaking?
2. Does -ಬೇಕು mean "want to" or does it mean "must / have to"? English keeps those apart
   and we need to know which one a learner will hear.
3. Sentence 11 and seven others use **ಬಯಸ್ತೀನಿ** for "I'd like / I want". Is that a
   different shade of meaning from -ಬೇಕು, or the same thing said another way?

## A4. "with you" — is ನಿನ್ನ ಜೊತೆ two words or one idea?

We teach **ನಿನ್ನ ಜೊತೆ** as a single card meaning *"with you"*, and separately note that
ನಿನ್ನ is "you" and ಜೊತೆ is "with".

1. Is that a fair way to break it up?
2. Would a learner who knows ನಿನ್ನ ಜೊತೆ ("with you") correctly produce ಇನ್ನೊಬ್ಬರ ಜೊತೆ
   ("with someone else") by swapping the first word? That is what we are betting on.

---

## A5. "as often as possible" — we split it in two

Sentence 3 says **ಸಾಧ್ಯವಾದಷ್ಟು ಹೆಚ್ಚು ಬಾರಿ** for "as often as possible". That was too long
to teach as one piece, so we teach it as two: **ಹೆಚ್ಚು ಬಾರಿ** = "often", and
**ಸಾಧ್ಯವಾದಷ್ಟು** = "as possible".

1. Does **ಹೆಚ್ಚು ಬಾರಿ** on its own mean "often"? Would you say it by itself?
2. Does **ಸಾಧ್ಯವಾದಷ್ಟು** on its own mean something like "as much as possible"?
3. Sentence 7 uses a different phrase, **ನನ್ನಿಂದ ಆಗುವಷ್ಟು**, for "as hard as I can", and
   sentence 29 uses **ನನ್ನಿಂದ ಆದಷ್ಟು**. Are those the same thing? And does ನನ್ನಿಂದ mean
   they can only be used about **myself** — i.e. would they be wrong in a sentence about
   *you*?

## A6. The word for "a word", and a sentence we are unsure about

We teach **ಒಂದು ಪದ** as "a word" (ಒಂದು = "one", ಪದ = "word").

1. Is ಒಂದು needed, or would a Kannada speaker just say ಪದ?
2. We wrote the practice sentence **ನಾನು ಈಗ ಕನ್ನಡದಲ್ಲಿ ಒಂದು ಪದ ಮಾತಾಡಬೇಕು** for "I want to
   speak a word in Kannada now". Does that sound wrong? In English you *say* a word, you
   don't *speak* a word — does Kannada have the same problem here?

## A7. Word order in our invented sentences

Every practice sentence we wrote follows the pattern we observed in the seeds: the person
first, then time, then who-with, then the thing, then the verb last. For example:

**ನಾನು ಇವತ್ತು ಇನ್ನೊಬ್ಬರ ಜೊತೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಮಾತಾಡ್ತೀನಿ**
(I / today / with someone else / Kannada / speak)

1. Does that order sound natural?
2. Is there anywhere in the sentences we built where the order sounds wrong, even if the
   words are all correct?

## A8. Sentence 31 — a possible mistake in the existing translation

Sentence 31 is *"You wanted to speak with me tonight."* and reads
**ನೀನು ಇವತ್ತು ರಾತ್ರಿ ನನ್ನ ಜೊತೆ ಮಾತಾಡಬೇಕು ಅಂದುಕೊಂಡಿದ್ದೆ.**

Sentence 30 is *"I wanted to ask you something yesterday."* and ends with the **same**
word **ಅಂದುಕೊಂಡಿದ್ದೆ**, but its subject is ನಾನು ("I"), not ನೀನು ("you").

Should the ending change when the subject changes from "I" to "you"? Sentence 32 ("Did you
want to show me something?") uses **ಅಂದುಕೊಂಡಿದ್ದೆಯಾ**, which looks like it does.

*(Not blocking — sentences 30–32 are not built yet.)*

## How to answer

You can answer in plain English, in any order, and you can say "I don't know" — that is
genuinely useful too. The ones that hold up the course are **A0** and **A1**.
