# The over-long lesson labels: what actually changed

Applied to the live database on 28 August 2026. Every number below was read back
from live rows after the change, not predicted before it.

## The one-line version

**53 of the 70 lessons are fixed, 146 recorded practice sentences that no learner
could reach are now switched on, and it cost nothing in audio — not one clip was
generated.**

## The counts

| | |
|---|---|
| Lesson labels corrected | **52** |
| Lessons absorbed (taken off the path because the word was already taught) | **1** |
| Silent lessons switched back on | **14** (146 practice sentences) |
| New practice sentences written | **1** |
| Existing recordings re-pointed to the corrected labels | **156** |
| **New recordings generated** | **0** |
| **Money spent on audio** | **£0.00** |
| Cases held back | **17** |

## Why nothing had to be recorded

Every corrected label is a phrase the same lesson already drills, and every one of
those already had its three recordings. The database turned out to have a rule
built into it: when a lesson's words change, it looks for an existing recording of
the new words *in the same voice* and moves the link across rather than throwing it
away. It found one 156 times out of 156 — the learner hears the same speaker
saying the right word.

The four duplicate labels the plan flagged: French 395 and French 408 are done.
Arabic 419 and English-for-Korean 61 are in the held group below.

## What a learner notices

Take German seed 445. The lesson was labelled *that suitcase* / **diesen Koffer**,
but every sentence it drilled was built on **diesen** — *this*. The next lesson was
labelled *suitcase* and correctly built **Koffer → diesen Koffer**. The learner was
being shown the answer to the next lesson as the title of this one.

Now that lesson is labelled *this* / **diesen**, which is what it teaches.

One thing does change for the worse, temporarily. Each of these 52 lessons had a
spoken introduction — "The German for *that suitcase*, as in…, is:" — and that
sentence is now wrong, so it has been unlinked rather than left lying. Until it is
re-recorded the learner hears the plain word instead of the sentence around it.
That re-recording is queued as an approved audio pass across the ten affected
courses; roughly 24,000 lessons across the estate already sit in exactly that state,
so it is a known, handled shape, not a new fault.

## The 14 silent lessons

These are fully written and fully recorded lessons — 146 practice sentences, every
single one with all three recordings present — that were marked "not new" and so
never reached a learner at all. Every one was checked for its sentences and its
audio before being switched on; all 14 passed, none were short of anything.

Arabic 407 · Arabic (Lebanese) 123 · Chinese 198 · Chinese 551 · French 600 ·
German 537 · German 634 · Italian 308 · Korean 454 · Korean 596 · Korean 636 ·
Korean 643 · Korean 647 · Spanish (Mexico) 106

This is the best-value item in the job: 146 sentences of finished, paid-for content
went from invisible to live for nothing.

## The one lesson absorbed

**German 475.** Its first lesson was labelled *many reasons* but drilled only
*many* — and *many* was already taught in its own lesson back at seed 109. So it
was re-teaching a known word. It has been taken off the learner path; the seed now
runs straight to the lesson that builds **Gründe → viele Gründe**. Nothing was
deleted — the lesson and its sentences are still in the database and can be
switched back on in one step.

## The one new sentence

**Brazilian Portuguese 161** had no rung anywhere that built *give me that book*
on its own — it only ever appeared wrapped inside a question. One build sentence
was added to close the gap. All three recordings for it already existed, so it too
cost nothing.

## The 17 held back, and why

### Three you asked to be held (untouched, as asked)

- **Korean 450** — the word appears nowhere earlier and the absorbing lesson has no scaffolding.
- **German 524** — the label never exists as a standalone chunk; wrong-label and missing-rung are indistinguishable from the data.
- **Spanish (Mexico) 211** — a wrong-person label (*they told us* vs *he told us*), not a length problem.

### Eleven that need eleven small recordings between them

In these the corrected label does exist elsewhere in the course with recordings —
but in a **different voice** from the one that lesson currently uses. The database
correctly refuses to swap a learner's voice mid-lesson, so it left those slots
empty. Applying the correction now would have left a lesson with no sound at all,
which is worse than a wrong label, so they were not applied.

They need **16 recordings in total** — a few pence of text-to-speech, in voices
already used by those exact courses. Say the word and they take minutes.

### Three where the live rows contradict the plan

These were stopped rather than applied, exactly as instructed.

- **Italian 308.** The plan absorbs this lesson because *un'amica* "was taught at
  seed 136". It was not. Seed 136 teaches *è una mia amica* and seed 265 teaches the
  masculine *un amico*; the elided feminine **un'amica** is introduced nowhere before
  seed 308. Absorbing the lesson would delete the only place a learner ever meets it.
  (Its silent companion lesson *di mia madre* **was** switched on, which is safe and
  is counted in the 14 above.)
- **Portuguese 408.** The plan absorbs this lesson on the grounds that *melhor* and
  *maneira* are long established — true, but the chunk the lesson actually drills is
  **essa**, and *essa* appears nowhere at all in the course before seed 408: not as a
  lesson, not inside a single practice sentence. Absorbing it un-teaches the word.
- **The Welsh anthem.** The plan says two incompatible things in one row: the label
  should become **bardd**, *and* the rung that drills *bardd* should be rewritten. They
  cannot both be right, and the rest of the course depends on the plural — *gwlad
  beirdd o fri*, *beirdd a chantorion* — so relabelling to the singular would leave
  every later line using a word never taught. This needs one human decision, not a
  guess.

## Also found, not acted on

**Brazilian Portuguese 161's second lesson** (*me dar*) is itself marked "not new"
and so is off the learner path — which means the new sentence added above will not
reach anyone until that lesson is switched on. It is not one of the 14: unlike those,
its word *dar* **is** taught earlier, at seed 54, so switching it on is a judgement
call the plan never assessed. Flagging rather than acting.
