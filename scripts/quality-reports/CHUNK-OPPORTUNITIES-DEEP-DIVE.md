# Chunk Opportunities - Deep Dive Analysis

_Detailed examination of high-impact chunk opportunities across all courses_

**Analysis Date:** 2026-02-02
**Total Dataset:** 5,867 LEGOs, 42,835 practice phrases across 5 languages

---

## Executive Summary

The analysis reveals significant opportunities for a "chunk-first" approach to LEGO decomposition. Across all five languages analyzed, we found:

- **1,150+ high-frequency patterns** (appearing 10+ times in practice phrases)
- **Universal chunks**: "want to", "I want", "I want to" appear 500-770+ times per course
- **Language gaps**: Many natural multi-word expressions appear in phrases but aren't taught as LEGOs
- **Overlap potential**: Single-letter LEGOs (a, i, to, he, in, etc.) appear in thousands of contexts

### Key Finding: The "Want To" Pattern

The bigram **"want to"** is the #1 most frequent pattern across ALL languages:
- German (deu_for_eng): 770 occurrences
- Arabic (ara_for_eng): 770 occurrences
- Chinese (zho_for_eng): 690 occurrences
- Welsh (cym_s_for_eng): 536 occurrences
- Spanish (spa_for_eng): 660 occurrences

**Total impact**: 3,426 phrases across 5 languages would benefit from teaching "want to" as a chunk.

**Current approach**: Likely taught as two separate LEGOs ("want" + "to")
**Chunk-first approach**: Teach "want to" as a single M-LEGO with components [want] + [to]

| Pattern | Frequency | Complement Type |

**Key Finding**: These patterns represent fundamental Chinese grammar (aspect markers, complements). They SHOULD be taught as chunks because:

**Expansion Needed**: Look for:

## Overlap Opportunities

### The "Letter-Level LEGO" Problem

The analysis revealed that many single-letter or very short LEGOs appear in thousands of contexts:

**German "a"**: 6,984 usages, 2,290 unique contexts
**Chinese "a"**: 7,567 usages, 3,659 unique contexts
**Arabic "i"**: 7,291 usages, 2,052 unique contexts

**The Issue**: These are likely substring matches (not actual LEGOs):
- "what" contains "a"
- "can" contains "a"
- "while" contains "i"

**Recommendation**:
1. Filter out single-letter LEGOs from overlap analysis
2. Focus on 3+ character LEGOs with high usage
3. Look for actual multi-word LEGOs that could benefit from overlapping variants

### Legitimate Overlap Candidates

After filtering single-letter matches, these LEGOs show genuine overlap potential:

**Spanish "I think"**: 400 usages, 173 contexts
- Context 1: "I think that what..."
- Context 2: "I think you should..."
- **Recommendation**: Create overlapping variants:
  - "I think that" (subordinate clause introducer)
  - "I think you" (second-person opinion)

**German "with you"**: 297 usages
- Frequently combines with verbs: "speak with you", "practice with you", "learn with you"
- **Recommendation**: Create M-LEGOs for "speak with you", "practice with you"

**Welsh "something"**: 382 usages, 219 contexts
- Appears in patterns like "do something", "find something", "learn something"
- **Recommendation**: Consider "do something", "learn something" as chunks
