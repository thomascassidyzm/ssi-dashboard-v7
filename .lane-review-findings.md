## 1. Hindi speaker conversion changes a different person's gender (P2)

**Location:** `tools/course-optimization/hin-for-eng-speaker-gender-2026-10-14.cjs:105` (clause splitting at lines 114–121).

**Concrete failing input:** `plan([{ kind: 'use', id: 'review-case', sn: 1, k: 'I was tired and he was sleeping', t: 'मैं थका था और वह सो रहा था।' }])`.

**Expected:** `expanded_f` is `मैं थकी थी और वह सो रहा था।` — only the speaker becomes feminine.

**Actual:** `expanded_f` is `मैं थकी थी और वह सो रही थी।` — the male third-person subject becomes feminine too.

`clauses()` does not split at `और` or before `वह`. `femClause()` therefore sees `मैं` anywhere in the combined sentence and feminises every auxiliary/verb group, even though `other` is true. `plan()` passes this reading to `applyExpansions()`, which inserts it into `course_gender_expansions`; `loadGenderMap()` in `services/gender-haiku-service.cjs` uses that value for target1, and `applyRenders()` can render and link it. This violates the tool's stated rule that only words referring to the speaker move.

**Smallest fix:** split before an explicit nominative subject such as `वह`/`वो` when an earlier clause is already present, so each verb group is evaluated against its own subject. Keep the regression below; do not simply skip every sentence containing another subject, which would leave the first-person verb unchanged.
