AUKE'S INFINITIVE RULE (binding, posted in course chat 28 Oct 12:53Z): Frisian has two infinitives. After modals (wol, kin, moat, sil, mei, hoecht, soe, woe, koe), after litte/sjen/gean/bliuwe/graach, and in bare 'to X' LEGOs, use the -e/-je form: leare, prate, sizze, besykje. Only directly after 'te' use the -en/-jen form: te learen, te besykjen. Teach 'te learen' as one unit, never 'learen' alone ('learen' alone is only the verbal noun: it praten, mei praten). 'to learn' = leare; 'I want to learn' = ik wol leare. Objects/adverbs go BEFORE te + verb: 'ik besykje mei dy te learen', never 'ik besykje te learen mei dy'. A basket never mixes the forms. THE PHRASE DOOR (v4/v3) DOES NOT KNOW THIS RULE and writes 'wol learen': read every returned phrase and correct or drop any that breaks it before submitting.

WORD ORDER: verb-second in main clauses, verb-final in subordinate clauses (dat, om't, as, oft, foar't, nei't, wylst, wêrom't, dy't, dêr't, hoe't, wat). A LEGO whose Frisian is a verb-final clause STARTS WITH ITS TRIGGER on both sides (K48): never 'prate kin', 'ik de hiele sin noch wit', 'mei my praatst' as LEGOs on their own. Clitics belong to their conjunction: 'that you | datstû' is a LEGO, 'you | stû' is not. Modal clusters stay main-infinitive-before-modal (ûnthâlde kinne); never resurrect 'kinne sil' where the seed has 'kin'. Separable verbs join under a modal/subclause (wol weromkomme, datstû ophâldst) and split in finite main clauses (hy komt moarn werom); once the subject-verb pattern is taught a separable verb's basket carries at least two split rows. om ... te rides with its phrase (no bare 'om | to'); coordinated infinitives share one om (65, 91): 'en dysels te testen' is not an om-clause. Preposition + relative uses the R-form (de man dêr'tstû mei pratest), and the LEGO grows to the whole relative clause. Nouns carry their article inside the LEGO (it antwurd, in wurd); diminutives are 'it'. Conversational past = perfect, participle at the end; a participle LEGO carries its auxiliary or trigger.

AUKE'S FORMS: informal you = dû / -stû (wolstû, datstû, astû) everywhere, never do/-sto. OUR / US IS ALWAYS ÚS, WITH THE ACCENT (Auke, 3 Nov 2026: unaccented "us" is a misspelling): ús mem, ús heit, fan ús heit, sy hawwe ús ferteld, mei ús komme. Never write lowercase "us" anywhere on the Frisian side, in a seed, LEGO, component, BUILD or USE phrase, even where an older seed or the phrase door has it; English "us" on the known side is untouched. (Sentence-initial capital "Us" in seeds 239, 240, 454, 462 is left as written for now and is a question for Auke.) Never útfine. 'trying to' = besykje te only where the seed has besykje; where the seed's English says 'trying to' and the Frisian has no besykje, the basket must not reintroduce it. 'remember' by sense (noch witte / betinke / op in wurd komme / ûnthâlde only for retaining). 'should' = moatte in 253, 328, 403-405; soe ... moatte in 98-100, 407 (Soene wy ... moatte), 438 (dwaan moatte soe), 499: never drill both as 'should' in one basket, and never teach moat as 'must' under a 'should' LEGO. 'I like doing' = ik fyn it moai/noflik om; opinion = ik fyn X goed. 'feel as if' = it gefoel ha dat. Do not de-Dutchify what Auke wrote (stopje, saterdei, poppe, fuortendaliks stay) and do not add Dutch-shaped forms he avoided. Spelling authority: Fryske Akademy 2015 / fkw lexicon; never invent a house spelling.

LEGO RULES (canon, HARD): LEGO English comes from THIS seed's known_text, never another course's English. A LEGO grows on both sides until both are meaningful; no bare function word, no ellipsis ('ik sil it net' for 'I'm not going to ... it' is wrong), no brackets, no slashes, no tags; 'as in' context lives in the presentation, never the LEGO text. LEGO order = build-up order; a word is never first taught by a later LEGO of the same seed. Every BUILD and USE phrase contains its LEGO on BOTH sides. Known side only uses words already given. Want-you-to (15, 169, 170, 171, 238, 249, 427, 432): first one or two presentations give the whole chunk with literal meaning ('I want that you speak'), then head (ik wol) + dat-clause with the verb at the end; English 'that you speak', NEVER 'you to speak'. Duplicate (is_new false) only when BOTH known and target match an earlier new LEGO.

PUNCTUATION (Kai, 3 Nov 2026): "?" GOES ON QUESTION SEEDS AND PHRASES, NEVER ON LEGOs. Every seed and every BUILD or USE phrase that is a real question ends with "?" in the Frisian AND the English (praatstû Frysk? | do you speak Frisian?; wêrom net? | why not?; ik bin wurch, silstû my helpe? | I'm tired, are you going to help me?). LEGO text never carries "?" on either side, even when the LEGO is itself a question (hastû it lêste idee heard | have you heard the latest idea; wa wie dy man | who was that man): the "?" belongs to the sentence the LEGO is used in, not to the piece. Decide by reading the sentence, never by its first word: embedded clauses and fragments are NOT questions (hoe't ik sizze kin | how I can say; wat ik bedoel is wichtich | what I mean is important; as hjir oare minsken binne kin ik neat sizze | when other people are here I can say nothing). NO ASK-THEN-STATE LINES (Kai, 3 Nov 2026): never write a phrase that asks and then states (kenstû ien, ik ken net folle minsken; hastû it lêste idee heard, it is in goed idee): one voice reads both halves, the line starts as a question and ends as a statement, and that confuses the learner. Make each line one thing, a question (moatstû fuortgean, of kinstû in bytsje wachtsje?) or a statement (ja, ik bin der wis fan, ik haw se sjoen; ik ken net folle minsken, allinne myn suster har freon). A statement followed by a question that ends with "?" (ik bin wurch, silstû my helpe?) is allowed. The phrase door writes ask-then-state lines: reword or drop every one before submitting. No other punctuation changes: phrases stay lowercase with no full stop (lowercase means no sentence-initial capital: on the English side "I", day and month names, Mum/Dad and language names KEEP their capitals — never write "i", "monday", "frisian"; stage-2 review #213 found 24 such rows), and a phrase is never a capitalised one-word or one-chunk sentence ('In âlde man.' is wrong; write in âlde man | an old man). The "?" must be there BEFORE audio is rendered, because it changes how the line is read.

NO PADDING TAILS (stage-2 review #199, 4 Nov 2026): every BUILD/USE phrase must make sense as something a person would actually say. Never glue a recent seed's clause onto an unrelated phrase to make it longer or to recycle vocabulary ("it's your turn because the children were tired", "the dog is dirty because the children were tired", "I'll call you back because I have got to be quiet"). Reviewing 301-560 found this in hundreds of rows: "after the meal" 46, "because the children were tired" 35, "before we stop" 27, "before we moved" 20, "when it really matters" 17, "while I fetch the keys" 16, "before everyone knew the news" 14. A causal or time clause must fit its main clause in meaning and tense. A shorter natural phrase beats a longer odd one; meet the phrase floor with natural lines, not tails.
