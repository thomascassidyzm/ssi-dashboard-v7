# Read-like-Deborah relay: calibration on North Welsh, seeds 100-200

**This is a calibration of the READER, not a proofread of Welsh. Nothing in it is a fix or an apply.** Four readers read `cym_n_for_eng` seeds 100-200 in order, one after another, each handing a notebook to the next: 101 seeds, 165 LEGOs, 1,658 phrases. Text came from the nightly copy of 18 Nov 03:30Z, never the live database.

## 1. The one question: is the reader too strict?

**Yes, in its labels. No, in what it sees.**

The readers wrote 202 findings and called 57 of them WRONG (28%). On a course in good shape that is too many. I checked every WRONG against the course text, the canon and the grammar, and kept 11 (5%).

| batch | findings | reader WRONG | lead WRONG | reader FINE + UNSURE | lead FINE + UNSURE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100-119 | 53 | 17 | 4 | 23 (43%) | 26 (49%) |
| 120-139 | 53 | 12 | 1 | 21 (40%) | 23 (43%) |
| 140-169 | 52 | 19 | 2 | 16 (31%) | 21 (40%) |
| 170-200 | 44 | 9 | 4 | 19 (43%) | 21 (48%) |
| all | 202 | 57 | 11 | 79 (39%) | 91 (45%) |

Final bins after my check: **WRONG 11, COULD MISLEAD 100, FINE 68, UNSURE 23.**

The observations themselves were accurate. I checked about 30 quotes against the course text and none was invented; one count was off by one (a basket said to hold 0 of 15 lines with its LEGO holds 1). Three findings did not hold at all. So the readers' eyes are good; what was wrong was how harshly they labelled.

**Where the 46 overturned WRONGs went, and why:**

- **21 were "this form was never taught".** True by the LEGO list every time I checked. But North Welsh is hand-built and words often first arrive inside phrases, so this is a teaching gap with a remedy, not an error. Moved to COULD MISLEAD. Example: *amdani* ("about it") is in about 25 lines and in seed 192, and no LEGO teaches it.
- **12 were loose or head-word labels.** Example: the LEGO *day | y dyddiau hyn* (really "these days"), or *we need | bo' ni angen* (really "that we need"). These are your "useful but not literal" case. Moved to COULD MISLEAD with a re-label remedy.
- **5 were rule-shape breaches that harm nobody**, such as a fragment filed as a use phrase, or "the young woman is five now" (odd but grammatical). Moved to FINE.
- **3 were thin drilling or a missing cue**: two baskets that barely drill their own LEGO, and *ti*/*chi* alternating with no cue. Moved to COULD MISLEAD with a drilling remedy.
- **2 were bookish grammar calls on spoken Welsh.** Example: the reader called *na dydy dy fam di ddim yn licio* ungrammatical by the written rule. Moved to UNSURE for a speaker.
- **3 did not hold.** Example: two intros reported as "truncated" (*The Welsh for: 'them', is:*) are just the newer intro template, where the Welsh plays after the line.

**How I tuned the brief (done, version 3):** WRONG is now limited to five things a reader can point at in the text: a typo or wrong mutation, English and target saying different things, the identical prompt with two answers, an intro that disagrees with its LEGO, and a form asked for before a later LEGO teaches it. A never-taught form is COULD MISLEAD on a hand-built course and WRONG on a machine-built one, and the dispatch now says which kind the course is. Dialect doubts go to UNSURE. Rule-shape breaches are counted in the notebook and written up once.

**Not re-tested.** I have not re-run a batch with the tuned brief, so "the tuned reader is no longer too strict" is expected, not measured. One batch (about 10 minutes) would settle it.

## 3. The best COULD MISLEAD examples (useful translation, phrases stray)

- **yet | hyd yn hyn** (S0013L01). "Not yet" is then *eto* in one line (S0148L01B07) and *hyd yn hyn* in another (S0156L01B06). Remedy: teach *eto* for "not yet", or keep the phrases to one.
- **are those? | ydy rheina?** (S0138L02). Every phrase is *ai eich plant chi ydy rheina?*, so a learner following the LEGO would say *ydy rheina eich plant?* Remedy: an "as in" example in the intro.
- **we wouldn't want | fyddan ni ddim isio** (S0151L01), then **we'd like | bydden ni'n licio** (S0187L01): two forms for "we would". Remedy: pick one.
- **his twenties / her thirties.** *ei hugeiniau* (her) and *ei dridegau* (his) are taught; the phrases then ask for *ei ugeiniau* and *ei thridegau*, never heard. Remedy: give both pairs in one intro.
- **ti and chi** alternate line by line from seed 147 with no cue in the English. Remedy: a cue, or accept both.

## 4. New kinds of problem (merged from the four notebooks)

- **Known form, new sense.** A taught word used in a meaning nobody gave. *am* taught as "for", then used as "about"; *yno* "there" used for pointing at someone in view.
- **Known form, new job.** *y* known as "the", used as "that"; *a* known as "and", used as "who"; *mor* in "how long", then "as … as", then "so".
- **Paradigm sibling.** One member of a family is taught and the others are asked for: his/her twenties, *athro*/*athrawes*, the whole *chi* verb set, *wrthyn*/*wrthon*/*amdani*/*amdano*.
- **Head-word label.** The LEGO's English names one word, its Welsh is the whole chunk: *short | mewn amser byr*, *important | mae'n bwysig*.
- **Label collision.** A new LEGO reuses an earlier English label with a bigger target: *when | pan* then *when | pan oeddan nhw*.
- **Label bites later.** A loose label is harmless where it is taught and misleads many seeds on: *I had | do, ges i*, *the film | y ffilm 'na*.
- **Seed part untaught.** The seed needs a word no LEGO covers: bare *wrth* (114), *y dylen* (190), *amdani* (192), *amdano fo* (196).
- **Hidden clitic.** A new word sits behind an apostrophe where a scan cannot see it: *i'w*, *'ma*, *dw i'm*.
- **Register clash.** The same "you" prompt is *ti* in one line and *chi* in the next.
- **Slot-fill nonsense.** A review frame refilled with words that make no sense: "the young woman is five now", "they think today".
- **Orphan phrase, orphan build, orphan use set.** A phrase with none of its LEGO (24-38% per batch); a LEGO whose first seven lines never use it; a LEGO never replayed because no use line contains it (S0197L01: 0 of 8).
- **LEGO slot swap.** A phrase keeps the LEGO's frame but swaps a word for its opposite (*llai* to *mwy*), so the LEGO is gone.
- **Re-taught as new.** An identical earlier LEGO comes back as new with an intro: *gest ti?*, *wnest ti wylio?*
- **Mutation drift in time words.** *benwythnos nesa'* in the seed, *penwythnos nesa'* in its phrases; day-parts mutate, weekend and month do not.

Several of these could become scans: label collision, re-taught as new, orphan use set, and hidden clitics.

## 6. Tough grammar mismatches, English against Welsh, met with no support

The readers logged 33; these are the ones that bite hardest, with the seed where each first bites.

- **"It" is gendered and built four ways** (*ei fwynhau*, *ei gwylio hi*, *wylio fo*, *amdani*); English gives no clue (107, 174).
- **Three "yes" systems**: *do* (past), *yndw* (present), *oes* (have you got); never set side by side (115, 125, 148).
- **Three words for "that" before a clause**: *bo'/bod*, *y* before a future or conditional, *na* for a negative; only the first is taught (115, 131).
- **A "that" clause carries no tense**: "that it was" is still *fod o* (110).
- **Prepositions conjugate**: *wrthaf fi*, *wrthon ni*, *iddyn nhw*, *amdanoch chi*; only some are taught (114, 151).
- **Possessives mutate differently**: *dy* soft, *fy* nasal, *ei* (his) soft, *ei* (her) aspirate plus h- (103, 155).
- **The object mutates after a short verb**: *ges i wydrad* (105).
- **Negative short past changes the first letter**: *clywes i* but *chlywes i ddim* (136).
- **"X is the Y" puts the noun first**: *yr hogan ydy'r fenga*; and the question form needs *ai* (138, 153).
- **Possession reverses for a definite noun**: *mae gynnon ni blant* but *bod ein plant gynnon ni* (147, 173).
- **Plural noun, singular verb**: *maen nhw* but *mae rhai pobl* (139).
- **English simple past splits**: states take the imperfect (*be' oeddat ti'n meddwl?*), events the short past (*wnest ti wylio?*) (197).
- **The chi register is a second verb set** (147).

The full 33 lines are in `grammar.tsv` on the branch.
