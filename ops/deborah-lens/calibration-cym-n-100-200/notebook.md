## 5. New kinds of problem
- **english-sense-drift**: S0138L01B07 'watch your children'; S0134L01B04.
- **known-form-new-sense**: a taught form used in a meaning never taught. am = about (103); barod = ready (S0123L04); gormod as time (S0134L01B04); yno = pointing 'there' (S0144L01B06 and 15 more).
- **known-form-new-function**: a known little word comes back as a particle. y = "that" (S0115L02B03); a = "who" (S0115L02U16); mor = "as" (S0133L01B01), then "so" (S0161L02).
- **head-word-gloss**: the LEGO's English names one word, the target is the chunk. S0101L02, S0123L01, S0126L01, S0127L01.
- **label-collision** (new, reader 3): a NEW LEGO reissues the exact English label of an earlier LEGO with a different, bigger target, so one English prompt has two answers at LEGO level. S0164L01 when | pan oeddan nhw (vs S0044L01 when | pan); S0165L03 yesterday | fel tasai hi ddoe (vs S0078L01 yesterday | ddoe). A scan can catch this: same English, different target, both NEW.
- **label-target-mismatch** (new, reader 3): label and target cover different stretches, beyond a head word. S0157L02 day | y dyddiau hyn; S0166L01 to work for the council | i'r cyngor; S0169L01 more time | treulio mwy o amser.
- **paradigm-sibling** (new, reader 3): a LEGO teaches one member of a set and the phrases demand the others, which were never heard. S0158L02B04 ei ugeiniau / B07 ei thridegau (taught ei hugeiniau, ei dridegau); the chi verb forms (dach, ydach, gaethoch, wnaethoch, oeddach) behind a single oes gynnoch chi; dau beside dwy (S0152L01B02); athro beside athrawes (S0159L01B03).
- **register-clash** (new, reader 3): the same English 'you' prompt is answered in ti in one line and chi in the next, with no cue. S0142L01B05 helpu chdi vs S0148L01B03 helpu chi; S0167L01U05 wyt ti isio vs S0153L01B01 dach chi isio.
- **slot-fill-nonsense** (new, reader 3): a review frame refilled with a new item until it is absurd. S0142L02B01 the young woman is ten; S0145L01B01 the young woman is five; S0151L01B07 we wouldn't want the oldest, thank you; S0154L02U04 have you got the youngest?
- **fragment-use** (new, reader 3): a USE phrase (played cold) that is a fragment, against P7. S0146L01U01 the young boy; S0153L01U04 the youngest; S0157L02U03 these days; S0169L02U01.
- **orphan-phrase**: 108/400 (100-119), 132/345 (120-139), 106/436 (140-169).
- **orphan-build**: every or nearly every build lacks the LEGO. S0120L01, S0125L01, S0135L01 (all 7); S0149L01 and S0161L01 (5/7).
- **gloss-bites-later**: S0094L01, S0088L01, S0097L01; S0057L01; S0013L01 yet | hyd yn hyn (now a clash, S0148/S0156); S0087L01 the book | y llyfr hwn (S0167L02U09).
- **seed-part-untaught**: seed 114 wrth fy mam; 131 y + future; 132 phosib; 138 ai and chi; 153 identification order (yr hogan ydy'r fenga).
- **hidden-clitic**: untaught clitics behind an apostrophe. i'w (S0122L01U01 …); 'ma (S0132L01U06, S0167L02U09); dw i'm (S0151L02B02). Scan change: treat 'w, 'ma, 'na, 'm as words.
