## 2. What the learner now expects
- Statements: dw i'n / dw i 'di / o'n i'n + verbnoun. Short pasts: wnes i / wnest ti / ges i / gest ti / weles i / es i / clywes i, plus ddudodd o/hi and ddudest ti (ddudes i used unannounced).
- Short future: be' wnei di?, wela i chdi (123). After os, both os medra i and os wela i appear.
- Negatives: dw i ddim, wnes i ddim, do'n i ddim, fedra i ddim, ddylwn i ddim, dydy X ddim, does neb; from 136, chlywes i ddim / chest ti ddim (aspirate). doedd and oeddet are used but untaught.
- Questions: wyt ti …? / wyt ti 'di cael? / wnest ti …? / be' wnei di? / lle wyt ti …?; the tag tydy?. From 138, ai … ydy rheina?
- Answers: do (past, in chunks); yndw (present, from 125). naddo is still unannounced.
- "that" + clause: bo' fi / bo' ti / bod hi / fod o (tenseless); na + soft/aspirate for negatives (untaught); y + short future (mae'n bosib y wela i; untaught as a LEGO).
- Time adverbs: the learner was taught that they soft-mutate (ddydd Gwener, fore dydd Sul, bnawn). In 120-139, penwythnos nesa'/diwetha' and dydd X diwetha' are mostly NOT mutated, while the seed says benwythnos nesa'. Expect the learner to be unsure.
- "something to eat/drink": i fwyta / i yfed AND i'w fwyta / i'w yfed, with no cue to choose between them.
- Possessives: fy + nasal, dy + soft (dy dad from 121 with no radical tad ever seen).
- An object or abstract "it" is a lottery between o and hi.
- Plural subject: mae rhai pobl (126) vs maen nhw (139).
- Contractions: be'/beth, 'di, 'fory, nesa', diwetha', and (untaught) 'ma.

## 5. New kinds of problem
- **known-form-new-sense**: a taught form used in a meaning never taught. Examples: am = about (seed 103); barod = ready after yn barod = already (S0123L04, announced); gormod as a length of time (S0134L01B04).
- **known-form-new-function**: a known little word comes back as a grammatical particle. Examples: y = "that" (S0115L02B03, S0127L01B07, seed 131); a = "who" (S0115L02U16); mor = "as" from pa mor hir? (S0133L01B01).
- **head-word-gloss**: the LEGO's English names one word and its target is the whole chunk. Examples: S0101L02, S0103L01, S0106L02, S0123L01 I'll see | wela i chdi, S0126L01 people | rhai pobl, S0127L01 important | mae'n bwysig.
- **orphan-phrase**: a basket phrase with none of its LEGO. Counted: 108/400 in 100-119, 132/345 in 120-139.
- **orphan-build** (new): EVERY build phrase, the ones played straight after the intro, lacks the LEGO, so the learner's first use of the new item is a cold USE phrase. Examples: S0120L01 (7/7), S0125L01 (7/7: yndw never in a build), S0135L01 (7/7). It is worse than a scattered orphan, because the build is the drill.
- **gloss-bites-later**: a loose earlier gloss that does no harm until a later seed needs the bare word. Examples: S0094L01, S0088L01, S0097L01; S0057L01 thank you | diolch yn fawr against 'thank you very much' (S0122L01B02 vs S0129L02U07).
- **seed-part-untaught**: the seed needs a form that no LEGO covers. Examples: seed 114 wrth fy mam; seed 131 y + future; seed 132 phosib; seed 138 ai and chi.
- **hidden-clitic** (new): an untaught clitic or clipped word hidden behind an apostrophe, so the FIRST-SEEN scan never fires. Examples: i'w (S0122L01U01 and about 15 more), 'ma (S0132L01U06). Worth a scan change: treat 'w, 'ma and 'na as words.
- **adverbial-mutation-drift** (new): the same time adverbial is mutated in some lines and not in others, against what the learner was taught. Examples: seed 121 benwythnos nesa' vs S0121L01B05 penwythnos nesa'; S0121L01U11 ddydd Sadwrn nesa' vs S0128L02B03 dydd Sadwrn diwetha'.
- **english-sense-drift** (new): the target word is fine, but the English prompt uses an English sense the target word does not have. Example: S0138L01B07 'watch your children' (look after) = gwylio (look at). Also partly S0134L01B04 'wait too much'.
