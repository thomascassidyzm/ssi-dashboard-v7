## 2. What the learner now expects

- Register: ti and chi alternate across lines. From 193 the intros name ti ('when you’re talking to a friend'), and 199 sets dach chi beside wyt ti ’di clywed.
- 'that' + clause: bo’ fi / ti / ni / nhw, bod hi, fod o (tenseless: 'that they wanted' = bo’ nhw isio, S0186L01B01); y + dylen / byddai (182, 190); na + negative (165); bo’ nhw ddim (185).

- Time adverbs: day-parts mutate (bnawn, fore); penwythnos / mis nesa’ do not.
- Ages without oed; possession with gan + soft mutation; definite possession bod ein plant gynnon ni (173).

## 4. House conventions
- Intros: "The Welsh/Cymraeg for X is Y. Y.", often "which you'll usually hear as …"; occasional as-in (145, 177 agor → agored) and playful asides ("Meanwhile, back at the ranch", 183; "easily enough", 190). Harmless.
- Head-word glosses bridged by the intro: reported once per batch. Label collisions and label-target mismatches are NOT this convention.
- Colloquial North forms: isio, efo, deud, dallt, rŵan, licio, chdi, ’na, rheina, wrthaf fi, clywes i, hogan, hogyn, y fenga, sgynnon ni, ffeindio, trio, bach yn arafach.
- Free variation: beth/be’, bod/bo’, sy’n/sydd yn, eich plant / eich plant chi, ’di / wedi, rhai ni yn / rhai ni’n.
- byddai fo is the taught 3rd-person conditional; noun-subject byddai’r X follows it.
- Ages without oed. yn fwy aml / yn fwy agored (rather than amlach) is consistent.
- Build-only baskets are common (172L02, 180, 183, 185 in this batch).
- Late seeds (194-200) are long English-led review baskets of 'did you …?' frames.

## 5. New kinds of problem
