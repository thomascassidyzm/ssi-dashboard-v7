# Missing-LEGO mismatch: scoping and calibration (job cs/161, 30 Oct 2026)

Read-only scoping for Kai. Nothing in any course was changed; no audio was rendered. Every number below comes from live Popty reads taken today (`GET /api/production/<course>/seeds`, one read per course) and from judge runs through the Claude CLI.

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

The class is real and it is concentrated where Kai said: in the later half of courses. In ita_for_eng a Sonnet sweep of every tenth played basket from seed 40 on (126 baskets, 1,171 phrases) raised 29 flags; I read every flag and 22 are genuine (12 clear, 10 mild), which is about 1.9% of played phrases, or roughly one defective phrase in every five baskets. The same sweep in fin_for_eng (111 baskets, 996 phrases) found none that survive reading, and my own full read of 100 Finnish phrases found none either. Hand-reading 36 baskets (364 phrases) across ita/fin/spa gave 5 positives; Haiku with the filtered context found all 5 with one false positive, and both models agree on the clear cases. So a Haiku fan-out is viable, with one rule: only confidence ≥4 flags go straight onto Kai's list (in the sweep, every confidence-4-or-5 flag was a real defect; the false positives were all confidence ≤3). The whole estate is 96,760 played baskets / 818,327 played phrases across 123 courses; the nine paid courses are 11,799 baskets / 112,190 phrases and run in about 2 hours on nine Haiku workers.

## 2. The detection method

**Unit of judgement = one played basket.** A basket is one `is_new` LEGO and its phrases with `phrase_role != 'component'`. Component rows are never played (Kai, 21 Oct) and not-new LEGOs' baskets are never played (P25, verified in `generateLearningScript.ts`), so both are excluded before anything is judged. Judging a basket, not a phrase, is what makes the taught context affordable: the ten or so phrases under a LEGO share one context.

**What each checker gets, verbatim in the prompt:**
1. the course code, and which side is known / target;
2. the seed sentence pair;
3. the basket's LEGO pair;
4. the TAUGHT PAIRS: every LEGO (known | target), in seed and LEGO order, from seed 1 up to and including this LEGO, plus each LEGO's component sub-pairs marked with a `c` suffix. Not-new LEGOs are included (they are still taught pairings). Two variants were measured: FULL (all of them; mean 1,444 pairs in ita, 1,658 in fin) and FILTERED (only pairs sharing a whole word, or a 5-letter stem of a 6+-letter word, with any phrase in the basket, plus every pair from the basket's own seed; mean 571 pairs in ita, 616 in fin, 577 in spa);
5. the phrases, as `id [role]: known | target`.

**The judging prompt** (the file `cal/prompt.txt`, reproduced in full):

```
You are checking practice phrases in a language course built by the SaySomethingin method. Each course teaches a TARGET language to speakers of a KNOWN language through LEGOs: a LEGO is a pair (known chunk | target chunk). The learner hears the known-side sentence and must produce the target-side sentence using only LEGOs taught so far.

THE DEFECT you are looking for: a practice phrase where one side of the pair carries a whole chunk of meaning (typically a whole LEGO's worth: a word or a phrase) that has NO counterpart on the other side, and that no taught LEGO pairing explains. Example: known "I want to speak Italian with you now" vs target "voglio parlare italiano adesso" — "with you" is missing on the target side. Either side may be the one missing something, and the chunk may also be WRONG rather than missing (a different word, e.g. "Friday" vs "Saturday", "this morning" vs "in the morning", "I" vs "we", negation present on one side only).

NOT A DEFECT (the test is the learner's TAUGHT EXPECTATION, not literal translation):
- A difference that follows from how a taught LEGO pairs the two sides. If a LEGO was introduced with an imperfect match (a case, tense, aspect, article, preposition, pronoun-drop, idiom or wording that is not 100% literal), every phrase built from it inherits that and is ACCEPTABLE. Cite the LEGO.
- Grammar the target language simply does: dropped subject pronouns, articles the other language lacks, word order, one-word verb forms rendering two-word known forms, clitics, agreement, a tense the language uses where the known side uses another (e.g. present for "have been ...-ing for"), politeness particles.
- Known-side inflections/contractions/synonym forms of taught words (the known side may outrun its introductions).
- A LEGO pair judged by its FUNCTION in its seed is fine even if not word-for-word (e.g. "until after | avant qu'" when the seed's meaning holds).
Do NOT judge naturalness, style, register, punctuation or capitalisation. Do NOT flag the mere use of a word not yet taught. Only flag MEANING present on one side and absent/different on the other, unexcused by the taught pairs.

You get: the seed sentence pair, this basket's LEGO pair, the taught LEGO pairs available at this point (earlier seeds and this seed up to this LEGO; "c" suffix = a component gloss inside a LEGO), and the basket's phrases. For EACH phrase decide:
- "DEFECT": a chunk of meaning on one side with no counterpart and no taught pairing explaining it. Name the side that is missing it ("target" or "known"), the chunk, and why no LEGO excuses it.
- "INHERITED": the sides differ but a taught LEGO pairing explains the difference. Cite the LEGO id.
- "OK": the two sides carry the same meaning (allowing for the grammar notes above).
Confidence 1-5 (5 = certain). Be precise and conservative: a DEFECT must name a concrete chunk.

Answer with ONLY a JSON array, one object per phrase, in order:
[{"id":"S0001L02B01","verdict":"OK|INHERITED|DEFECT","side":"target|known|null","chunk":"<the missing/wrong meaning, or null>","cites":["<lego ids>"],"why":"<one short sentence>","confidence":1-5}]
```

**Output:** one JSON object per phrase: `verdict` (DEFECT / INHERITED / OK), `side`, `chunk`, `cites` (LEGO ids), `why`, `confidence` 1-5. The `cites` field enforces Kai's rule that every row must name the LEGO that excuses it or fails to.

**Transport:** `claude --print --model haiku` with the prompt on stdin, never the SDK (CLAUDE.md rule). Measured on one late basket (seed 421, ita): filtered context = 24.6k prompt tokens + the CLI's own 14k cached system prompt, Haiku 16-18 s, Sonnet 5-7 s; API-equivalent cost per basket $0.007 Haiku / $0.107 Sonnet filtered, $0.009 / $0.163 full. Haiku's `why` fields are three to five times longer than Sonnet's (3.4k vs 0.7k output tokens), which is most of its time.

**Thresholding rule, from the calibration:** confidence ≥4 → goes on Kai's defect list as found; confidence 3 → a second-look list (a second model or a human reads it); confidence ≤2 → dropped (every one of these in the sweep was a non-defect, and three were the model withdrawing its own flag inside the `why`).

### 3d. False-positive classes (what the model flags that is not a defect)

1. **Pronoun conventions the course uses throughout**: it | questo / quello (ita). Not this class; a different, already-ruled question (Kai 28 Sept: dirlo = say it, dire questo = say this).
2. **Grammar the known language omits**: English "that"-deletion ("I know I enjoy" | "so che mi piace").
3. **Correct idiomatic equivalents**: at home | a casa; came round to see us | tuli käymään meille; seeing you | että me nähdään. The second and third are Finnish idioms where the LEGO itself is the idiom; the checker should treat "the basket's own LEGO pair differs literally" as INHERITED by construction. Worth one extra prompt line.
4. **Self-withdrawn flags**: verdict DEFECT with `why` saying it is fine, always confidence 1. The threshold removes them; a parser should also drop any DEFECT whose `why` contains "no defect" / "is fine" / "withdrawn".
5. **Nuance shifts** (anything | qualcosa; really want | help a lot): borderline, confidence 2-3, belong on the second-look list, not the defect list.

**Verdict on the brief's question:** Haiku is accurate enough to be the fan-out model, with the ≥4 threshold and a Sonnet second look on its flags; it loses one clear defect in twelve against Sonnet and finds more of the mild class. Both models together (union) is the honest ceiling for a cheap run; the first full-course run on ita should be hand-read afterwards to put a real recall number on it.

## 4. Fan-out plan

**Snapshot once, judge from disk.** One puller job reads every course once through Popty (`GET /api/production/<code>/seeds`, 2.4 s and ~20 MB per course, 157 courses in about 6 minutes sequentially, one request in flight at a time) into `~/ssi-evidence/ssi-dashboard-v7/ops/mismatch-161/snapshots/<course>.json` on the box. Judges read files, never the database, so the pooler sees 157 requests total and zero during judging. (Today's pull is in my `$CS_SCRATCH`, which is swept; the fan-out re-pulls.) The judge prompt, prep, scoring and labels are committed in this branch under `ops/mismatch-161/`, so every worker runs the same code.

**Shard = one course**, except the three courses over 1,600 baskets (hak, cym_sv2, mar) which split at seed 334. ## 5. What Kai gets, per course

One published document per course, nine-paid first, each in this shape, sentence pairs and seed numbers always shown (Kai rules by reading the sentence):

**Group A — a chunk missing on the target** ("somewhere else | da qualche parte"; "a better story | una storia"). Fix rule: one-off edit of the target to add the taught chunk if it is taught by that seed (ladder step 1), then verify the new pair against ZUT and P1 through the existing edit-batch gates (step 2), then search the same course for the same English chunk paired with the same incomplete target (step 3: a phrase was often copied). Audio: the changed slot re-renders through the one route after Kai's spend decision.

**Group B — a chunk missing on the known** ("I don't want trouble | no quiero causar problemas"; "he has heard stories about it | ne ha sentite alcune storie"). Fix rule: edit the English (free: known-side TTS is cheap and the known side may outrun its introductions), never the target, unless the target brought in an untaught word.

**Group C — wrong chunk** (needed → wanted; other → many; matters → è importante; she → I; negation on one side). Fix rule: decide per row which side the seed context supports (the LEGO's function in the seed, Tom 15 Oct); fix that side; then step 3 across the course for the same wrong pairing, because a wrong word tends to be a builder's habit (deu #435's "want/möchte" was 100+ rows).

**Group D — extra chunk on the target** ("continuano a", "vogliono", "molto", "in questo"). Fix rule: remove from target if what remains is still a taught tiling; otherwise add the English. These are the ones most likely to have been introduced by a seed edit whose phrases did not follow: check `course_seeds.updated_at` vs the phrase's `updated_at` and group those as "seed edited, phrases stale" with the seed number, which Kai fixes as one seed-level action.

**Group E — dropped clitic / demonstrative (mild)**: list in one table per course for a single yes/no from Kai on whether the subclass is a defect in that language (per-course decision, consistency-decisions-are-per-course).

**Second-look section**: confidence-3 and model-disagreement rows, sentence pairs shown, no proposed fix.

**Held for native speaker**: anything where the judge's `why` turns on a language fact Kai cannot adjudicate (kai-language-competence): route to Deborah for spa/deu/fra/jpn, Shuchita for hin, native lists for the rest.

Each row: `phrase id · seed · known | target · the chunk · the LEGO cited · proposed side to fix · audio consequence (slot count)`. The pattern step (ladder 3/4) is a count line under each group: "same English chunk / same incomplete target appears N more times in this course", computed from the snapshot, not re-judged.

## 6. Honest gaps

- **Recall is measured on 5 hand-found positives**, not 50: the class is rare (≈2% of phrases), so 398 hand-read phrases yielded 5. Precision is measured on 34 model flags. Both models found all 5. A 50-positive recall figure needs the first fan-out course to be fully hand-read afterwards (ita, Kai is fluent) — I recommend that as the acceptance test for wave 1 rather than more pre-work.
- **Only Latin-script courses were calibrated** (ita, fin, spa). ara, zho, jpn, kor are five of the nine paid courses and nothing here shows Haiku reads them as well. Wave 1 should run jpn_for_eng alongside ita and have its confidence ≥4 flags read by Deborah before the other three start.
- **fin hand labels are mine, not a Finnish speaker's.** Twelve baskets, zero positives; Kai can confirm in minutes.
- **Dropped-clitic subclass is unruled.** Ten of the 22 genuine ita findings are this class; the rate halves if Kai rules it acceptable.
- **No tool landed on main.** The harness (`prep.py`, `judge.py`, `score.py`, `prompt.txt`, labels, adjudications, census) is committed on branch `cs/161-missing-lego-mismatch-scope` under `ops/mismatch-161/`, not merged. Snapshots are not committed (1.6 GB).
- **The CLI's 14k-token system prompt is paid on every call.** It is more than half the input on early-seed baskets. Batching baskets per call is the fix; not measured here.
