
# Part 2. Where rulings pull against each other

Each item says what cannot all hold, what the plan does, and my recommendation. None is resolved silently: the plan's choice is marked in Part 1 and can be reversed.

1. **Seed 300, Eoghan's tense against Kai's 10 November ruling.** Eoghan wrote *go mbeidh cuma mhíchairdiúil uirthi* (future). Kai ruled that a go-clause after a "want" head is always conditional in this course (seeds 15, 170, 249). The plan writes *go mbeadh*. Recommendation: keep the conditional, and ask Eoghan (question 2).
2. **Seeds 110 and 219, "to relax".** Eoghan wrote *ár scíth a ligean* in both. Seed 110 says "I'd like to relax", so it has to be *mo scíth*. Taking *ár* at 219 would then give "to relax" two Irish answers. The plan uses *mo scíth a ligean* in both. 28 phrases with "I" are reworded; 36 phrases about other people still say bare *scíth a ligean* and need the right word for the person (*do*, *a*, *ár*) or deleting at apply time. Recommendation: *mo* in both, ask Eoghan about 219 (question 3).
3. **"someone".** Eoghan corrects *duine* to *duine éigin* at 234, 235, 236 and 341, but leaves *duine* at 128 and 133. A bare LEGO "someone | duine" at 128 beside "someone | duine éigin" at 234 is one English with two answers. The plan teaches bare "someone | duine éigin" at 234 and keeps *duine* inside a bigger piece at 128 ("you're like someone") and 133 (the frame "you get to know someone"). Recommendation: as planned.
4. **"exactly the same".** Eoghan wrote *dhíreach chéanna* at 153 and *cheannann chéanna* at 518. The plan uses *díreach céanna* at both, because 153 is early and *díreach* is already taught. Recommendation: one form; ask Eoghan if 518 can follow 153 (question 5).
5. **Seed 570, "pay for".** Eoghan wrote *i leith do thicéid* here but *íoc as* at 508 and 509. The plan writes *a íoc as do thicéad* (his own majority form, as the clash forecast #251 proposed). Recommendation: as planned.
6. **Seed 220.** Eoghan's sentence asks "do you watch much television?", which changes our English ("did you watch a bit of television?"). The final check took only his *ar an teilifís*. The plan writes *ar fhéach tú ar an teilifís beagán?*. I am not sure this is natural. Recommendation: use it as a draft and ask Eoghan (question 6).
7. **Seeds 392 and 394.** Eoghan's 392 adds "and who is standing", which the English does not say; the plan leaves it out. At 394 he wrote *á caitheamh*; *gúna* is masculine, so the plan writes *á chaitheamh*, and keeps *sin* for his *úd*. Recommendation: ask Eoghan (questions 7 and 8).
8. **Wrap frames against the canon rule that a LEGO holds only its own seed's words (L26).** Kai's model frame, "it excites me | cuireann sé sceitimíní orm", works at 122 because *sé* is in that sentence. Three other frames need a stand-in word the seed does not have: 100 *agus é á dhéanamh agat*, 105 *nach raibh sé ar eolas aige*, 290 *an bhfuil sé ar eolas aige*. Recommendation: allow a stand-in *é* or *sé* in a frame, and record it in the canon as Kai's exception to L26.
9. **"Every part is a LEGO" against canon L30.** L30 says a stretch built only from taught pieces needs no LEGO of its own. Kai's 12 November rule says taught pieces still appear, marked not-new. The plan follows 12 November: 104 not-new copies are added. They never play, so the learner sees no difference; the gain is that the whole sentence is accounted for. Recommendation: follow 12 November and add a line to L30.
10. **"Hardest part first".** The commission lists it as a LEGO-order rule. Kai's own words on 12 November were "small pieces first, then the combined chunk", and "hardest first" was the order for showing him the fix items. The canon (L13, K47) also says small before big. The plan orders LEGOs small first. Recommendation: confirm that reading.
11. **The 8-syllable cap against Eoghan's wording.** Three of his stretches are 9 syllables and cannot be one LEGO: 117 *ná mar a bhí á dhéanamh agam* (cut as two overlapping pieces, "than I was" and "that I was doing"); 104 *an rud atá á dhéanamh againn* (kept as two pieces, with "what" relabelled "the thing that is" so that bare "what" has no second answer); 75 *an bhfuil níos mó le foghlaim agat* (cut in three with plain labels). His Irish is untouched in all three. Recommendation: accept the cuts; they are the least bad.
12. **"to know" (seeds 45 and 201).** *a bheith ar eolas agam* and *a fhios a bheith againn* both carry the label "to know". Growing either one breaks the cap. The plan leaves both. Recommendation: keep both and say in each introduction which is which, as the final check proposed; this is the one clash the plan does not remove.
13. **Seed 36 and "we don't want".** Moving "I wouldn't like" to 36 (ruled) removes the only home of *ní mian linn*: its 17 basket phrases go, and about 10 later phrases use it untaught. See open question 2.
14. **"when".** Kai asked for "when" to be taught at 34 (*nuair*) and ruled no *cathain* LEGO at 255. The live course also has "when | cathain a" at seed 79. The plan folds that into "when did you start | cathain a thosaigh tú", so bare "when" has one answer. Recommendation: as planned.
15. **Seed 12 and the recorded rule "going to = tá … chun".** The ruled sentence uses the future (*cad a tharlóidh*) under "going to". Recommendation: record the exception the final check proposed (C7): "going to be" and "going to happen" take the future.
16. **Seed 13 (ruled A3) moves "very well | go han-mhaith" to seed 72**, the first sentence that says it. Seed 13 now teaches the frame "you speak Irish" and the whole "you speak Irish very well". Phrases between 14 and 71 that say *go han-mhaith* stay, since *an-mhaith* is inside seed 13's piece.
