## The one question: where does *en* go?

**My recommendation: put *en* with *tardar* in L02. L02 = "to take too long" = *tardar demasiado en*, and L03 = "to answer" = *contestar*. Y/N?**

**Why.** In Spanish, *en* belongs to *tardar*, not to *contestar*: it is *tardar en* + infinitive. This course already keeps a verb's preposition with the verb, in the LEGO the learner says first:
- S0005 "I'm going to" = *voy a*
- S0029 "I'm looking forward to" = *tengo ganas de*
- S0037 "I started to" = *empecé a*
- S0010 "I'm not sure" = *no estoy seguro de*

The canon says the same about bound elements: join them to the verb in the seed, and keep them as a component so they can be reused later. With this cut, "to answer" = *contestar* stays exactly what it already is in S0043L02 and about 30 later rows, so nothing clashes. The LEGO is 7 syllables, under the cap of 8.

**The cost.** The bare LEGO ends in *en*, just as "I'm going to" = *voy a* does. Every L02 phrase therefore has to carry a verb after it ("taking too long to speak / to come back"), which is how people use it anyway. L02 is an M-LEGO with components *tardar* "to take long", *demasiado* "too much" and *en* "in".

**What I rejected:**
- **Your literal cut, L02 = *tardar demasiado* with *en* left over.** *en* would then either go untaught or be counted as the existing "in" = *en* (S0004). That tells the learner it means "in", and the English gives them no cue for it.
- **L03 = "to answer" = *en contestar*.** This clashes with "to answer" = *contestar* in S0043L02 and about 30 other rows, as you said.
- **A separate *en* LEGO.** It would need a placeholder English gloss, which is ruled out (no invented notation).
- **One LEGO, "to take too long to answer" = *tardar demasiado en contestar*.** That is 10 syllables, over the cap of 8, and *contestar* would get stuck inside it.
