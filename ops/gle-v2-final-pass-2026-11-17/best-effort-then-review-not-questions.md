## The failure, in the shapes it actually takes

1. **Holding a fix because no native speaker is available** — "X isn't fixed, needs a native
   speaker", "native speaker needed (my rec)", "probably should be Y" left unapplied.
2. **Leaving a "list for the proofreader"** — a numbered question list as the deliverable of a
   fix pass, items left as they were until someone answers.
3. **Waiting for a proofreader before audio** — not re-recording / not queueing the audio pass
   "until the proofreader has seen it".
4. **Routing a language call to a named person by default** ("take it to Deborah", "ask Auke",
   "send it to the native-speaker list") when online evidence could settle it.

## How to apply

- **Research, decide, apply, cite.** Dictionaries, grammars, the language's own Wikipedia, forum
  and corpus attestations, the course's own clean sibling rows, the old SSi course where one
  exists. A Fable researcher is one dispatch. Put the sources in the record next to the change.
- **The bar is "better than what is there now", not certainty.** Say your confidence in one
  word per item (sure / likely / best guess) and apply anyway. Low confidence is a label, not a hold.
- **Then queue the audio as normal.** A pending proofread is never a reason to delay the audio pass.
- **No question lists for proofreaders.** The proofreader reads through the finished course and
  fixes what they find. Our record may carry a short "least sure about" line so they know where
  to look, but nothing waits on it and nobody is asked to answer it.
- **Wording to avoid in reports and memory lines:** "needs a native speaker", "for the
  proofreader", "held for <name>", "awaits <native> Qs". If you catch yourself writing one, go
  and do the research instead.

## What stays as it was (the carve-outs Kai keeps)

- **Principle-level calls are Kai's**: how literal we are willing to be, whether to split or mix a
  contrast, a whole-course structural switch, spend at batch scale. Ask him one at a time.
  A big re-record (e.g. ~44k chars to switch hoću→želim) is a spend call for Kai, not a reason to
  hold the TEXT fix — present the fix and the cost together, decided.
- **A native speaker's existing rulings are applied, but a clash is DISCUSSED** (Kai 16 Nov:
  "I wouldn't say Eoghan beats ours, but a clash should definitely be discussed"). Eoghan
  (Irish), Auke (Frisian), Deborah, Doug (pdc): apply what they have already said; where it
  clashes with our version, raise it with Kai — neither side wins by default. Asking them NEW
  questions is not the job.
- **When a native IS actively reviewing** and asks, answer them; and their suggestions still get
  validated against the course (canon O7).
- **Never invent a word in a minority orthography with no evidence at all.** "Could not find any
  evidence" is a real gap and worth one line; "could not verify to certainty" is not.
- **Do not overclaim**: a build is "internally consistent, best effort from <sources>", not
  "verified correct".
