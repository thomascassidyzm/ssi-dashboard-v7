# Irish course: a few questions for Eoghan

Hi Eoghan,

Thank you again for going through the course. We've taken almost all your corrections as written. These are the few places where we'd like your ear before we finalise. Irish is in italics; the English is the prompt learners hear. Short answers are fine, even just "fine" or "no, say X".

**1. "She doesn't want to seem unfriendly" (seed 300)**
We have *Ní mian léi go mbeadh cuma mhíchairdiúil uirthi*. You wrote *go mbeidh*. In this course, after "want" we always use the conditional in the *go* clause, so we kept *go mbeadh*. Does *go mbeadh* sound right to you here, or does *go mbeidh* really sound better?

**2. "It was nice to relax for a while" (seed 219)**
We have *bhí sé go deas mo scíth a ligean ar feadh tamaill*. You wrote *ár scíth*. This is a note more than a question: earlier in the course (seed 110) "to relax" is taught as *mo scíth a ligean*, and for a long time learners only use it about themselves. The other forms (*do*, *a*, *ár*) come in late, once learners have met that pattern through other phrases. So we kept *mo* here. Are you happy with *mo* at 219, or does your *ár* change the meaning you had in mind?

**3. "I couldn't imagine myself having exactly the same problem" (seed 518)**
We have *Ní fhéadfainn mé féin a shamhlú leis an bhfadhb dhíreach chéanna*. You wrote *leis an bhfadhb chéanna díreach* here, and *sa chaoi dhíreach chéanna* at seed 153. We'd like to use one pattern for "exactly the same" throughout, and 153 is much earlier, so we used *dhíreach chéanna* at 518 too. Is that fine, or do you prefer *chéanna díreach* here?

**4. A new first-week sentence (seed 12)**
"I wouldn't like to say what's going to happen tomorrow": *níor mhaith liom a rá cad a tharlóidh amárach*. This one is our own draft, you haven't seen it before. Is it natural?

**5. "Do you watch much television?" (seed 220)**
Just so you know: we've changed the English to match your sentence, and we use your Irish, *An mbíonn tú ag féachaint ar an teilifís mórán?*

**6. "That child with the black hair who's standing opposite the post office" (seed 392)**
Just so you know: your line gave the alternatives *uirthi / air* and *ina seasamh / ina sheasamh* (for her and for him), and we extended the English to match. We took the masculine choices, so ours reads *an páiste sin a bhfuil gruaig dhubh air agus atá ina sheasamh os comhair na hoifige poist*. The only thing we added is *sin* for "that child". Please say if you'd rather leave it out.

**7. Short pieces we teach before the full sentence**
For a few long sentences we teach a short stretch first, ending with a little "it" in place of the thing it stands for, and then practise filling it in. Are these natural Irish on their own?
- *agus é á dhéanamh agat* ("while you're doing it", seed 100)
- *nach raibh sé ar eolas aige* ("he didn't know it", seed 105)
- *an bhfuil sé ar eolas aige* ("if he knows it", seed 290)

- Seed 294: *Níl dóthain ama agam anocht chun glaoch a chur ort*. Your line began *Níl bheidh dóthain ama*, which we took to be a slip. Is our reading right?

The rest is as you wrote it. Still right to your ear?
